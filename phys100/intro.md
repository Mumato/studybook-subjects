## Welcome to PHYS100

This book covers a first university course in **Conceptual Physics**: how the physical world works, from falling apples to atoms and starlight. The goal is to *comprehend* physics: to see why things happen, picture them, and predict what comes next. There is very little algebra: if you can multiply, divide and rearrange $d = vt$, you can do every problem. Each lesson notes the matching chapter of the free OpenStax textbook *College Physics 2e* if you want to read more.

### How a lesson works

Every lesson gives you **a rule**, **a worked example** and then **practice problems**, with pictures wherever a picture helps.

- Type your answer and press **Enter**, or click an option. The box turns **green** when you're right and **red** when you're not.
- Stuck? Click **Hint**. Missed it twice? The worked **Solution** opens for you.
- Problems marked with a star are **creative**: they need more thinking.
- Many questions target a common wrong idea about physics. If one surprises you, read the solution: that's where the learning happens.

### Typing answers

- Type numbers only, in the unit the question asks for: `45`, not `45 m`.
- Big and small numbers: type `3000000` or `3*10^6`. Very big or small answers are usually asked "in units of" a power of ten, so you type just the front number.
- Use $g = 10$ m/s² unless the problem says otherwise.

### Tests

Each chapter ends with a **test**. Score 80% to complete the chapter.

### The course

- Part 1: Describing motion (speed, velocity, acceleration, free fall)
- Part 2: Newton's laws (inertia, force, F = ma, action and reaction)
- Part 3: Momentum and energy
- Part 4: Rotation and gravity (circular motion, torque, orbits)
- Midterm practice: two practice exams on Parts 1-4
- Part 5: Properties of matter (density, pressure, buoyancy, gases)
- Part 6: Heat (temperature, heat transfer, thermodynamics)
- Part 7: Waves and sound
- Part 8: Electricity and magnetism
- Part 9: Light (colour, mirrors, lenses)
- Part 10: Atoms and nuclei (photons, spectra, radioactivity, fission and fusion)
- Part 11: Special relativity
- Final practice: two cumulative practice exams
