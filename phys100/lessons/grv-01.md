title: Universal gravitation
---
Newton's great leap: the force that pulls an apple down is the same force that holds the Moon in orbit. Every mass in the universe pulls on every other mass. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 6)

::: rule
$$F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{d^{2}}$$
- The pull grows with both masses and shrinks with the **square of the distance** $d$ between their centres: an **inverse-square law**.
- Twice as far: $\tfrac{1}{4}$ the force. Three times as far: $\tfrac{1}{9}$.
- $G = 6.67 \times 10^{-11}$ N·m²/kg² is tiny, so we only notice gravity when at least one mass is huge, like a planet.
- The pull is mutual: the apple pulls Earth up exactly as hard as Earth pulls the apple down.
:::

![](assets/grv-inverse.3x.png)

::: example
You weigh 800 N on Earth's surface, 1 Earth radius from its centre. At 2 Earth radii from the centre (one radius up, about 6400 km), your weight would be
$$800 \times \frac{1}{2^{2}} = 200 \text{ N}$$
Gravity weakens with distance, but it never reaches zero.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="1/4"
The distance between two masses doubles. By what factor does the gravitational force change? (Type a fraction like 1/2.)
solution: $\frac{1}{2^{2}} = \frac{1}{4}$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="1/9"
The distance between two masses triples. By what factor does the force change?
solution: $\frac{1}{3^{2}} = \frac{1}{9}$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="2"
One of the two masses doubles; the distance stays the same. By what factor does the force change?
solution: The force is proportional to each mass: it doubles.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="1"
Both masses double, and so does the distance between them. By what factor does the force change?
hint: Multiply the mass factors, then divide by the distance factor squared.
solution: $\frac{2 \times 2}{2^{2}} = 1$: the force is unchanged.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="100"
A satellite weighs 900 N on Earth's surface. What would it weigh 3 Earth radii from Earth's centre, in newtons?
solution: Three times as far from the centre: $900 / 9 = 100$ N.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
You and the person next to you attract each other by gravity. Why don't you drift together?
- [ ] People don't attract each other.
- [x] The force is tiny, because $G$ is so small, far weaker than friction with the floor.
- [ ] Earth's gravity cancels it.
solution: Two people a metre apart pull on each other with less than a millionth of a newton.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
An apple falls from a tree. Compared with Earth's pull on the apple, the apple's pull on Earth is:
- [ ] much smaller
- [x] exactly the same size
- [ ] zero
solution: Third law. Earth's enormous mass means its acceleration is too small to notice.
:::
