title: Radioactivity and half-life
---
Some nuclei are unstable. Sooner or later they break down and fling out particles or energy: **radioactivity**. You can't say when one nucleus will go, but a large pile follows a clockwork rule. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 31)

::: rule
- A nucleus has **protons** and **neutrons**. The number of protons (the **atomic number**) decides the element. **Isotopes** of an element have different numbers of neutrons. The **mass number** counts protons + neutrons.
- **Alpha** decay: emits 2 protons + 2 neutrons (a helium nucleus), so the mass number drops by 4 and the atomic number by 2. Stopped by paper.
- **Beta** decay: a neutron turns into a proton and shoots out an electron, so the atomic number rises by 1. Stopped by thin aluminium.
- **Gamma**: a high-energy photon. Needs thick lead or concrete.
- **Half-life:** the time for half of a sample to decay. After each half-life, half of what's left remains.
:::

::: example
Iodine-131 has a half-life of 8 days. A hospital has 80 mg:
- after 8 days: 40 mg
- after 16 days: 20 mg
- after 24 days: 10 mg

![](assets/nuc-half@3x.png)
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="100"
A sample of 800 g has a half-life of 5 days. How much is left after 15 days, in grams?
solution: 15 days is 3 half-lives: $800 \to 400 \to 200 \to 100$ g.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="1/8"
What fraction of a radioactive sample is left after 3 half-lives? (Type a fraction.)
solution: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times \tfrac{1}{2} \times \tfrac{1}{2} = \tfrac{1}{8}$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="11460"
Carbon-14 has a half-life of 5730 years. A bone has 25% of its original carbon-14. How old is it, in years?
solution: 25% is 2 half-lives: $2 \times 5730 = 11460$ years.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="90"
Uranium-238 (atomic number 92) emits an alpha particle. What is the atomic number of the new nucleus?
solution: Alpha decay removes 2 protons: $92 - 2 = 90$ (thorium).
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="234"
What is the new nucleus's mass number?
solution: Alpha decay removes 4 nucleons: $238 - 4 = 234$.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Which radiation can pass through a sheet of aluminium but is mostly stopped by thick lead?
![](assets/nuc-rays@3x.png)
- [ ] alpha
- [ ] beta
- [x] gamma
solution: Gamma rays are photons with no charge; they're the most penetrating.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Carbon-12 and carbon-14 are isotopes. They have:
- [x] the same number of protons, different numbers of neutrons
- [ ] different numbers of protons
- [ ] the same mass
solution: Both are carbon (6 protons); carbon-14 has 2 extra neutrons.
:::
