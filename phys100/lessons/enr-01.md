title: Work and power
---
In everyday talk, holding a heavy bag for an hour is hard work. In physics it's no work at all. Physics work means a force that *moves* something. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 7)

::: rule
$$W = F d \qquad P = \frac{W}{t}$$
- **Work** = force × distance moved *in the direction of the force*. Unit: the **joule** (J) = 1 N·m.
- No movement, no work. A force at right angles to the motion does no work either.
- **Power** is how fast work is done. Unit: the **watt** (W) = 1 J per second.
- Lifting something at steady speed takes a force equal to its weight, so the work is weight × height.
:::

::: example
You push a crate 5 m across the floor with 80 N: $W = 80 \times 5 = 400$ J. If you do it in 4 s, your power is $400 / 4 = 100$ W. Do it in 2 s: the same 400 J of work, but 200 W.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="100"
You pull a sled 5 m with a force of 20 N along the ground. How much work do you do, in joules?
solution: $W = 20 \times 5 = 100$ J.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="200"
You lift a 10 kg box 2 m straight up at a steady speed. How much work do you do, in joules? (Use $g = 10$ m/s².)
hint: The lifting force equals the weight.
solution: Weight $= 10 \times 10 = 100$ N. $W = 100 \times 2 = 200$ J.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A weightlifter holds 1000 N above her head, perfectly still, for 5 s. How much work does she do on the weights?
- [ ] 5000 J
- [ ] 200 J
- [x] 0 J
solution: The weights don't move, so no work is done on them, however tiring it feels. (Her muscles do use energy, but it all turns into heat inside her.)
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="200"
A motor does 600 J of work in 3 s. What is its power, in watts?
solution: $P = 600 / 3 = 200$ W.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="300"
A 60 kg student runs up stairs 4 m high in 8 s. What power does the student develop, in watts?
solution: $W = 600 \times 4 = 2400$ J; $P = 2400 / 8 = 300$ W.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
You carry a heavy box across a level room at constant speed. How much work does your upward holding force do on the box?
- [ ] a lot, since the box is heavy
- [x] none, since the force is at right angles to the motion
- [ ] it depends on how fast you walk
solution: Your force is up, the motion is sideways: no distance along the force, so no work.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Two people climb the same stairs. One runs up, one walks. They weigh the same. Which is true?
- [ ] The runner does more work.
- [x] They do the same work, but the runner uses more power.
- [ ] The walker uses more power.
solution: The same weight lifted the same height means the same work. The runner does it in less time, so more power.
:::
