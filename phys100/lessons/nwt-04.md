title: Action and reaction: Newton's third law
---
You can't touch without being touched. Every force is one half of an interaction between two objects. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 4)

::: rule
**Newton's third law:** whenever object A pushes on object B, B pushes back on A with a force that is **equal in size and opposite in direction**.
- The two forces of a pair act on **different objects**, so they never cancel each other.
- The pair is the same size even when the objects are very different. What differs is the *effect*: $a = F/m$ for each object.
:::

![](assets/nwt-wall@3x.png)

::: example
When you walk, your foot pushes the ground backward, and the ground pushes you forward. That forward push from the ground is what moves you. On slippery ice your foot can't push back on the ground, so you can't walk.
:::

::: example
A rocket pushes hot gas out the back, and the gas pushes the rocket forward. It doesn't need air to push against, so rockets work in space.

![](assets/nwt-rocket@3x.png)
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
You push a wall with 50 N. The wall pushes you with:
- [ ] 0 N, because walls don't push
- [ ] less than 50 N
- [x] 50 N, away from the wall
- [ ] more than 50 N
solution: Third law: equal and opposite.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
A tiny bug splats on a truck's windshield. Which force is bigger?
- [ ] the force of the truck on the bug
- [ ] the force of the bug on the truck
- [x] they are the same size
solution: It's one interaction, so the forces are equal. The bug's tiny mass gives it a huge acceleration; the truck barely notices.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="4"
A 50 kg skater on smooth ice pushes on a wall with 200 N. What is the skater's acceleration while pushing, in m/s²?
hint: Which force acts on the skater, and how big is it?
solution: The wall pushes the skater with 200 N (third law), so $a = 200 / 50 = 4$ m/s², away from the wall.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="2"
A 60 kg skater and a 30 kg skater stand on ice and push each other apart. The 60 kg skater moves off at 1 m/s. How fast does the 30 kg skater move, in m/s?
hint: Equal forces for the same time; half the mass gets twice the acceleration.
solution: Equal and opposite forces act for the same time. With half the mass, the 30 kg skater gets twice the acceleration and ends up at twice the speed: 2 m/s.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A book rests on a table. Its weight (Earth pulls book down) is one force. What is the third-law partner of that force?
- [ ] the table pushing the book up
- [x] the book pulling Earth up
- [ ] the book pushing the table down
- [ ] there isn't one
solution: The pair of "Earth pulls book" is "book pulls Earth". The table's push is a different interaction: equal here only because the book is in equilibrium.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
What pushes a swimmer forward?
- [ ] the swimmer's arms
- [x] the water, pushing back on the swimmer's arms and legs
- [ ] the air
solution: The swimmer pushes water backward; the water pushes the swimmer forward.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
A horse refuses to pull a cart: "Whatever force I put on the cart, it pulls back on me just as hard, so we can never move." What is wrong with the horse's argument?
- [ ] Nothing: a horse really can't pull a cart.
- [ ] The horse pulls a little harder than the cart pulls back.
- [x] The two forces act on different objects. The horse moves forward because the ground pushes it forward harder than the cart pulls it back.
solution: The equal pair acts on two different objects, so it never cancels. To see whether the horse accelerates, add only the forces *on the horse*: the ground pushes it forward (reacting to its hooves pushing back), and the cart pulls it back. If the ground's push is bigger, horse and cart speed up together.
:::
