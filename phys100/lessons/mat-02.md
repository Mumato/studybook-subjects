title: Pressure and buoyancy
---
Why does a steel ship float while a steel nail sinks? Why do your ears hurt at the bottom of a pool? Both come down to pressure in a liquid. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 11)

::: rule
$$\text{pressure} = \frac{\text{force}}{\text{area}}$$
- Unit: the **pascal** (Pa) = 1 N/m².
- Pressure in a liquid grows with **depth** (and with the liquid's density). It pushes equally in all directions, and doesn't depend on the container's shape. In water it rises by about one atmosphere every 10 m.
- **Archimedes' principle:** an object in a fluid is pushed up by a **buoyant force equal to the weight of the fluid it displaces**.
- An object floats if it's less dense than the fluid. A floating object sinks until it displaces **its own weight** of fluid.
:::

![](assets/mat-buoy.3x.png)

::: example
A 2 L (0.002 m³) block is held completely under water. It displaces 2 L of water, which has a mass of 2 kg and a weight of 20 N. So the buoyant force is 20 N, whatever the block is made of. If the block weighs 50 N it sinks, and feels like it weighs only $50 - 20 = 30$ N under water.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="20000"
A 600 N person stands on two feet with a total area of 0.03 m². What pressure do they put on the floor, in pascals?
solution: $600 / 0.03 = 20000$ Pa.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
Water spurts from three holes in a tall tank. Which jet comes out fastest?
![](assets/mat-holes.3x.png)
- [ ] the top one
- [ ] the middle one
- [x] the bottom one
solution: The deepest hole has the most water above it, so the greatest pressure.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="30"
A 3 L block is held fully under water. What buoyant force acts on it, in newtons?
hint: 1 L of water weighs 10 N.
solution: It displaces 3 L of water, which weighs 30 N.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="20"
A rock weighs 50 N in air and 30 N when hanging fully under water. What is the buoyant force on it, in newtons?
solution: $50 - 30 = 20$ N.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="3000"
A boat with a mass of 3000 kg floats on a lake. What mass of water does it displace, in kg?
solution: A floating object displaces its own weight of fluid: 3000 kg.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="90"
Ice has a density of 0.9 g/cm³. What percent of a floating ice cube is under water?
![](assets/mat-float.3x.png)
solution: It sinks until it displaces its own weight: $0.9 / 1 = 90\%$ under water. That's why icebergs hide most of their bulk.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
A solid steel ball sinks, but a steel ship floats. Why?
- [ ] The ship's steel is a lighter kind.
- [x] The ship's hollow shape displaces a large volume of water, weighing as much as the ship.
- [ ] Moving ships are held up by their motion.
solution: Shaped as a hull, the same steel pushes aside far more water, so the buoyant force can match its weight.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=choice creative=true
You're in a small boat on a pond with a big rock in the boat. You throw the rock into the pond and it sinks. Does the pond's water level rise, fall, or stay the same?
- [ ] rise
- [x] fall
- [ ] stay the same
solution: In the boat, the rock displaced its own **weight** of water: much more than its volume, because rock is denser than water. On the bottom, it displaces only its own **volume**. Less water is displaced, so the level falls slightly.
:::
