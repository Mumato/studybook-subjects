title: Momentum and impulse
---
A slow truck and a fast bullet are both hard to stop. **Momentum** captures this "how hard to stop" in one number, and **impulse** tells you what it takes to change it. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 8)

::: rule
$$p = m v$$
$$\text{impulse} = F t = \text{change in } p$$
- **Momentum** $p$ is mass times velocity, in kg·m/s. It has a direction.
- To change momentum you need **impulse**: force × the time it acts.
- The same change in momentum can come from a **big force for a short time** or a **small force for a long time**. Air bags, padded floors and bending your knees all stretch out the time, so the force is smaller.
- Bouncing back needs *more* impulse than just stopping, because the momentum reverses.
:::

::: example
A 60 kg person moving at 10 m/s in a crash has $p = 600$ kg·m/s. Stopping them takes an impulse of 600 N·s.
- A dashboard stops them in 0.1 s: $F = 600 / 0.1 = 6000$ N.
- An air bag stops them in 0.5 s: $F = 600 / 0.5 = 1200$ N.

Same impulse, five times less force.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="30000"
What is the momentum of a 1200 kg car moving at 25 m/s, in kg·m/s?
solution: $p = 1200 \times 25 = 30000$ kg·m/s.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="10"
A force of 50 N acts on a puck for 0.2 s. What impulse does it give, in N·s?
solution: $50 \times 0.2 = 10$ N·s.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="20"
A 0.5 kg ball is kicked from rest to 40 m/s. What impulse did the kick give it, in N·s?
hint: Impulse equals the change in momentum.
solution: $\Delta p = 0.5 \times 40 - 0 = 20$ kg·m/s, so the impulse is 20 N·s.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="400"
That kick lasted 0.05 s. What was the average force on the ball, in newtons?
solution: $F = 20 / 0.05 = 400$ N.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Why does catching a fast ball hurt less if you pull your hands back as you catch it?
- [ ] It reduces the ball's momentum before it arrives.
- [x] It makes the stopping time longer, so the force is smaller.
- [ ] It makes the impulse smaller.
solution: The ball's momentum must go to zero either way, so the impulse is the same. A longer time means less force.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="4"
A 0.2 kg ball hits a wall at 10 m/s and bounces straight back at 10 m/s. What is the size of its change in momentum, in kg·m/s?
hint: Going from +10 to −10 is a change of 20 m/s.
solution: $\Delta p = 0.2 \times (10 - (-10)) = 0.2 \times 20 = 4$ kg·m/s. Just stopping would be only 2.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
A bullet and a truck have the same momentum. Which statement must be true?
- [ ] They have the same speed.
- [x] The bullet is much faster, because it has much less mass.
- [ ] The truck is faster.
solution: $p = mv$: a tiny mass needs a huge speed to match a big mass moving slowly.
:::
