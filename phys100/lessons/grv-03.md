title: Projectiles and satellites
---
Throw a ball sideways and it curves down to the ground. Throw it fast enough and the ground curves away beneath it as fast as it falls: it's in orbit. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 3, 6)

::: rule
- A **projectile**'s sideways motion and its up-down motion are **independent**. Ignoring air, the sideways speed stays constant, while it falls exactly like a dropped object.
- A ball thrown horizontally and a ball dropped from the same height **land at the same time**.
- A **satellite** is a projectile moving so fast sideways that as it falls, Earth's surface curves away beneath it. Just above the atmosphere that takes about **8 km/s**.
:::

![](assets/grv-proj.3x.png)

::: example
A ball rolls off a 5 m high wall at 3 m/s. Falling 5 m takes 1 s (since $5 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 1^{2}$). In that 1 s it moves $3 \times 1 = 3$ m sideways, so it lands 3 m from the wall.
:::

::: example
At 8 km/s, a satellite moves 8 km sideways each second and falls 5 m toward Earth, just as a dropped stone falls 5 m in its first second. Over 8 km, Earth's curved surface drops away by about 5 m. So the satellite falls, but never gets closer.

![A: slow. B: faster. C: fast enough to orbit](assets/grv-cannon.3x.png)
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="3"
A stone is thrown horizontally at 10 m/s off a cliff 45 m high. How many seconds does it take to land?
hint: The sideways speed doesn't matter for the fall: $45 = \tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times t^{2}$.
solution: $t^{2} = 9$, so $t = 3$ s.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="30"
How far from the foot of the cliff does that stone land, in metres?
solution: $10 \text{ m/s} \times 3 \text{ s} = 30$ m.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A bullet is fired horizontally from a rifle, and at the same moment another bullet is dropped from the same height. Ignoring air, which hits the level ground first?
- [ ] the fired bullet
- [ ] the dropped bullet
- [x] they hit at the same time
solution: Vertical motion doesn't depend on the sideways speed: both fall the same height in the same time.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
A plane flying level at a steady speed drops a supply box. Ignoring air, where is the box when it hits the ground?
- [ ] far behind the plane
- [x] directly below the plane
- [ ] ahead of the plane
solution: The box keeps the plane's sideways speed, so it stays under the plane all the way down.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="5"
Ignoring air, how far does any projectile fall in its first second, in metres?
solution: $\tfrac{1}{2} \times 10 \times 1^{2} = 5$ m. That's the "5 m per 8 km" that makes orbits work.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
What keeps a satellite in orbit from falling to Earth?
- [ ] There's no gravity up there.
- [ ] Its engines push it up.
- [x] Nothing: it *is* falling, but it moves sideways so fast that Earth curves away beneath it.
solution: An orbit is endless falling around the planet.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
A monkey hangs from a branch. A hunter aims a dart gun straight at it. The monkey lets go at the very instant the dart leaves the gun. Ignoring air, should the hunter have aimed higher, lower or straight at the monkey to hit it?
- [ ] higher, because the dart falls
- [ ] lower, because the monkey falls
- [x] straight at it: the dart and the monkey fall the same amount in the same time
solution: Without gravity, the dart would fly straight to the monkey. With gravity, both fall the same distance $\tfrac{1}{2} g t^{2}$ during the flight, so the dart still meets the monkey, just lower down.
:::
