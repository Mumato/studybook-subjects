title: Conservation of momentum
---
When objects push only on each other, the third law says their pushes are equal and opposite. So whatever momentum one gains, the other loses. That makes momentum one of the great conservation laws. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 8)

::: rule
**Conservation of momentum:** if no outside net force acts on a group of objects, their **total momentum stays the same**, through any collision, explosion or push.
$$\text{total } p \text{ before} = \text{total } p \text{ after}$$
- Momentum to the right counts plus, to the left minus.
- In a **sticky** (inelastic) collision the objects move off together. In a **bouncy** (elastic) collision they bounce apart. Momentum is conserved in both.
- **Recoil:** if the total starts at zero (a gun at rest, a skater standing still), the parts fly apart with equal and opposite momenta.
:::

::: example
In the picture, a 2 kg cart at 3 m/s hits a 1 kg cart at rest, and they stick.
$$p_{before} = 2 \times 3 + 1 \times 0 = 6$$
After, 3 kg moves together: $3 v = 6$, so $v = 2$ m/s. Slower, but the same total momentum.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="4"
A 4000 kg train car at 5 m/s runs into a 1000 kg car at rest, and they couple together. How fast do they move off, in m/s?
solution: $p = 4000 \times 5 = 20000$. Together: $5000 v = 20000$, $v = 4$ m/s.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="1"
A 50 kg skater at rest throws a 5 kg ball forward at 10 m/s. How fast does the skater move backward, in m/s?
![](assets/mom-recoil.3x.png)
solution: Total momentum starts at 0. The ball has $5 \times 10 = 50$, so the skater has $-50$: $50 v = 50$, $v = 1$ m/s backward.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="1"
A 4 kg rifle fires a 0.01 kg bullet at 400 m/s. How fast does the rifle recoil, in m/s?
solution: Bullet: $0.01 \times 400 = 4$ kg·m/s. Rifle: $4 v = 4$, $v = 1$ m/s backward.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="0"
Two identical 1 kg lumps of clay fly toward each other at 5 m/s each, hit head on and stick. How fast is the combined lump moving, in m/s?
solution: Total momentum: $1 \times 5 + 1 \times (-5) = 0$. So the lump is at rest.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A moving pool ball hits an identical ball at rest head on, in a bouncy collision. What happens?
- [ ] Both move forward at half the speed.
- [x] The first ball stops and the second moves on at the first ball's speed.
- [ ] The first ball bounces back.
solution: All the momentum passes to the second ball. (Sticky identical balls would move on together at half speed instead.)
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
A firecracker at rest explodes into many pieces. What is the total momentum of all the pieces just after the explosion?
- [ ] large, since the pieces move fast
- [x] zero
- [ ] it depends on how many pieces there are
solution: The explosion is all internal pushes. The total starts at zero and stays zero: the pieces' momenta cancel.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
An astronaut floats motionless 10 m from her spaceship, with a heavy wrench in her hand. Her safety line has snapped. How can she get back?
- [ ] Swim with her arms.
- [x] Throw the wrench hard, directly away from the ship.
- [ ] Throw the wrench toward the ship.
- [ ] She can't: with nothing to push on, she's stuck.
solution: She and the wrench start with zero momentum. Throwing the wrench away from the ship gives her equal momentum toward the ship. Swimming does nothing: there's nothing to push against.
:::
