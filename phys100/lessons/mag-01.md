title: Magnets and electromagnets
---
Magnetism looks like a separate force, but it isn't: magnetism is what electric charges do when they **move**. That single idea gives us electromagnets, motors and the compass. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 22)

::: rule
- Every magnet has a **north** and a **south pole**. **Like poles repel, unlike poles attract.** Break a magnet in half and you get two magnets, each with both poles.
- A magnet is surrounded by a **magnetic field**. Outside the magnet, field lines run from N to S; a compass needle lines up with them.
- **Moving charges make magnetic fields.** A current in a wire circles it with a field; a coil of wire with current is an **electromagnet**, stronger with more current and more turns.
- A magnetic field pushes sideways on a current: that's how **electric motors** turn.
- Earth is a giant magnet, so a compass's north pole points north.
:::

::: example
Wrap 100 turns of wire around an iron nail and connect a battery: the nail picks up paper clips. Disconnect it, and they fall. Double the turns or the current and it lifts about twice as many. Scrapyard cranes work this way.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
You snap a bar magnet in half. What do you get?
- [ ] a north pole on its own and a south pole on its own
- [x] two smaller magnets, each with a north and a south pole
- [ ] two pieces that are no longer magnetic
solution: Poles always come in pairs. Every tiny piece of a magnet is itself a magnet.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
The north pole of one magnet is brought near the north pole of another. They:
- [ ] attract
- [x] repel
- [ ] don't interact
solution: Like poles repel.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Outside a bar magnet, its field lines run:
![](assets/mag-bar.3x.png)
- [x] from the N pole around to the S pole
- [ ] from the S pole around to the N pole
- [ ] straight out of the middle
solution: A small compass placed on a line points along it, away from N and toward S.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
What is the source of all magnetism?
- [ ] iron atoms only
- [x] moving electric charges
- [ ] gravity
solution: Currents in wires, and the spinning and orbiting electrons inside atoms.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
How can you make an electromagnet stronger?
- [ ] use less current
- [x] add more turns of wire or more current
- [ ] remove the iron core
solution: Each turn adds its field to the others, and more current makes each turn's field stronger. An iron core helps too.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
An electric motor works because:
- [x] a magnetic field pushes on a current-carrying coil, making it turn
- [ ] electricity is converted into magnets
- [ ] magnets store energy
solution: The sideways push on the coil's two sides makes a torque.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
A compass needle's north end points toward Earth's geographic North Pole. So the magnetic pole up there is actually a:
- [ ] magnetic north pole
- [x] magnetic south pole
solution: Unlike poles attract: the needle's north end is pulled toward a magnetic south pole.
:::
