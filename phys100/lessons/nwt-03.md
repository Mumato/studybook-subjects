title: F = ma: Newton's second law
---
A net force changes an object's motion. Newton's second law says *how much*: a bigger force gives more acceleration, more mass gives less. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 4-5)

::: rule
$$a = \frac{F_{net}}{m} \qquad\text{or}\qquad F_{net} = m a$$
- The acceleration points the same way as the net force. 1 N is the force that gives 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m/s².
- **Weight** is the force of gravity on a mass: $W = m g$, about 10 N per kg on Earth. Mass is the same everywhere; weight depends on where you are.
- Falling through air, **air resistance** grows with speed until it equals the weight. Then the net force is zero and the faller keeps a steady **terminal speed**.
:::

::: example
You push a 50 kg sled with a net force of 100 N.
$$a = \frac{100}{50} = 2 \text{ m/s}^2$$
Push twice as hard: twice the acceleration. Load it to 100 kg: half the acceleration.
:::

::: example
Why do heavy and light objects fall together? A 10 kg ball has 10 times the weight of a 1 kg ball, but also 10 times the mass to move:
$$a = \frac{100 \text{ N}}{10 \text{ kg}} = \frac{10 \text{ N}}{1 \text{ kg}} = 10 \text{ m/s}^2$$

:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="3"
A net force of 12 N acts on a 4 kg cart. What is its acceleration, in m/s²?
solution: $a = 12 / 4 = 3$ m/s².
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="2000"
A 1000 kg car accelerates at 2 m/s². What net force acts on it, in newtons?
solution: $F = 1000 \times 2 = 2000$ N.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="700"
What is the weight, on Earth, of a 70 kg person, in newtons? (Use $g = 10$ m/s².)
solution: $W = 70 \times 10 = 700$ N.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="70"
That person goes to the Moon, where gravity is about one sixth as strong. What is their mass there, in kg?
solution: Mass doesn't change: still 70 kg. (Their weight drops to about 117 N.)
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="2"
You push a 30 kg box with 100 N, and friction pulls back with 40 N. What is the box's acceleration, in m/s²?
hint: Find the net force first.
solution: $F_{net} = 100 - 40 = 60$ N, so $a = 60 / 30 = 2$ m/s².
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
A skydiver falls at a steady terminal speed. Compared with her weight, the air resistance on her is:
- [ ] smaller
- [x] equal
- [ ] larger
solution: Steady speed means zero acceleration, so the net force is zero: air resistance equals weight.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
A skydiver opens her parachute while falling at terminal speed. Right after it opens, she:
- [ ] keeps falling at the same speed
- [x] slows down, because air resistance is now bigger than her weight
- [ ] goes upward
- [ ] speeds up
solution: The big parachute makes air resistance jump above her weight, so the net force points up and she slows, until a new, lower terminal speed. She never goes up: she only slows.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=choice creative=true
A tennis ball and a solid steel ball of the same size are dropped from a high tower. Which reaches the ground first, and why?
- [ ] the tennis ball: it's lighter, so air slows it less
- [x] the steel ball: the same air resistance is a smaller fraction of its much larger weight
- [ ] they land together, because $g$ is the same
solution: Same size and speed means about the same air resistance. For the heavy steel ball that force is tiny compared with its weight, so its net force per kg stays close to 10 N/kg. The light ball's acceleration drops much sooner. Without air, they would land together.
:::
