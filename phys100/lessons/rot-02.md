title: Torque, balance and centre of gravity
---
A force makes things move; a **torque** makes things turn. That's why door handles sit far from the hinges and why long wrenches loosen stuck bolts. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 9)

::: rule
$$\text{torque} = \text{force} \times \text{lever arm}$$
- The **lever arm** is the distance from the turning point (pivot) to where the force acts, measured at right angles to the force. Unit: N·m.
- Something balances when the **clockwise torques equal the counterclockwise torques**.
- The **centre of gravity** (CG) is the point where all the weight seems to act.
- An object **tips over** when its CG moves past the edge of its base. A low CG and a wide base make things stable.
:::

![600 × 1 = 300 × 2: balanced](assets/rot-seesaw1@3x.png)

::: example
A 400 N child sits 1.5 m from the pivot of a seesaw. Where must a 200 N child sit to balance?
$$400 \times 1.5 = 200 \times d$$
so $d = 600 / 200 = 3$ m. The lighter child needs the longer lever arm.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="15"
You push a wrench handle with 50 N at 0.3 m from the bolt, at right angles. What torque do you apply, in N·m?
solution: $50 \times 0.3 = 15$ N·m.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="3"
Where must the 200 N child sit to balance the seesaw, in metres from the pivot?
![](assets/rot-seesaw2@3x.png)
solution: $400 \times 1.5 = 600 = 200 \times d$, so $d = 3$ m.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="200"
A stuck bolt needs 80 N·m of torque. Your wrench is 0.4 m long. How hard must you push at the end, in newtons?
solution: $F = 80 / 0.4 = 200$ N.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
A bolt won't budge. Which helps most?
- [ ] pushing on the wrench closer to the bolt
- [x] slipping a pipe over the handle to make it longer
- [ ] pushing along the handle toward the bolt
solution: A longer lever arm gives more torque for the same force. Pushing along the handle gives no torque at all.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Each box is let go in the position shown. The red dot is its centre of gravity. Which one falls over?
![](assets/rot-tip@3x.png)
- [ ] the left box
- [x] the right box
- [ ] both
- [ ] neither
solution: The right box's CG is past the corner it rests on, so its weight turns it over. The left box's CG is still above its base, so it falls back.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Racing cars are built low and wide because:
- [x] a low centre of gravity over a wide base is hard to tip over
- [ ] it makes them lighter
- [ ] it reduces their mass
solution: The CG must travel far sideways before it passes the edge of the base.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Why is a door handle placed far from the hinges?
- [ ] so the door is lighter
- [x] for a long lever arm, so a small force gives enough torque
- [ ] so the door swings faster
solution: Try pushing a door near its hinges: you need a much bigger force.
:::
