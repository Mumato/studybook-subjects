title: Inertia: Newton's first law
---
For 2000 years people believed, like Aristotle, that a moving thing needs a push to keep moving. Galileo and Newton showed the opposite: things keep moving *by themselves*. What stops them is friction. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 4)

::: rule
**Newton's first law (law of inertia):** an object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion keeps moving at the same speed in a straight line, **unless a net force acts on it**.
- **Inertia** is this resistance to any change in motion.
- **Mass** measures inertia: more mass, harder to start, stop or turn. Mass is measured in kilograms (kg).
- No force is needed to *keep* something moving. A force is needed to *change* its motion.
:::

::: example
A hockey puck slides across smooth ice and keeps going far, at almost the same speed. Nothing is pushing it forward: it simply keeps its motion. On rough concrete it stops quickly, because friction is a force that changes its motion.
:::

::: example
You're standing on a bus that brakes hard, and you lurch forward. Nothing pushed you forward: your body just *kept moving* while the bus slowed down under you.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
A spacecraft far from any star or planet turns off its engines while moving at 10 km/s. What happens next?
- [ ] It slows down and stops.
- [x] It keeps moving at 10 km/s in a straight line.
- [ ] It speeds up.
- [ ] It starts to drift in a circle.
solution: With no force acting, nothing changes its motion. It needs no fuel to keep going.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
A ball rolling across a carpet slows down and stops. Why?
- [ ] It runs out of the force you gave it.
- [x] Friction from the carpet acts on it.
- [ ] Moving things naturally stop.
- [ ] Its inertia wears off.
solution: The push only acted while your hand touched the ball. After that, the only horizontal force is friction, which slows it.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Which has more inertia?
- [x] a 1000 kg car
- [ ] a 2 kg bowling ball rolling very fast
- [ ] they have the same inertia
solution: Inertia depends on mass only, not on speed.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
You pull a tablecloth out quickly from under the dishes, and the dishes barely move. This shows:
- [ ] gravity
- [x] inertia
- [ ] the dishes have no mass
- [ ] friction is very large
solution: The dishes stay at rest because the friction acts only for a very short time. A slow pull would drag them along.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A car turns left suddenly. A box on the back seat slides toward the right door. Why?
- [ ] A force pushes the box to the right.
- [x] The box keeps going straight while the car turns left under it.
- [ ] The box is pulled outward by the turn.
solution: Seen from the road, the box goes straight on. The car turns, so the door comes toward the box.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
A hockey puck slides on perfectly frictionless ice at 5 m/s. To keep it moving at 5 m/s, you need:
- [ ] a small steady push forward
- [ ] a push equal to its weight
- [x] no force at all
solution: With no friction, nothing changes its motion. Pushing would speed it up.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Why do cars have headrests?
- [ ] to stop your head flying forward when the car brakes
- [x] to push your head forward with your body when the car is hit from behind
- [ ] for comfort only
solution: When the car is hit from behind, the seat pushes your body forward. Without a headrest, your head stays behind (inertia) and your neck snaps back.
:::
