title: Vibrations and waves
---
A wiggle in time is a **vibration**; a wiggle that spreads through space is a **wave**. Sound, light, earthquakes and ripples on a pond are all waves, and they all follow the same few rules. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 16)

::: rule
- **Frequency** $f$: vibrations per second, in hertz (Hz). **Period** $T$: seconds per vibration. $T = 1/f$.
- **Wavelength** $\lambda$: distance from one crest to the next. **Amplitude**: how far it swings from the middle.
$$v = f \lambda$$
- A wave carries **energy** from place to place, not the material itself.
- **Transverse:** the medium moves at right angles to the wave (a shaken rope, light). **Longitudinal:** it moves back and forth along the wave (sound, a pushed spring).
:::

::: example
Water waves pass a pier: 2 crests every second, 3 m apart. Frequency 2 Hz, wavelength 3 m, so
$$v = 2 \times 3 = 6 \text{ m/s}$$
The period is $1/2 = 0.5$ s.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="0.2"
A guitar string vibrates 5 times a second. What is its period, in seconds?
solution: $T = 1/5 = 0.2$ s.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="2"
A pendulum takes 0.5 s for each full swing. What is its frequency, in Hz?
solution: $f = 1/0.5 = 2$ Hz.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="12"
A wave has a frequency of 4 Hz and a wavelength of 3 m. What is its speed, in m/s?
solution: $v = 4 \times 3 = 12$ m/s.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="2"
Sound travels at 340 m/s. What is the wavelength of a 170 Hz note, in metres?
solution: $\lambda = v / f = 340 / 170 = 2$ m.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A rubber duck floats on a pond as waves pass under it. The duck:
- [ ] travels along with the waves
- [x] mostly bobs up and down in place
- [ ] stays perfectly still
solution: The wave carries energy past the duck; the water (and the duck) mostly moves up and down.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
In the picture, which shows the amplitude?
![](assets/wav-parts@3x.png)
- [ ] the distance from crest to crest
- [x] the distance from the middle line to a trough (or crest)
- [ ] the distance from a crest to a trough
solution: Amplitude is measured from the middle (rest) position, so crest to trough is twice the amplitude.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
In which kind of wave do the particles move back and forth *along* the direction the wave travels?
![](assets/wav-types@3x.png)
- [ ] transverse
- [x] longitudinal
solution: Like a spring pushed and pulled from one end: squeezed regions (compressions) travel along it.
:::
