title: The atom and its light
---
Heat a gas of one element and it glows in a few sharp colours, a fingerprint unique to that element. Explaining those colours revealed how atoms are built. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 30)

::: rule
- **Rutherford** fired alpha particles at gold foil: most went straight through, a few bounced back. So an atom is mostly empty space with a tiny, dense, positive **nucleus**.
- **Bohr:** electrons can only have certain **energy levels**. When an electron drops to a lower level, it emits a photon whose energy equals the gap. Jumping up, it absorbs exactly that energy.
- Each element has its own levels, so its own set of colours: its **line spectrum**. That's how we know what stars are made of.
:::

![](assets/atm-levels.3x.png)

::: example
In hydrogen, level 2 is at −3.4 eV and level 3 at −1.5 eV (eV is a tiny energy unit used for atoms). An electron dropping from 3 to 2 emits a photon of
$$-1.5 - (-3.4) = 1.9 \text{ eV}$$
That's red light: hydrogen's bright red line.

![](assets/atm-spectrum.3x.png)
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
In Rutherford's experiment, most alpha particles passed straight through the gold foil. This showed that:
- [x] atoms are mostly empty space
- [ ] atoms are solid spheres
- [ ] gold has no nucleus
solution: And the rare ones that bounced back showed a tiny, heavy, positive nucleus.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="10.2" tolerance=0.01
In hydrogen, level 1 is at −13.6 eV and level 2 at −3.4 eV. What energy photon is emitted when an electron drops from 2 to 1, in eV?
solution: $-3.4 - (-13.6) = 10.2$ eV, an ultraviolet photon.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="12.1" tolerance=0.01
Level 3 is at −1.5 eV. What energy photon is emitted in a drop from 3 straight to 1, in eV?
solution: $-1.5 - (-13.6) = 12.1$ eV.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="3"
An electron sits in level 3. Dropping one or more levels at a time, how many different photon energies could it give off on its way down to level 1?
hint: List the possible jumps.
solution: 3→2, 2→1 and 3→1: three different energies.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Why does each element have its own pattern of spectral lines?
- [ ] Each has a different colour of nucleus.
- [x] Each has its own set of electron energy levels, so different gaps.
- [ ] Bigger atoms give brighter light.
solution: The lines are the gaps between levels, unique for each element.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
How do astronomers know there is helium in the Sun?
- [ ] They sent a probe to collect it.
- [x] Sunlight contains helium's spectral lines.
- [ ] Helium is in every star by definition.
solution: In fact helium was discovered in the Sun's spectrum before it was found on Earth.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
A neon sign glows orange-red, but the glass tube is clear. Where does the colour come from?
- [ ] The tube is painted inside.
- [x] Electricity excites the neon atoms, and as their electrons drop back they emit neon's particular colours.
- [ ] Neon gas is orange.
solution: Other gases give other colours: mercury vapour is bluish, sodium yellow. Each colour is a gap in that atom's levels.
:::
