title: Speed and velocity
---
Physics starts with one question: how do things move? To answer it we need words that mean exactly one thing. The first two are **speed** and **velocity**. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 2)

::: rule
$$\text{speed} = \frac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$$
- **Average speed** is the *total* distance divided by the *total* time. **Instantaneous speed** is the speed at one moment: what a speedometer shows.
- **Velocity** is speed *in a given direction*. "60 km/h" is a speed; "60 km/h north" is a velocity.
- Velocity is **constant** only if *both* the speed and the direction stay the same.
:::

![](assets/mot-steady.3x.png)

::: example
A train covers 240 km in 3 hours. Its average speed is
$$\frac{240 \text{ km}}{3 \text{ h}} = 80 \text{ km/h}$$
It may have stopped at stations and raced between them: the average hides that. The speedometer showed the instantaneous speed at each moment.
:::

::: example
A car drives around a circular track at a steady 50 km/h. Its **speed** is constant, but its **velocity** is not, because its direction keeps changing.

![Arrows show the velocity at four points](assets/mot-velocity.3x.png)
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="50"
A car travels 150 km in 3 hours. What is its average speed, in km/h?
solution: $150 / 3 = 50$ km/h.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="5"
A runner covers 400 m in 80 s. What is the runner's average speed, in m/s?
solution: $400 / 80 = 5$ m/s.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="120"
A cyclist rides at a steady 6 m/s for 20 s. How far does the cyclist go, in metres?
hint: distance = speed × time.
solution: $6 \times 20 = 120$ m.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="40"
You drive 60 km in 1 hour, then another 60 km in 2 hours because of traffic. What is your average speed for the whole trip, in km/h?
hint: Total distance over total time. It is *not* the average of the two speeds.
solution: Total distance $120$ km, total time $3$ h: $120 / 3 = 40$ km/h. Averaging 60 and 30 would give 45, which is wrong: you spent more *time* at the slow speed.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A car goes around a bend at a steady 50 km/h. Which is true?
- [ ] Its speed and its velocity are both constant.
- [x] Its speed is constant, but its velocity changes.
- [ ] Its velocity is constant, but its speed changes.
- [ ] Both change.
solution: The speedometer doesn't move, so the speed is constant. The direction changes, so the velocity changes.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
A car's speedometer shows:
- [x] its instantaneous speed
- [ ] its average speed for the trip
- [ ] its velocity
- [ ] its acceleration
solution: It shows how fast you're going right now, without a direction, so it's the instantaneous speed.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=numeric answer="500" tolerance=1
Light travels at $3 \times 10^{8}$ m/s. The Sun is $1.5 \times 10^{11}$ m away. How many seconds does sunlight take to reach us?
hint: time = distance ÷ speed.
solution: $\frac{1.5 \times 10^{11}}{3 \times 10^{8}} = 500$ s, a bit over 8 minutes.
:::
