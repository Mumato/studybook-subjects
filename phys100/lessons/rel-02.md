id: rel-02
title: Length, simultaneity and the speed limit
---
If time stretches for moving things, space must shrink to keep the speed of light the same for everyone. Together these lead to $E = mc^2$ and a cosmic speed limit. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 28)

::: rule
- **Length contraction:** a moving object is measured shorter along its direction of motion: $L = L_0 / \gamma$, where $L_0$ is its length at rest. Its crew measures the normal length.
- **Simultaneity is relative:** two events at the same time for one observer can happen at different times for another moving past.
- Nothing with mass can reach the speed of light: speeding up gets harder and harder, needing unlimited energy.
- Mass and energy are equivalent: $E = mc^{2}$.
:::

::: example
A spaceship 100 m long at rest flies past Earth at $0.6c$ ($\gamma = 1.25$). Earth measures it
$$L = \frac{100}{1.25} = 80 \text{ m}$$
long. Its crew, measuring with their own rulers, still find 100 m.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="80"
A 100 m spaceship passes Earth at $0.6c$ ($\gamma = 1.25$). How long is it, as measured from Earth, in metres?
solution: $100 / 1.25 = 80$ m.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="18"
A 30 m rocket flies at $0.8c$ ($\gamma = 5/3$). How long is it, as measured from Earth, in metres?
solution: $30 / (5/3) = 30 \times 3/5 = 18$ m.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="100"
How long does the crew of the 100 m ship in problem 1 measure their own ship to be, in metres?
solution: They're at rest relative to their ship: 100 m.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Why can't a rocket be pushed past the speed of light, even with an endless supply of fuel?
- [ ] Space has friction.
- [x] The closer it gets to $c$, the more energy each further bit of speed takes, without limit.
- [ ] The rocket would melt.
solution: The energy needed grows without bound as $v$ approaches $c$.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="18"
How much energy is 2 kg of mass equivalent to, in units of $10^{16}$ J?
solution: $2 \times 9 \times 10^{16} = 18 \times 10^{16}$ J.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
GPS satellites carry very precise clocks. Engineers must:
- [x] correct them for relativity, or positions would drift by kilometres a day
- [ ] ignore relativity, since satellites are slow
- [ ] reset them each hour by hand
solution: Their speed (special relativity) and weaker gravity (general relativity) both shift their time; uncorrected, errors would pile up fast.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
A train speeds past a platform. Lightning strikes both ends of the train at the same instant according to someone on the platform, standing exactly halfway between the strikes. A passenger at the middle of the train will see:
- [ ] both flashes at the same moment
- [x] the flash from the front of the train first
- [ ] the flash from the back first
solution: The passenger moves toward the front flash and away from the back one, so the front light reaches them first. Since both measure the same light speed, the passenger concludes the front strike really happened first. "At the same time" depends on who is watching.
:::
