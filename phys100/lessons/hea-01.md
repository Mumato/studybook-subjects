title: Temperature, heat and expansion
---
Touch a hot pan and atoms in your fingers get jostled harder. That jostling is what temperature measures, and heat is the energy that flows when things at different temperatures touch. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 13-14)

::: rule
- **Temperature** measures the average kinetic energy of the jiggling atoms. On the **Kelvin** scale, 0 K is **absolute zero**, where that motion is as small as it can be: $K = °C + 273$.
- **Heat** is energy that flows from a hotter object to a colder one. A bathtub of warm water holds more total thermal energy than a cup of boiling water.
- **Specific heat** $c$ says how much heat it takes to warm 1 kg by 1 °C. Water's is very high, about 4200 J per kg per °C:
$$Q = m c \Delta T$$
- Most things **expand** when heated. Water is odd: it expands when it freezes, so ice floats.
:::

::: example
Warming 3 kg of water from 20 °C to 30 °C takes
$$Q = 3 \times 4200 \times 10 = 126000 \text{ J}$$
The same mass of sand needs only about a fifth as much, which is why beach sand gets scorching while the sea stays cool.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="300"
A room is at 27 °C. What is that in kelvin?
solution: $27 + 273 = 300$ K.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="-273"
What is absolute zero, 0 K, in degrees Celsius?
solution: $0 - 273 = -273$ °C.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="84000"
How much heat warms 2 kg of water by 10 °C, in joules? (Use $c = 4200$ J per kg per °C.)
solution: $2 \times 4200 \times 10 = 84000$ J.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
On a sunny day, the sand at the beach is hot while the sea is cool. Why?
- [ ] The sea is in shade.
- [x] Water has a much higher specific heat, so the same sunshine warms it less.
- [ ] Sand is closer to the Sun.
solution: Each kilogram of water needs far more energy for each degree of warming.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A strip of brass and steel stuck together bends when heated, as shown. Which metal expands more?
![](assets/hea-strip.3x.png)
- [x] brass, on the outside of the curve
- [ ] steel, on the inside of the curve
- [ ] neither: the strip bends because it gets softer
solution: The longer metal ends up on the outside of the curve. Thermostats use strips like this as switches.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Why does ice float on water?
- [ ] It has air bubbles in it.
- [x] Water expands when it freezes, so ice is less dense than liquid water.
- [ ] Cold things rise.
solution: Water molecules lock into an open crystal when they freeze. That's also why lakes freeze from the top down, and fish survive below.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
A full bathtub of water at 40 °C and a cup of water at 90 °C. Which has more total thermal (internal) energy?
- [ ] the cup, since it's hotter
- [x] the bathtub, since it has far more water
- [ ] they're the same
solution: Temperature is the *average* energy per atom; the bathtub has vastly more atoms.
:::
