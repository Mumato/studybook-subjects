title: Sound
---
Sound is a longitudinal wave of squeezes and stretches in air. A drum skin or a vocal cord pushes the air, and the pattern of pressure travels to your ear. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 17)

::: rule
- Sound needs a **medium**: it can't cross empty space. In air it travels about **340 m/s**, faster in water (about 1500 m/s) and steel (about 5000 m/s).
- **Pitch** is set by **frequency**: higher frequency, higher note. People hear about 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz. Higher is **ultrasound**.
- **Loudness** is set by **amplitude**. Loudness is measured in decibels: every +10 dB means 10 times the intensity.
- An **echo** is sound reflected back: it travels there *and* back.
:::

::: example
You see lightning and hear the thunder 3 s later. Light arrives almost instantly, so the sound took 3 s:
$$d = 340 \times 3 = 1020 \text{ m}$$
About 1 km away. (Rule of thumb: 3 seconds per kilometre.)
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="1700"
Thunder arrives 5 s after the lightning flash. How far away was the strike, in metres?
solution: $340 \times 5 = 1700$ m.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="340"
You shout at a cliff and hear the echo 2 s later. How far away is the cliff, in metres?
hint: The sound goes there and back.
solution: The sound travels $340 \times 2 = 680$ m in total, so the cliff is half of that: 340 m away.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Two notes are played. Note A vibrates at 440 Hz, note B at 880 Hz. Compared with A, note B sounds:
- [ ] lower
- [x] higher
- [ ] louder, but the same pitch
solution: Higher frequency, higher pitch. (880 Hz is exactly one octave above 440 Hz.)
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
An astronaut on the Moon drops a hammer next to another astronaut. Does the second astronaut hear it hit the ground through the "air"?
- [ ] yes, but quietly
- [x] no: there's no air to carry the sound
- [ ] yes, louder than on Earth
solution: Sound needs a medium. (They could feel it through the ground, though.)
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="100"
A concert is 20 dB louder than a busy street. How many times more intense is the concert sound?
solution: Each +10 dB is ×10: $10 \times 10 = 100$ times.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="0.5"
What is the wavelength of a 680 Hz sound in air, in metres?
solution: $\lambda = 340 / 680 = 0.5$ m.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Bats find insects with sounds of about 50 000 Hz. These sounds are:
- [ ] infrasound, too low for us to hear
- [x] ultrasound, too high for us to hear
- [ ] audible to people
solution: Above 20 000 Hz is ultrasound. Its short wavelength lets bats detect tiny insects.
:::
