title: Machines and efficiency
---
A lever lets a child lift a grown-up; a ramp lets you push a piano into a truck. Machines can multiply force, but never energy. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 7, 9)

::: rule
In an ideal machine, **work in = work out**:
$$F_{in} \times d_{in} = F_{out} \times d_{out}$$
- To get a **bigger** force out, you must push over a **longer** distance. Levers, ramps and pulleys all trade distance for force.
- Real machines lose some energy to friction, as heat. **Efficiency** = useful energy out ÷ energy in, usually as a percent.
- The lost energy isn't destroyed: it becomes heat. Energy just changes form.
:::

![](assets/enr-lever@3x.png)

::: example
In the picture, the long arm is 4 times the short arm. Pushing down 100 N on the long end lifts 400 N. But the long end must move 4 times as far as the load rises:
$$100 \times 1 \text{ m} = 400 \times 0.25 \text{ m}$$
Same work, 100 J, on both sides.
:::

::: example
A car engine burns fuel with 1000 J of chemical energy and does 250 J of useful work. Its efficiency is $250 / 1000 = 25\%$. The other 750 J heats the engine, exhaust and air.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="400"
You push down on a lever with 100 N and your end moves 1 m. The load end rises 0.25 m. If the lever is ideal, how big a load can it lift, in newtons?
solution: $100 \times 1 = F \times 0.25$, so $F = 400$ N.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="100"
You push a 500 N box up a smooth ramp 5 m long to a height of 1 m. With no friction, how hard must you push along the ramp, in newtons?
hint: Work in (push × ramp length) = work out (weight × height).
solution: $F \times 5 = 500 \times 1$, so $F = 100$ N. Easier than lifting 500 N, but over 5 times the distance.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="25"
A machine takes in 800 J and gives out 200 J of useful work. What is its efficiency, in percent?
solution: $200 / 800 = 0.25 = 25\%$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="90"
An old light bulb takes in 100 J of electrical energy and gives out 10 J of light. How many joules become heat?
solution: Energy is conserved: $100 - 10 = 90$ J of heat. That's why old bulbs get so hot.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A salesman says his new lever "puts out more work than you put in". What's wrong?
- [ ] Nothing, levers do multiply work.
- [x] It breaks conservation of energy: a machine can multiply force, never work.
- [ ] Levers can only multiply distance.
solution: Work out can at best equal work in. More force out always means less distance out.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="800"
A pulley system is 80% efficient. You do 1000 J of work pulling the rope. How much useful work is done lifting the load, in joules?
solution: $0.8 \times 1000 = 800$ J. The other 200 J heats the rope and pulleys.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
A ball is dropped, bounces, and rises less high each time until it stops. Where did its energy go?
- [ ] It was destroyed in the bounces.
- [x] It turned into heat and sound in the ball, the floor and the air.
- [ ] It went back into the Earth's gravity.
solution: Energy is never destroyed. Each bounce turns some of it into thermal energy and sound.
:::
