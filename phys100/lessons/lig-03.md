title: Lenses, eyes and images
---
A lens is just shaped glass that bends light on purpose. Cameras, telescopes, glasses and your own eye all use lenses to make images. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 25-26)

::: rule
- A **converging** lens (thicker in the middle) bends parallel rays to meet at the **focal point**. Its distance from the lens is the **focal length** $f$.
- A **diverging** lens (thinner in the middle) spreads rays apart.
- **Lens power** in diopters $= 1 / f$, with $f$ in metres.
- An object farther than $f$ from a converging lens gives a **real, upside-down** image (cameras, your eye's retina). An object closer than $f$ gives an **upright, enlarged** virtual image: a magnifying glass.
- **Nearsighted** eyes focus in front of the retina and need diverging lenses; **farsighted** eyes need converging lenses.
:::

::: example
A magnifying glass focuses sunlight to a hot spot 20 cm from the lens. Its focal length is 20 cm = 0.2 m, so its power is $1/0.2 = 5$ diopters. To use it as a magnifier, hold it less than 20 cm from the page.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="10"
A lens focuses sunlight into a small bright spot 10 cm away. What is its focal length, in centimetres?
solution: Sunlight arrives as parallel rays, so they meet at the focal point: $f = 10$ cm.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="2"
A lens has a focal length of 0.5 m. What is its power, in diopters?
solution: $1 / 0.5 = 2$ diopters.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="0.25"
A reading-glasses lens has a power of 4 diopters. What is its focal length, in metres?
solution: $f = 1/4 = 0.25$ m.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
To see an enlarged, upright view of an insect with a magnifying glass, the insect must be:
- [ ] exactly at the focal point
- [x] closer to the lens than the focal point
- [ ] much farther than the focal point
solution: Inside the focal length, a converging lens gives an upright, magnified virtual image.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
The image your eye's lens forms on your retina is:
- [ ] upright
- [x] upside down
- [ ] not focused
solution: It's a real image, so it's inverted. Your brain flips it.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
A nearsighted person sees close things clearly but far things blurry. Their glasses have:
- [x] diverging lenses
- [ ] converging lenses
- [ ] flat glass
solution: Their eye bends light too strongly, focusing in front of the retina. A diverging lens spreads the light a little first.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
Underwater without goggles, everything looks blurry. With goggles, it's sharp. Why?
- [ ] Salt water irritates the eyes.
- [x] The eye's cornea does most of its focusing because light slows going from air into it. Water and the cornea have similar indices, so in water that bending almost vanishes. Goggles put air back in front of the eye.
- [ ] Light travels faster underwater.
solution: Refraction happens only where the index of refraction changes. Water against the eye barely changes it, so the eye can't focus.
:::
