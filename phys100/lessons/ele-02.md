title: Current, voltage and resistance
---
A circuit is like water pipes: a pump pushes water around, narrow pipes resist the flow. In a wire, the "pump" is a battery's voltage and the "water" is moving charge. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 20)

::: rule
- **Current** $I$: how much charge flows past each second, in **amperes** (A).
- **Voltage** $V$: the electrical "push" (energy per charge), in **volts** (V).
- **Resistance** $R$: how hard it is for current to flow, in **ohms** (Ω).
$$I = \frac{V}{R} \qquad P = I V$$
- **Ohm's law** (left) gives the current. **Power** $P$ (right) is in watts. Energy used = power × time; your bill counts **kilowatt-hours** (kWh).
:::

::: example
A 12 V battery runs a bulb of resistance 6 Ω.
$$I = \frac{12}{6} = 2 \text{ A}$$
$$P = 2 \times 12 = 24 \text{ W}$$
Left on for 10 hours, it uses $24 \times 10 = 240$ Wh $= 0.24$ kWh.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="3"
A 12 V battery is connected across a 4 Ω resistor. What current flows, in amperes?
solution: $I = 12 / 4 = 3$ A.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="12"
A current of 2 A flows through a 6 Ω resistor. What is the voltage across it, in volts?
solution: $V = I R = 2 \times 6 = 12$ V.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="60"
A lamp on a 120 V outlet draws 0.5 A. What is its power, in watts?
solution: $P = 0.5 \times 120 = 60$ W.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="5"
A 600 W microwave runs on a 120 V outlet. What current does it draw, in amperes?
solution: $I = P / V = 600 / 120 = 5$ A.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="3"
A 1000 W heater runs for 3 hours. How many kilowatt-hours does it use?
solution: $1 \text{ kW} \times 3 \text{ h} = 3$ kWh.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="45"
Electricity costs 15 cents per kWh. How many cents does running that heater for 3 hours cost?
solution: $3 \times 15 = 45$ cents.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Doubling the voltage across the same resistor:
- [ ] halves the current
- [x] doubles the current
- [ ] doesn't change the current
solution: $I = V/R$: with $R$ fixed, the current grows with the voltage.
:::
