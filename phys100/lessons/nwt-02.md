title: Net force and equilibrium
---
A force is a push or a pull, measured in **newtons** (N). One newton is about the weight of a small apple. Usually several forces act at once, and what matters is their combined effect. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 4)

::: rule
The **net force** is the sum of all the forces on an object.
- Forces in the **same** direction add. Forces in **opposite** directions subtract, and the net force points the way of the bigger one.
- If the net force is zero, the object is in **equilibrium**: at rest, or moving at constant velocity.
- A surface pushes back on whatever presses on it. This **support force** (or **normal force**) is why a book on a table doesn't fall through.
:::

![Weight down, support up: net force zero](assets/nwt-book.3x.png)

::: example
Two people push a box: one with 30 N to the right, one with 20 N to the left.
$$F_{net} = 30 - 20 = 10 \text{ N}$$
to the right.
If both pushed to the right, the net force would be $30 + 20 = 50$ N.
:::

::: example
A lamp hangs at rest from a cord. It's in equilibrium, so the cord pulls up exactly as hard as the lamp's weight pulls down.

![](assets/nwt-lamp.3x.png)
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="10"
What is the net force on the box, in newtons?
![](assets/nwt-net1.3x.png)
solution: $30 - 20 = 10$ N, to the right.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="25"
What is the size of the net force on the box, in newtons?
![](assets/nwt-net2.3x.png)
solution: $40 - 15 = 25$ N, to the left.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
The box in the picture is sliding to the right. What happens to its motion?
![](assets/nwt-net3.3x.png)
- [ ] It slows down, because the forces cancel.
- [x] It keeps sliding at the same speed.
- [ ] It speeds up to the right.
solution: The net force is zero, so its velocity doesn't change. Zero net force doesn't mean "stopped": it means "no change".
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="600"
A 600 N person stands still on the floor. How big is the support force from the floor, in newtons?
solution: The person is in equilibrium: support force = weight = 600 N, upward.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="300"
Two ropes hold an 800 N sign at rest, both pulling straight up. One rope pulls with 500 N. How hard does the other pull, in newtons?
hint: In equilibrium the upward pulls add up to the weight.
solution: $500 + T = 800$, so $T = 300$ N.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
A crate slides across the floor at a **constant** velocity while you push it with 50 N. The friction force on it is:
- [ ] less than 50 N
- [x] exactly 50 N
- [ ] more than 50 N
- [ ] zero
solution: Constant velocity means equilibrium: friction must cancel your push exactly.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
A hanging lamp weighs 20 N and hangs at rest. What is the tension in the cord?
- [ ] 0 N
- [ ] 10 N
- [x] 20 N
- [ ] 40 N
solution: At rest, the net force is zero: tension up = weight down = 20 N.
:::
