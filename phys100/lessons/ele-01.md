title: Electric charge and force
---
Rub a balloon on your hair and it sticks to the wall. The same force holds atoms together and runs every phone and computer: the electric force between charges. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 18)

::: rule
- There are two kinds of charge: **positive** (protons) and **negative** (electrons). **Like charges repel, unlike charges attract.**
- Charge is **conserved**: rubbing just moves electrons from one object to another.
- **Coulomb's law:** like gravity, the force weakens with the square of the distance:
$$F = k \frac{q_1 q_2}{d^{2}}$$
- In **conductors** (metals) some electrons move freely. In **insulators** (rubber, glass, plastic) they can't.
- A charged object attracts a neutral one by **polarizing** it: pulling its opposite charges slightly closer.
:::

![](assets/ele-charges.3x.png)

::: example
Rubbing a balloon on your hair moves electrons from your hair to the balloon. The balloon becomes negative and your hair positive, by exactly the same amount. Your hair strands, all positive, repel each other and stand up; the balloon pulls them toward it.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="1/4"
Two charges are moved twice as far apart. By what factor does the electric force between them change? (Type a fraction.)
solution: Inverse square: $\frac{1}{2^{2}} = \frac{1}{4}$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="3"
One of two charges is tripled; the distance stays the same. By what factor does the force change?
solution: The force is proportional to each charge: ×3.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="9"
Two charges are moved to a third of their distance apart. By what factor does the force change?
solution: $\frac{1}{(1/3)^{2}} = 9$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
After you rub a balloon on your hair, the balloon is negative. What happened?
- [ ] Positive charge moved from the balloon to your hair.
- [x] Electrons moved from your hair to the balloon.
- [ ] The rubbing created new negative charge.
solution: Electrons are what move; charge is never created.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A negatively charged balloon sticks to a neutral wall. Why?
![](assets/ele-balloon.3x.png)
- [ ] The wall is secretly positive.
- [x] The balloon pushes the wall's electrons slightly away, leaving the nearby surface a bit positive, which attracts the balloon.
- [ ] Gravity holds it.
solution: The near charges are positive and closer, so their attraction beats the repulsion from the farther negatives.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Metals conduct electricity well because:
- [x] some of their electrons are free to move through the metal
- [ ] they're heavy
- [ ] they have no electrons
solution: Those free electrons are what flows as current.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Two identical balls hang near each other. One is positive and one is negative. They will:
- [x] attract
- [ ] repel
- [ ] not affect each other
solution: Unlike charges attract.
:::
