title: Interference, resonance and the Doppler effect
---
Two waves can pass through each other, adding up or cancelling where they meet. Sources that move squeeze their waves. And pushes in time with a natural rhythm build huge vibrations. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 16-17)

::: rule
- **Interference:** where waves overlap, they add. Crest meets crest: a bigger wave (**constructive**). Crest meets trough: they cancel (**destructive**).
- A **standing wave** forms when a wave reflects back on itself: some points never move (**nodes**), others swing the most (**antinodes**). Neighbouring nodes are half a wavelength apart.
- **Resonance:** pushes at an object's natural frequency build up a large vibration.
- **Doppler effect:** a source moving toward you bunches its waves up (higher pitch); moving away, it spreads them out (lower pitch).
:::

![](assets/wav-interf@3x.png)

::: example
An ambulance siren drops in pitch as it passes you. Coming toward you, each wave crest is sent from a little closer, so the crests arrive closer together: higher frequency. Going away, they're stretched: lower frequency.

![](assets/wav-doppler@3x.png)
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
Noise-cancelling headphones make a sound wave that cancels outside noise. They rely on:
- [ ] constructive interference
- [x] destructive interference
- [ ] resonance
solution: They play the noise's mirror image: crests where the noise has troughs.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="4"
How many nodes does this standing wave have, counting both fixed ends?
![](assets/wav-standing@3x.png)
solution: Three loops are separated by nodes: the two ends plus two in between, 4 in all.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="2"
A 3 m string vibrates in the pattern above, with 3 loops. Each loop is half a wavelength. What is the wavelength, in metres?
![](assets/wav-standing@3x.png)
solution: Each loop is $3/3 = 1$ m, which is half a wavelength: $\lambda = 2$ m.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
A police car with its siren on drives toward you. Compared with the siren at rest, you hear:
- [x] a higher pitch
- [ ] a lower pitch
- [ ] the same pitch, but louder
solution: The waves arrive bunched up, at a higher frequency.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A singer shatters a wine glass by holding one note. Why that note?
- [ ] it's the loudest note she can sing
- [x] it matches the glass's natural frequency, so resonance builds up a large vibration
- [ ] high notes are always destructive
solution: Each push arrives in step with the glass's own vibration and adds to it.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
A jet flying faster than sound makes a sonic boom because:
- [ ] its engines explode
- [x] it outruns its own sound waves, which pile up into a cone-shaped shock wave
- [ ] the air freezes
solution: The extreme Doppler case: the crests can't get ahead of the plane, so they overlap into one huge pressure wave.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
Two speakers play the same steady tone. Walking slowly across the room, you hear loud spots and quiet spots. Why?
- [ ] The speakers keep changing volume.
- [x] At some spots the two waves arrive in step and add; at others one arrives crest-to-trough with the other and they cancel.
- [ ] The walls absorb sound unevenly.
solution: The difference in distance to the two speakers sets whether the waves arrive in step. Every half wavelength of extra distance flips loud to quiet.
:::
