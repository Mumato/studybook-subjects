title: Fission, fusion and E = mc²
---
A tiny amount of mass holds an enormous amount of energy. Nuclear power plants release it by splitting heavy nuclei; the Sun releases it by joining light ones. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 28, 32)

::: rule
$$E = m c^{2}$$
- Mass is a form of energy. Since $c^{2} = 9 \times 10^{16}$ m²/s², a little mass is a lot of energy.
- **Fission:** a heavy nucleus such as uranium-235 absorbs a neutron and splits into two medium nuclei plus 2-3 neutrons. Those neutrons can split more nuclei: a **chain reaction**.
- **Fusion:** light nuclei (hydrogen) join into heavier ones (helium). It powers the Sun, but needs millions of degrees to push the positive nuclei together.
- In both, the products have slightly **less mass** than what went in. The missing mass comes out as energy.
:::

::: example
Turning 1 g (0.001 kg) of mass entirely into energy gives
$$E = 0.001 \times 9 \times 10^{16} = 9 \times 10^{13} \text{ J}$$
about the energy of 20 000 tonnes of TNT. A nuclear plant turns only about a gram of mass into energy each day.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="9"
How much energy is 1 kg of mass equivalent to, in units of $10^{16}$ J?
solution: $1 \times 9 \times 10^{16}$ J: 9.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="18"
How much energy is 2 g (0.002 kg) of mass equivalent to, in units of $10^{13}$ J?
solution: $0.002 \times 9 \times 10^{16} = 18 \times 10^{13}$ J.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
What keeps a fission chain reaction going?
![](assets/nuc-fission.3x.png)
- [ ] heat from each split
- [x] the neutrons released by each fission, which split more nuclei
- [ ] electricity
solution: In a reactor, control rods soak up spare neutrons to keep it steady.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
What powers the Sun?
- [ ] burning (chemical reactions)
- [ ] fission of uranium
- [x] fusion of hydrogen into helium
solution: Deep in the Sun's core, the temperature and pressure are high enough to fuse hydrogen.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
After uranium splits, the total mass of the pieces is:
- [ ] more than the original
- [x] slightly less than the original
- [ ] exactly the same
solution: The "missing" mass became the energy released.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Why is fusion so hard to achieve on Earth?
- [ ] Hydrogen is rare.
- [x] The nuclei are positive and repel, so they must be heated to millions of degrees to collide hard enough.
- [ ] Fusion releases too little energy.
solution: Only at enormous temperatures do nuclei move fast enough to overcome their electric repulsion.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
Splitting uranium releases energy, and so does fusing hydrogen. How can both breaking up and joining release energy?
- [ ] One of them actually uses energy.
- [x] Medium nuclei (around iron) are the most tightly bound. Heavy nuclei release energy by splitting toward iron; light nuclei release energy by fusing toward it.
- [ ] Energy is created in nuclear reactions.
solution: Nucleons in medium-sized nuclei have the least mass each. Moving toward iron from either side turns a little mass into energy.
:::
