title: Rotational inertia and angular momentum
---
Mass resists changes in motion; **rotational inertia** resists changes in spin. And spinning things carry a quantity that, like momentum, is conserved. (OpenStax College Physics 2e ch. 10)

::: rule
- **Rotational inertia** depends on mass *and where it is*: mass far from the axis makes something much harder to start or stop spinning.
- **Angular momentum** = rotational inertia × rotational speed.
- **Conservation of angular momentum:** with no outside torque, angular momentum stays the same. Pull mass in (less rotational inertia) and you spin faster; spread it out and you spin slower.
$$I_1 \omega_1 = I_2 \omega_2$$
:::

![](assets/rot-skater.3x.png)

::: example
A skater spins at 2 turns per second with arms out. Pulling her arms in cuts her rotational inertia to a third. Her spin rate triples:
$$I \times 2 = \tfrac{1}{3} I \times \omega$$
so $\omega = 6$ turns per second.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="4"
A diver leaves the board turning once per second, then tucks into a ball, cutting her rotational inertia to one quarter. How many turns per second does she make now?
solution: $1 \times I = \omega \times \tfrac{1}{4} I$, so $\omega = 4$ turns/s.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="3"
A spinning chair turns at 1 turn per second while you hold heavy weights near your chest. You stretch your arms out and your rotational inertia triples. How many seconds does one turn take now?
solution: Angular momentum stays the same, so the rotational speed drops to $\tfrac{1}{3}$ turn per second: one turn takes 3 s.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A solid disc and a ring (hoop) with the same mass and radius roll down the same ramp. Which reaches the bottom first?
- [ ] the ring
- [x] the solid disc
- [ ] they tie
solution: The ring has all its mass at the rim, so it has more rotational inertia and is harder to get spinning. More of its energy goes into spin, less into speed.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Why does a tightrope walker carry a long pole?
- [ ] to be heavier
- [x] it has large rotational inertia, so the walker tips more slowly and has time to correct
- [ ] to push off the rope
solution: Mass far from the axis resists turning, so any wobble starts slowly.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
When you run, you bend your legs at the knee as you swing them forward. This:
- [x] brings the leg's mass closer to the hip, so it's easier to swing
- [ ] makes the leg heavier
- [ ] increases the leg's rotational inertia
solution: Less rotational inertia means less effort to swing. Long-legged animals have thin lower legs for the same reason.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
A helicopter has a small rotor on its tail. Without it, when the main rotor spins one way:
- [x] the body would spin the opposite way
- [ ] nothing would happen
- [ ] the helicopter would fly faster
solution: The engine twists the rotor one way and the rotor twists the body back. The total angular momentum stays zero unless the tail rotor pushes against the air.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
Suppose everyone on Earth moved to the equator. What would happen to the length of a day, very slightly?
- [ ] It would get shorter.
- [x] It would get longer.
- [ ] It would stay exactly the same.
solution: Moving mass away from Earth's axis raises its rotational inertia. Angular momentum stays the same, so Earth spins a tiny bit slower: a longer day. (Melting polar ice moving toward the equator does this for real.)
:::
