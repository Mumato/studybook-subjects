---
::: problem id=t1 type=numeric answer="3"
One photon has three times the frequency of another. By what factor is its energy greater?
solution: $E = hf$: ×3.
:::

::: problem id=t2 type=choice
In the photoelectric effect, whether any electrons come out depends on the light's:
- [ ] brightness
- [x] frequency (colour)
- [ ] direction
solution: Each photon must carry enough energy on its own.
:::

::: problem id=t3 type=numeric answer="6.6" tolerance=0.01
What is the energy of a photon of frequency $10^{15}$ Hz, in units of $10^{-19}$ J? ($h = 6.6 \times 10^{-34}$ J·s)
solution: $6.6 \times 10^{-34} \times 10^{15} = 6.6 \times 10^{-19}$ J.
:::

::: problem id=t4 type=choice
Light behaves:
- [ ] only as a wave
- [ ] only as particles
- [x] as both: waves when travelling, photons when absorbed or emitted
solution: Wave-particle duality.
:::

::: problem id=t5 type=numeric answer="2.5" tolerance=0.01
An electron drops from a level at −3 eV to one at −5.5 eV. What energy photon comes out, in eV?
solution: $-3 - (-5.5) = 2.5$ eV.
:::

::: problem id=t6 type=choice
Most of an atom's mass is in its:
- [ ] electrons
- [x] nucleus
- [ ] empty space
solution: The nucleus is tiny but holds nearly all the mass.
:::

::: problem id=t7 type=choice
A gas glows in a few sharp colours, not a full rainbow, because:
- [x] its electrons can only drop between fixed energy levels
- [ ] the gas is cold
- [ ] the tube filters the light
solution: Fixed levels mean only certain photon energies, so only certain colours.
:::

::: problem id=t8 type=numeric answer="6"
An electron sits in level 4. Counting every possible jump down (one level or more at a time) toward level 1, how many different photon energies are possible?
solution: 4→3, 4→2, 4→1, 3→2, 3→1, 2→1: six.
:::
