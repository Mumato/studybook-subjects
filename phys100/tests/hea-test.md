::: problem id=t1 type=numeric answer="373"
Water boils at 100 °C. What is that in kelvin?
solution: $100 + 273 = 373$ K.
:::

::: problem id=t2 type=numeric answer="42000"
How much heat raises 1 kg of water by 10 °C, in joules? (Use $c = 4200$ J per kg per °C.)
solution: $1 \times 4200 \times 10 = 42000$ J.
:::

::: problem id=t3 type=choice
Temperature is a measure of:
- [ ] the total energy in an object
- [x] the average kinetic energy of its atoms
- [ ] how much heat it contains
solution: It's an average per atom, not a total.
:::

::: problem id=t4 type=choice
A wool sweater keeps you warm mainly because:
- [ ] wool produces heat
- [x] it traps still air, a poor conductor
- [ ] it reflects radiation
solution: Wool's tangled fibres hold air still, so heat escapes slowly.
:::

::: problem id=t5 type=choice
Heat reaches you from a campfire mostly by:
- [ ] conduction
- [ ] convection
- [x] radiation
solution: Hot air rises *above* the fire (convection), but your face, beside it, is warmed by infrared radiation.
:::

::: problem id=t6 type=numeric answer="25"
An engine runs between 400 K and 300 K. What is its ideal efficiency, in percent?
solution: $1 - 300/400 = 0.25 = 25\%$.
:::

::: problem id=t7 type=numeric answer="600"
A gas takes in 800 J of heat and does 200 J of work. By how much does its internal energy rise, in joules?
solution: $800 - 200 = 600$ J.
:::

::: problem id=t8 type=choice
While ice is melting at 0 °C, adding heat:
- [ ] raises its temperature
- [x] melts more ice, with the temperature staying at 0 °C
- [ ] lowers its temperature
solution: The heat breaks the crystal bonds; the temperature stays flat until all the ice has melted.
:::
