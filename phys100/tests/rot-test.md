::: problem id=t1 type=choice
A ladybug sits near the edge of a spinning record and another near the middle. Which has the greater linear speed?
- [x] the one near the edge
- [ ] the one near the middle
- [ ] they are equal
solution: Same rotational speed, bigger circle: faster.
:::

::: problem id=t2 type=choice
What keeps the Moon moving in a (nearly) circular path around Earth?
- [ ] its inertia alone
- [x] Earth's gravity, pulling it toward Earth
- [ ] a centrifugal force
solution: Gravity is the centripetal force. Without it, the Moon would fly off in a straight line.
:::

::: problem id=t3 type=numeric answer="200"
A 2 kg ball on a 1 m string whirls at 10 m/s. What tension must the string supply, in newtons? ($F = mv^{2}/r$)
solution: $2 \times 10^{2} / 1 = 200$ N.
:::

::: problem id=t4 type=numeric answer="30"
You push on a door 1.5 m from its hinges with 20 N, at right angles. What torque do you apply, in N·m?
solution: $20 \times 1.5 = 30$ N·m.
:::

::: problem id=t5 type=numeric answer="2"
A 300 N child sits 2 m from a seesaw's pivot. Where must a 300 N child sit on the other side to balance, in metres?
solution: Equal weights need equal lever arms: 2 m.
:::

::: problem id=t6 type=choice
A tall, narrow bookcase is more likely to tip over than a short, wide one because:
- [x] its centre of gravity is high and its base is narrow
- [ ] it is heavier
- [ ] it has more rotational inertia
solution: A small tilt moves a high CG past a narrow base.
:::

::: problem id=t7 type=numeric answer="3"
A spinning ice skater turns once per second, then pulls in her arms so her rotational inertia drops to one third. How many turns per second does she make?
solution: Angular momentum is conserved: $\omega = 1 \times 3 = 3$ turns/s.
:::

::: problem id=t8 type=choice
Which is easier to start spinning about its centre: a 1 kg solid disc or a 1 kg ring of the same size?
- [x] the solid disc
- [ ] the ring
- [ ] equally easy
solution: The ring's mass is all far from the axis, giving it more rotational inertia.
:::
