---
id: tef-03
title: Linking actions: ～て and ～てから
---
English joins actions with "and" and "then". Japanese uses the て-form: every verb except the last goes into the て-form, and the last one shows the tense.

::: rule
- **A and B (in order)**: A in て-form, then B: !say[あさ おきて、シャワーを あびて、がっこうに いきます。] *In the morning I get up, take a shower and go to school.*
- **Tense is set by the last verb**: !say[きのう ともだちに あって、えいがを みました。] *Yesterday I met a friend and saw a movie.* (あって is past because みました is.)
- **After doing A**: て-form + **から**: !say[しゅくだいを してから、テレビを みます。] *I'll watch TV after I do my homework.*
- The て-form can also give a **reason** in a simple way: !say[かぜを ひいて、がっこうを やすみました。] *I caught a cold and (so) missed school.*
- Two linked requests: !say[ここに なまえを かいて、はんこを おして ください。] *Please write your name here and stamp it.*
:::

::: example
A Saturday:

!say[m|どようびは じゅうじに おきて、ジムに いきました。それから ともだちと ひるごはんを たべて、かいものを しました。うちに かえってから、すこし ねました。]

*On Saturday I got up at ten and went to the gym. Then I had lunch with a friend and went shopping. After I got home, I slept a little.*
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=text answers="おきてかおをあらいます"
Type "I get up and wash my face." (face = かお, wash = あらう)
hint: okite, kao o araimasu
solution: おきて、かおを あらいます。
Hear it: !say[おきて、かおを あらいます。]
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
きのう こうえんに いって、さんぽしました means:
- [x] Yesterday I went to the park and took a walk.
- [ ] Yesterday I will go to the park and walk.
- [ ] I went to the park because I walked.
solution: The last verb (さんぽしました) is past, so the whole sentence is past.
Hear it: !say[きのう こうえんに いって、さんぽしました。]
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=text answers="てをあらってからたべます"
Type "I eat after washing my hands." (hands = て)
hint: te o aratte kara tabemasu
solution: てを あらってから たべます。
Hear it: !say[てを あらってから たべます。]
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Which sentence means "After I graduate, I'll work in Japan"? (graduate = そつぎょうする)
- [x] そつぎょうしてから、にほんで はたらきます。
- [ ] そつぎょうしますから、にほんで はたらきます。
- [ ] にほんで はたらいてから、そつぎょうします。
solution: ～てから means "after". ～ますから would mean "because".
Hear it: !say[そつぎょうしてから、にほんで はたらきます。]
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Fill in: でんしゃに ＿、かいしゃに いきます。 (I take the train and go to work.)
- [ ] のりますて
- [x] のって
- [ ] のりて
solution: のる (ride) is Group 1: のって.
Hear it: !say[でんしゃに のって、かいしゃに いきます。]
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Listen: !listen[m|うちに かえってから、ばんごはんを つくりました。]
What did the speaker do first?
- [x] went home
- [ ] made dinner
- [ ] ate out
solution: かえってから: after going home, (they) made dinner.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Listen: !listen[あさ コーヒーを のんで、しんぶんを よみます。]
What does the speaker do in the morning?
- [ ] reads the paper, then has coffee
- [x] has coffee and reads the paper
- [ ] has coffee instead of reading
solution: のんで、よみます: drinks coffee and (then) reads the paper.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=choice creative=true
English can say "I'll eat, then go", putting the tense on both verbs. Japanese marks tense only on the last verb. What does that mean for listening?
- [x] You often don't know whether a sentence is past or future until its very end, so Japanese listeners wait for the last verb.
- [ ] Japanese has no tenses.
- [ ] The first verb always shows the tense.
solution: Japanese puts the most important information (tense, negation, politeness, question) at the end. Get used to listening all the way to the end of a sentence.
:::
