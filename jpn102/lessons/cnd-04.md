---
id: cnd-04
title: Reading: an email to a professor
---
Writing to a teacher in Japanese has its own polite style. This email from a student uses ～ので, ～んです, ～たら and ～とき to explain an absence and ask for help.

::: rule
How a polite email works:
- **Opening**: the professor's name + 先生, then the writer identifies themselves (class and name).
- **The reason** comes with ので or ～んです, softly, before the request.
- **The request** uses ～て いただけませんか or ～ても よろしいでしょうか, extra-polite forms of ～て くださいませんか and ～ても いいですか.
- **Closing**: よろしく おねがいいたします (the humble form of よろしく おねがいします).
:::

::: example
{山田|やまだ}{先生|せんせい}

にほんご II の リー・ミンです。

きのうは ねつが あったので、じゅぎょうを やすみました。もうしわけありません。びょういんに いったら、かぜだと いわれました。

しゅくだいの プリントを まだ もらって いないんです。けんきゅうしつに いった とき、プリントを いただいても よろしいでしょうか。

あしたは げんきに なったら、じゅぎょうに でます。

よろしく おねがいいたします。

リー・ミン

New words: もうしわけありません I'm very sorry, いわれました was told, プリント handout, けんきゅうしつ professor's office, いただく receive (humble), じゅぎょうに でる attend class.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
Why did Lee miss class?
- [x] She had a fever.
- [ ] She overslept.
- [ ] She went to the hospital for a check-up.
solution: ねつが あったので: because she had a fever.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
What did the doctor say?
- [x] It's a cold.
- [ ] She should rest for a week.
- [ ] Nothing was wrong.
solution: かぜだと いわれました: she was told it was a cold.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
What hasn't Lee received yet?
- [ ] her test results
- [x] the homework handout
- [ ] the textbook
solution: しゅくだいの プリントを まだ もらって いないんです.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
What is she asking?
- [x] whether she may get the handout when she visits the professor's office
- [ ] whether she can skip the homework
- [ ] whether the professor can email the handout
solution: けんきゅうしつに いった とき、プリントを いただいても よろしいでしょうか.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
What will she do tomorrow?
- [x] attend class if she's better
- [ ] stay home all day
- [ ] go back to the hospital
solution: げんきに なったら、じゅぎょうに でます: if (once) she's well, she'll attend.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Why does the email use ～ので and ～んです instead of から?
- [x] They sound softer and more polite when explaining reasons to a superior.
- [ ] から is wrong grammar.
- [ ] They're more casual.
solution: ので and ～んです give reasons gently; から can sound like an excuse or a demand.
:::
