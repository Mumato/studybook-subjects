id: cnd-03
title: Reasons and explanations: ～ので, ～んです
---
You already know から (because). **ので** also gives reasons, more softly and politely, and **んです** turns a sentence into an explanation, which is how Japanese people ask what's going on and explain themselves.

::: rule
- **ので** (because, so): plain form + ので: !say[あたまが いたいので、はやく かえります。] *I have a headache, so I'll go home early.* Nouns and な-adjectives use **なので**: !say[あしたは やすみなので、ゆっくり ねます。]
- ので sounds softer and more objective than から, so it's preferred for excuses and polite requests: !say[よく わからないので、もう いちど せつめいして ください。]
- **んです** (explaining): plain form + **んです**: !say[きのう、がっこうを やすんだんです。] *(The thing is,) I was off school yesterday.* Nouns and な-adjectives use **なんです**: !say[びょうきなんです。]
- **Asking for an explanation**: !say[どうしたんですか。] *What's wrong? (What happened?)* !say[どうして おくれたんですか。] *Why were you late?*
- Giving the reason: !say[でんしゃが おくれたんです。] *The train was late (that's why).*
- Overusing んです in plain statements sounds pushy, so use it when there's something to explain.
:::

::: example
Arriving late to class:

- Teacher: !say[m|どうしたんですか。ずいぶん おそいですね。] *What happened? You're very late.*
- Student: !say[すみません。でんしゃが とまったんです。じこが あったので。] *I'm sorry. The train stopped. Because there was an accident.*
- Teacher: !say[m|そうですか。たいへんでしたね。] *I see. That must have been tough.*
:::
