---
id: pat-03
title: Going to do, already and not yet: ～にいく, もう, まだ
---
Say why you're going somewhere ("I'm going to buy bread"), and whether something has happened yet.

::: rule
- **Go / come to do**: ます-stem + **に いく / くる / かえる**: !say[パンを かいに いきます。] *I'm going (out) to buy bread.* !say[にほんへ にほんごを べんきょうしに きました。] *I came to Japan to study Japanese.* With する-nouns you can drop する: !say[かいものに いきます。]
- **Already**: **もう** + past: !say[もう ひるごはんを たべました。] *I've already had lunch.*
- **Not yet**: **まだ** + て-form + **いません**: !say[まだ たべて いません。] *I haven't eaten yet.* (Not たべませんでした, which means "didn't eat" at a finished time.)
- **Asking**: !say[もう しゅくだいを しましたか。] *Have you done your homework yet?* Answers: !say[はい、もう しました。] / !say[いいえ、まだです。]
- **Still**: まだ + positive: !say[まだ ねて います。] *(He)'s still asleep.*
:::

::: example
- A: !say[もう ばんごはんを たべましたか。] *Have you had dinner yet?*
- B: !say[m|いいえ、まだ たべて いません。] *No, not yet.*
- A: !say[じゃあ、いっしょに ラーメンを たべに いきませんか。] *Then would you like to go and eat ramen together?*
- B: !say[m|いいですね！ いきましょう。] *Great! Let's go.*
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=choice creative=true
In English "I didn't eat" and "I haven't eaten" are different. Japanese uses たべませんでした and まだ たべて いません. Why do you think Japanese uses the ～ている form for "not yet"?
- [x] ～ている describes the current state: "I am in a not-having-eaten state", so it still applies now, while the plain past closes the matter.
- [ ] Japanese has no past tense.
- [ ] It's random.
solution: Answering "まだ たべませんでした" sounds like you had your chance and missed it. "まだ たべて いません" leaves it open: you might still eat.
:::
