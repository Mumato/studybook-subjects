title: Composition, kernel and range
---
Doing one transformation after another multiplies their matrices. And two subspaces tell you what a transformation loses and what it can reach. (Nicholson ch. 2, 7)

::: rule
- **Composition:** "first $T$, then $S$" is $S \circ T$, with matrix $BA$ (where $A$ is $T$'s matrix and $B$ is $S$'s). The matrix of the **first** step goes on the **right**.
- The **kernel** of $T$ is everything sent to $\vec{0}$: the null space of $A$. The **range** (image) is everything $T$ can output: the column space of $A$.
- $T$ is **one-to-one** exactly when its kernel is $\{\vec{0}\}$; **onto** $\mathbb{R}^m$ exactly when its range is all of $\mathbb{R}^m$.
- $\dim \ker T + \dim \text{range}\,T = n$ (rank-nullity again).
:::

::: example
$R$ = rotation by 90° (rows $(0, -1)$, $(1, 0)$); $S$ = reflection in the $x$-axis (rows $(1, 0)$, $(0, -1)$).
"First $R$, then $S$" has matrix $SR$, with rows $(0, -1)$ and $(-1, 0)$: a reflection in the line $y = -x$.
In the other order, $RS$ has rows $(0, 1)$ and $(1, 0)$: reflection in $y = x$. Order matters.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="-1"
In the example, what is entry (2, 1) of $SR$?
solution: Row 2 of $S$, $(0, -1)$, times column 1 of $R$, $(0, 1)$: $-1$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=point answer="-1,-2"
Rotate $(2, 1)$ by 90°, then reflect it in the $x$-axis. Plot the result.
graph:
x -3..3
y -3..3
point 2,1 P blue
solution: Rotating gives $(-1, 2)$; reflecting gives $(-1, -2)$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="2"
$T(\vec{x}) = A\vec{x}$ with $A$ having rows $(1, 2, -1)$ and $(2, 4, -2)$. What is the dimension of the kernel?
solution: Rank 1 (row 2 = 2 × row 1). Nullity $= 3 - 1 = 2$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="1"
Same $T$. What is the dimension of its range?
solution: The rank: 1. The range is the line spanned by $(1, 2)$.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Same $T$. Is it one-to-one?
- [ ] Yes
- [x] No: its kernel isn't just $\vec{0}$
solution: Different inputs, like $\vec{0}$ and $(2, -1, 0)$, both go to $\vec{0}$.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
A linear $T: \mathbb{R}^3 \to \mathbb{R}^5$ can never be:
- [ ] one-to-one
- [x] onto $\mathbb{R}^5$
- [ ] the zero map
solution: Its range has dimension at most 3, less than 5.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
$T$ rotates the plane by 30°. Which transformation undoes it?
- [x] rotation by $-30°$
- [ ] rotation by 30°
- [ ] reflection in the $x$-axis
solution: The inverse of a rotation matrix is the rotation the other way, which is also its transpose.
:::
