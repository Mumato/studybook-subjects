title: Confidence intervals for a mean
---
A sample mean is a single best guess. A **confidence interval** adds a margin around it, wide enough to catch the true mean most of the time. (OpenStax ch. 8)

::: rule
When $\sigma$ is known:
$$\bar{x} \pm z^* \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$$
- The part after $\pm$ is the **margin of error** $E$.
- Common $z^*$: 1.645 for 90%, 1.96 for 95%, 2.576 for 99%.
- **Meaning of 95%:** if you repeated the sampling many times, about 95% of the intervals built this way would contain $\mu$.
- Higher confidence makes the interval wider; a bigger $n$ makes it narrower.
- Sample size for a margin $E$: $n = \left(\frac{z^* \sigma}{E}\right)^2$, rounded **up**.
:::

::: example
A sample of 36 has $\bar{x} = 72$; $\sigma = 12$. For 95%: $E = 1.96 \times 12/\sqrt{36} = 1.96 \times 2 = 3.92$.
Interval: $72 \pm 3.92$, from $68.08$ to $75.92$.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="4.935" tolerance=0.01
$\sigma = 15$, $n = 25$. What is the margin of error for 90% confidence ($z^* = 1.645$)? (3 decimals)
solution: $1.645 \times 15/5 = 1.645 \times 3 = 4.935$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="51.96"
$\bar{x} = 50$, $\sigma = 10$, $n = 100$. What is the **upper** end of the 95% interval?
solution: $E = 1.96 \times 10/10 = 1.96$. Upper end $= 51.96$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="187.12"
$\bar{x} = 200$, $\sigma = 30$, $n = 36$. What is the **lower** end of the 99% interval ($z^* = 2.576$)?
solution: SE $= 30/6 = 5$. $E = 2.576 \times 5 = 12.88$. Lower end $= 187.12$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="4"
A 95% interval for $\mu$ is (48, 56). What is the margin of error?
solution: Half the width: $(56 - 48)/2 = 4$. (The sample mean is the middle, 52.)
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="97"
$\sigma = 10$. How large a sample gives a 95% margin of error of 2?
solution: $n = (1.96 \times 10 / 2)^2 = 9.8^2 = 96.04$; round **up** to 97.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Going from 95% to 99% confidence with the same data makes the interval:
- [x] wider
- [ ] narrower
- [ ] the same width
solution: To be more sure of catching $\mu$, you need a bigger net: $z^*$ goes from 1.96 to 2.576.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
A 95% interval for the mean commute is 24 to 30 minutes. Which statement is right?
- [ ] 95% of commuters take 24 to 30 minutes
- [ ] There's a 95% chance the sample mean is between 24 and 30
- [x] The method catches the true mean in about 95% of samples, and this interval is our estimate of where it is
solution: The interval is about the population **mean**, not individual commutes, and the sample mean is surely inside it (it's the center).
:::
