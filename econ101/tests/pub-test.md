::: problem id=t1 type=choice
A public good is:
- [x] not rival and not excludable
- [ ] rival and excludable
- [ ] rival but not excludable
- [ ] any good the government provides
solution: Governments provide plenty of private goods too; what matters is rivalry and excludability.
:::

::: problem id=t2 type=choice
A lighthouse is often given as a classic public good because:
- [x] every passing ship benefits, and none can be made to pay
- [ ] it's expensive
- [ ] only one ship uses it
- [ ] it's a natural monopoly
solution: Ships can free ride on its light.
:::

::: problem id=t3 type=choice
Free public grazing land gets overgrazed. This is:
- [x] the tragedy of the commons
- [ ] adverse selection
- [ ] a public good working well
- [ ] a price ceiling
solution: A common resource: rival, but no one can be kept off.
:::

::: problem id=t4 type=numeric answer="150"
Three residents value a new park at \$50, \$70 and \$30. What is its total value to them, in dollars?
solution: $50 + 70 + 30 = \$150$. If it costs less than that, it's worth building.
:::

::: problem id=t5 type=choice
Which is moral hazard?
- [x] A company with a government bailout guarantee takes bigger risks
- [ ] Only risky drivers buy car insurance
- [ ] A buyer can't tell good cars from bad
- [ ] A worker earns a degree to impress employers
solution: Protection from losses after the deal changes behaviour.
:::

::: problem id=t6 type=choice
Which is adverse selection?
- [x] Mostly people with bad backs buy back-pain insurance
- [ ] People drive faster after buying car insurance
- [ ] Workers slack off when unsupervised
- [ ] Insured people skip check-ups
solution: The riskiest buyers are the most eager to buy, before any deal is made.
:::

::: problem id=t7 type=numeric answer="6000"
60% of used cars are good (worth \$8,000) and 40% are lemons (worth \$3,000). A buyer who can't tell them apart pays the average value. How much, in dollars?
solution: $0.6 \times 8000 + 0.4 \times 3000 = 4800 + 1200 = \$6{,}000$.
:::
