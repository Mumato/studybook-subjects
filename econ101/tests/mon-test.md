::: problem id=t1 type=numeric answer="15"
Demand is $P = 80 - 2Q$ and $MC = 20$. What quantity maximizes the monopoly's profit?
solution: $MR = 80 - 4Q = 20$, so $Q = 15$.
:::

::: problem id=t2 type=numeric answer="50"
Demand $P = 80 - 2Q$, $MC = 20$, best quantity 15. What price does the monopoly charge?
solution: $P = 80 - 30 = 50$.
:::

::: problem id=t3 type=numeric answer="450"
Same monopoly: 15 units at \$50, each costing \$20, no fixed costs. What is its profit?
solution: $(50 - 20) \times 15 = 450$.
:::

::: problem id=t4 type=numeric answer="225"
Same market (demand $P = 80 - 2Q$, $MC = 20$, monopoly 15 units at \$50). What is the deadweight loss?
solution: Competitive $Q$: $80 - 2Q = 20$ gives 30. Deadweight loss $= \frac{1}{2} \times (30 - 15) \times (50 - 20) = 225$.
:::

::: problem id=t5 type=choice
One electricity grid serves a whole city because a second grid would double the cost. This is a:
- [ ] legal monopoly
- [x] natural monopoly
- [ ] cartel
- [ ] perfectly competitive market
solution: Economies of scale make one supplier cheapest.
:::

::: problem id=t6 type=choice
For a monopoly, marginal revenue is:
- [ ] equal to the price
- [x] less than the price
- [ ] more than the price
- [ ] always zero
solution: Selling more means lowering the price on every unit.
:::

::: problem id=t7 type=choice
An airline charges more for tickets bought the day before than three months ahead. This is:
- [x] price discrimination
- [ ] perfect competition
- [ ] a price ceiling
- [ ] a natural monopoly
solution: Late buyers (often business travellers) are willing to pay more, so they're charged more.
:::
