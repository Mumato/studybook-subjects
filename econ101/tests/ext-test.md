::: problem id=t1 type=numeric answer="30"
Demand is $P = 80 - Q$ and private supply is $P = 20 + Q$. How much does the market produce?
solution: $80 - Q = 20 + Q$, so $Q = 30$.
:::

::: problem id=t2 type=numeric answer="25"
Same market (demand $P = 80 - Q$, private supply $P = 20 + Q$); each unit causes \$10 of damage. What quantity is best for society?
solution: $80 - Q = 30 + Q$, so $Q = 25$.
:::

::: problem id=t3 type=numeric answer="10"
In that market, what per-unit tax would bring the market to the best quantity?
solution: The external cost per unit: \$10.
:::

::: problem id=t4 type=choice
Goods with positive externalities are, without government help:
- [ ] overproduced
- [x] underproduced
- [ ] produced in the right amount
solution: Buyers ignore the benefit to others.
:::

::: problem id=t5 type=choice
Under cap and trade, pollution is cut mostly by:
- [x] the firms that can cut it most cheaply
- [ ] the biggest firms
- [ ] every firm equally
- [ ] the government
solution: Cheap cutters sell permits to expensive cutters; the cap is met at the lowest cost.
:::

::: problem id=t6 type=choice
Which is a **negative** externality?
- [x] Your neighbour's late-night parties
- [ ] A beautiful garden your neighbour plants
- [ ] A vaccine
- [ ] The price of rent rising
solution: The noise costs you sleep, and you weren't part of the decision. (A price change isn't an externality: it works through the market.)
:::

::: problem id=t7 type=numeric answer="130"
A homeowner gets \$100 of benefit from insulating their house, and others get \$30 of benefit from the lower pollution. What is the social benefit, in dollars?
solution: $100 + 30 = \$130$.
:::
