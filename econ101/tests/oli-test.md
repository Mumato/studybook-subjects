::: problem id=t1 type=choice
In the long run, a firm in monopolistic competition earns:
- [ ] large economic profits, protected by its brand
- [x] zero economic profit
- [ ] losses
- [ ] the same profit as a monopoly
solution: Free entry competes away the profit.
:::

::: problem id=t2 type=numeric answer="90"
The five largest firms have shares of 30%, 25%, 20%, 15% and 10%. What is the four-firm concentration ratio? Type the percent.
solution: $30 + 25 + 20 + 15 = 90\%$.
:::

::: problem id=t3 type=numeric answer="5200"
Two firms have shares of 60% and 40%. What is the HHI?
solution: $3600 + 1600 = 5200$.
:::

::: problem id=t4 type=choice
A dominant strategy is a choice that:
- [x] is best for a player whatever the others do
- [ ] gives every player the most
- [ ] the biggest firm makes
- [ ] is chosen by most players
solution: It doesn't depend on the rival's move.
:::

::: problem id=t5 type=numeric answer="3"
Payoffs (A, B): both cheat (3, 3); only A cheats (9, 1); only B cheats (1, 9); both cooperate (6, 6). What does each player earn in the Nash equilibrium?
solution: Cheating is dominant for each (9 > 6 and 3 > 1), so both cheat and earn 3.
:::

::: problem id=t6 type=choice
Cartels are unstable because:
- [x] each member can earn more by cheating on the agreement
- [ ] they make too little profit
- [ ] buyers organize against them
- [ ] their costs are higher
solution: The cartel price is above each firm's marginal cost, so selling extra pays, as long as the others don't.
:::

::: problem id=t7 type=numeric answer="18"
A firm in monopolistic competition faces demand $P = 30 - Q$ and $MC = 6$. What price does it charge?
solution: $MR = 30 - 2Q = 6$ gives $Q = 12$. $P = 30 - 12 = 18$.
:::
