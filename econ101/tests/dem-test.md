::: problem id=t1 type=numeric answer="20"
$Q_d = 90 - 3P$ and $Q_s = 2P - 10$. What is the equilibrium price?
solution: $90 - 3P = 2P - 10$, so $100 = 5P$ and $P = 20$.
:::

::: problem id=t2 type=numeric answer="30"
$Q_d = 90 - 3P$ and $Q_s = 2P - 10$. What is the equilibrium quantity?
solution: $P = 20$, so $Q = 90 - 60 = 30$.
:::

::: problem id=t3 type=numeric answer="20"
$Q_d = 90 - 3P$ and $Q_s = 2P - 10$. At $P = 24$, how many units of surplus are there?
solution: $Q_d = 18$, $Q_s = 38$. Surplus $= 20$.
:::

::: problem id=t4 type=choice
A change in a good's own price causes:
- [x] a movement along its demand curve
- [ ] a shift of its demand curve
- [ ] a shift of its supply curve
- [ ] a change in demand
solution: Only other factors shift the curve. A price change changes the quantity demanded.
:::

::: problem id=t5 type=choice
A new technology lowers the cost of making phones. In the phone market:
- [ ] price and quantity both rise
- [x] price falls, quantity rises
- [ ] price rises, quantity falls
- [ ] price and quantity both fall
solution: Supply shifts right.
:::

::: problem id=t6 type=choice
Hot dogs and buns are complements. The price of hot dogs rises. The demand for buns:
- [ ] shifts right
- [x] shifts left
- [ ] doesn't move
solution: Fewer hot dogs are bought, so fewer buns are needed at every price.
:::

::: problem id=t7 type=choice
Incomes rise. The demand for an inferior good:
- [ ] shifts right
- [x] shifts left
- [ ] doesn't move
solution: That's what "inferior" means: people buy less of it as they get richer.
:::

::: problem id=t8 type=choice
Supply and demand both shift right. Then:
- [x] quantity rises; the price could go either way
- [ ] price rises; the quantity could go either way
- [ ] price and quantity both rise
- [ ] price falls and quantity rises
solution: Both shifts raise quantity. Demand pushes the price up, supply pushes it down.
:::
