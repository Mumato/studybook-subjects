::: problem id=t1 type=numeric answer="1.8" tolerance=0.01
The price rises from \$8 to \$10 and the quantity demanded falls from 60 to 40. Using the midpoint method, what is $E_d$?
solution: $\%\Delta Q = 20/50 = 40\%$, $\%\Delta P = 2/9 = 22.2\%$. $E_d = 40/22.2 = 1.8$.
:::

::: problem id=t2 type=choice
Demand is elastic. If a firm raises its price, its total revenue:
- [ ] rises
- [x] falls
- [ ] stays the same
solution: The quantity falls by a bigger percent than the price rises.
:::

::: problem id=t3 type=numeric answer="8" tolerance=0.01
$E_d = 2$. The price falls 4%. By what percent does the quantity demanded rise?
solution: $2 \times 4\% = 8\%$.
:::

::: problem id=t4 type=numeric answer="2.5" tolerance=0.01
Incomes rise 10% and spending on cruises rises 25%. What is the income elasticity?
solution: $25/10 = 2.5$: a luxury.
:::

::: problem id=t5 type=choice
Two goods have a cross-price elasticity of $-0.8$. They are:
- [ ] substitutes
- [x] complements
- [ ] luxuries
- [ ] inferior goods
solution: Negative cross-price elasticity means complements.
:::

::: problem id=t6 type=choice
Which elasticity of demand is **inelastic**?
- [x] 0.6
- [ ] 1.0
- [ ] 1.5
- [ ] 3.0
solution: Less than 1.
:::

::: problem id=t7 type=numeric answer="10"
Demand is $Q = 60 - 3P$. At what price is total revenue largest?
solution: The curve meets the price axis at $P = 20$; the midpoint is $P = 10$.
:::

::: problem id=t8 type=choice
A tax falls mostly on:
- [ ] the sellers, always
- [ ] the buyers, always
- [x] whichever side is less elastic
- [ ] whoever sends the money to the government
solution: The side that can least easily walk away bears more of it.
:::
