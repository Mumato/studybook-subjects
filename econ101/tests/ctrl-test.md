::: problem id=t1 type=numeric answer="16"
$Q_d = 80 - 4P$ and $Q_s = 4P$ (equilibrium price 10). A price ceiling of 8 is set. How big is the shortage?
solution: $Q_d = 48$, $Q_s = 32$. Shortage $= 16$.
:::

::: problem id=t2 type=numeric answer="24"
$Q_d = 80 - 4P$ and $Q_s = 4P$. A price floor of 13 is set. How big is the surplus?
solution: $Q_d = 28$, $Q_s = 52$. Surplus $= 24$.
:::

::: problem id=t3 type=choice
A price ceiling set **above** the equilibrium price:
- [x] has no effect
- [ ] causes a shortage
- [ ] causes a surplus
- [ ] raises the market price
solution: The market price is already below the ceiling, so it's legal and nothing changes.
:::

::: problem id=t4 type=numeric answer="112.5"
Demand is $P = 30 - Q$ and supply is $P = Q$. What is the consumer surplus at equilibrium?
solution: Equilibrium $Q = 15$, $P = 15$. Consumer surplus $= \frac{1}{2} \times 15 \times 15 = 112.5$.
:::

::: problem id=t5 type=numeric answer="225"
Demand $P = 30 - Q$, supply $P = Q$. What is the total surplus (consumer + producer) at equilibrium?
solution: Producer surplus is also $\frac{1}{2} \times 15 \times 15 = 112.5$. Total $= 225$.
:::

::: problem id=t6 type=choice
A minimum wage above the equilibrium wage tends to cause:
- [x] more people looking for work than there are jobs
- [ ] more jobs than people looking for work
- [ ] no change in employment
- [ ] lower wages
solution: A binding floor in the labour market creates a surplus of labour: unemployment.
:::

::: problem id=t7 type=numeric answer="40"
You sell your old bike for \$120. You would have accepted anything from \$80 up. What is your producer surplus, in dollars?
solution: $120 - 80 = 40$.
:::
