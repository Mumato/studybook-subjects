title: Supply
---
Supply describes how much sellers want to sell at each price.

::: rule
**Law of supply:** when a good's price rises, the **quantity supplied** rises (other things equal). The supply curve slopes up.
- A change in the good's **own price** moves you **along** the curve.
- Anything else **shifts** it: input prices, technology, the number of sellers, expectations, taxes and subsidies, and the prices of other goods the sellers could make instead.
- Lower costs shift supply **right** (more at every price); higher costs shift it **left**.
:::

::: example
Supply is $Q_s = 10P - 20$. At $P = 5$: $Q_s = 50 - 20 = 30$.
If flour gets more expensive, bakeries make less bread at every price: bread supply shifts **left**.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="60"
Supply is $Q_s = 10P - 20$. What is the quantity supplied at $P = 8$?
solution: $10 \times 8 - 20 = 60$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="12"
Supply is $Q_s = 4P$. At what price is the quantity supplied 48?
solution: $4P = 48$, so $P = 12$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
The price of flour rises. The supply curve of bread:
- [x] shifts left
- [ ] shifts right
- [ ] doesn't move; we move along it
solution: Bread costs more to make, so less is offered at every price.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
A new technology makes solar panels cheaper to build. The supply of solar panels:
- [x] shifts right
- [ ] shifts left
- [ ] doesn't change
solution: Lower costs mean more is offered at every price.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
The price of bread rises. In the bread market:
- [x] the quantity supplied rises: a move along the supply curve
- [ ] the supply curve shifts right
- [ ] the supply curve shifts left
solution: A change in the good's own price moves along the curve.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Farmers can grow corn or soybeans on the same land. The price of soybeans rises. The supply of corn:
- [x] shifts left
- [ ] shifts right
- [ ] doesn't change
solution: Soybeans now pay better, so farmers move land out of corn.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
The government pays producers a subsidy for every unit they make. Supply:
- [x] shifts right
- [ ] shifts left
- [ ] doesn't change
solution: A subsidy works like a lower cost: more is offered at every price.
:::
