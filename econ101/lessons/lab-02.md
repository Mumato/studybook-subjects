id: lab-02
title: Wages in labour markets
---
The wage is the price of labour, set by supply and demand, unless something stands in the way.

::: rule
- The market wage is where labour supply equals labour demand.
- A **minimum wage** above that is a price floor: more people want work than there are jobs (**unemployment**).
- **Unions** bargain for higher wages; a **monopsony** (the only employer in town) pays less and hires fewer than a competitive market would.
- Wages also differ because of **human capital** (skills and education raise productivity) and **compensating differentials** (dangerous or unpleasant jobs must pay more).
:::

::: example
Labour demand $L_d = 1000 - 20W$ and supply $L_s = 30W - 250$. $1000 - 20W = 30W - 250$ gives $W = 25$ and $L = 500$.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="20"
Labour demand is $L_d = 600 - 10W$ and supply is $L_s = 20W$. What is the equilibrium wage?
solution: $600 - 10W = 20W$, so $W = 20$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="400"
Same market ($L_d = 600 - 10W$, $L_s = 20W$). How many workers are employed?
solution: $20 \times 20 = 400$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="150"
Same market. A minimum wage of \$25 is set. How many people want work but can't find it?
solution: $L_d = 600 - 250 = 350$ and $L_s = 500$. Unemployment $= 150$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="50"
With that \$25 minimum wage, how many fewer people are employed than at the equilibrium of 400?
solution: $400 - 350 = 50$.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A town's only big employer is a mine (a monopsony). Compared with a competitive labour market, wages are:
- [x] lower, and fewer people are hired
- [ ] higher, and more people are hired
- [ ] the same
- [ ] lower, but more people are hired
solution: To hire more, the mine must raise everyone's wage, so it hires fewer at a lower wage.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Deep-sea fishers earn more than office clerks with similar skills. The best explanation is:
- [x] a compensating differential for danger and hard conditions
- [ ] a minimum wage
- [ ] monopsony
- [ ] adverse selection
solution: Unpleasant or risky jobs must pay more to attract workers.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
**Human capital** is:
- [ ] machines and buildings
- [x] the skills and knowledge that make a worker more productive
- [ ] the money a worker saves
- [ ] the number of workers in a country
solution: Education and training are investments in human capital, which is why they tend to raise wages.
:::
