id: pc-01
title: Price takers and the best output
---
In a perfectly competitive market no single firm can change the price. All it chooses is how much to make. (OpenStax ch. 8)

::: rule
**Perfect competition:** many small firms, identical products, easy entry and exit. Each firm is a **price taker**: it can sell all it wants at the market price $P$, so every extra unit brings in $MR = P$.
- Profit is largest at the output where $P = MC$: keep making units while each one brings in more than it costs.
- Profit $= (P - ATC) \times Q$.
:::

::: example
The price is \$10. The 6th unit costs \$8 to make, the 7th \$10 and the 8th \$13. Make 7: the 8th would cost \$3 more than it brings in.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="12"
A wheat farm sells at the market price of \$12 a bushel. How much revenue does one more bushel bring in?
solution: For a price taker, $MR = P = 12$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="4"
The price is \$15. The marginal cost of the 1st to 5th units is \$5, \$8, \$11, \$15, \$20. How many units should the firm make?
solution: Units 1 to 4 each cost no more than \$15. The 5th costs \$20 and would lose \$5. Make 4.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Which market is closest to perfect competition?
- [x] Wheat, sold by thousands of farms
- [ ] Smartphones
- [ ] The local electricity company
- [ ] Fast-food restaurants
solution: Wheat is the same whoever grows it, and no farm is big enough to move the price.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="400"
The price is \$20. The firm makes 100 units at an ATC of \$16. What is its profit?
solution: $(20 - 16) \times 100 = \$400$.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="-200"
The price is \$20. The firm makes 100 units at an ATC of \$22. What is its profit? (Negative for a loss.)
solution: $(20 - 22) \times 100 = -\$200$.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="15"
A firm's marginal cost is $MC = 2Q$ and the price is \$30. What output maximizes profit?
solution: $30 = 2Q$, so $Q = 15$.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Why can't a perfectly competitive firm charge more than the market price?
- [x] Buyers would switch to the many other firms selling the same thing
- [ ] The government sets the price
- [ ] Its costs are too high
- [ ] It's not allowed to advertise
solution: Its product is identical to everyone else's, so at a higher price it sells nothing.
:::
