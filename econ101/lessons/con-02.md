id: con-02
title: Getting the most from a budget
---
With a fixed budget, how do you choose what to buy? Compare what each dollar buys you.

::: rule
You get the most utility from a budget when the **marginal utility per dollar** is the same for every good, and you spend all of it:
$$\frac{MU_X}{P_X} = \frac{MU_Y}{P_Y}$$
If $\frac{MU_X}{P_X} > \frac{MU_Y}{P_Y}$, move a dollar from Y to X. When X gets pricier, $MU_X / P_X$ falls and you buy less X: that's why demand slopes down.
:::

::: example
A coffee costs \$3 and gives MU 30: 10 per dollar. A muffin costs \$2 and gives MU 40: 20 per dollar. Spend less on coffee and more on muffins until the two are equal.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="15"
A \$4 sandwich has a marginal utility of 60. What is its marginal utility per dollar?
solution: $60 / 4 = 15$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
For you, $MU_X / P_X = 12$ and $MU_Y / P_Y = 8$. To be better off with the same budget:
- [x] buy more X and less Y
- [ ] buy more Y and less X
- [ ] keep things as they are
- [ ] buy more of both
solution: A dollar moved from Y to X gains 12 and loses 8.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="3" creative=true
Tacos cost \$2; the 1st to 4th give MU 20, 16, 12, 8. Drinks cost \$1; the 1st to 5th give MU 12, 10, 8, 6, 4. With \$10, how many tacos give the most total utility?
hint: Work out MU per dollar for each unit and buy the best ones first, until the \$10 is spent.
solution: Per dollar: tacos 10, 8, 6, 4; drinks 12, 10, 8, 6, 4. Buying the best first: drink 1, taco 1, drink 2, taco 2, drink 3, taco 3, drink 4: that's $3 \times 2 + 4 \times 1 = \$10$. **3 tacos** and 4 drinks, where both last units give 6 per dollar. Total utility $48 + 36 = 84$; any other affordable mix gives less.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="4"
Same tacos (\$2; MU 20, 16, 12, 8) and drinks (\$1; MU 12, 10, 8, 6, 4), \$10 to spend. How many drinks are in the best mix?
solution: 3 tacos use \$6, leaving \$4 for 4 drinks. The last taco and the last drink both give 6 utility per dollar.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="12"
At the best mix, $MU_X = 30$ and $P_X = 5$. Good Y costs \$2. What must $MU_Y$ be?
solution: $30/5 = 6$ per dollar, so $MU_Y = 6 \times 2 = 12$.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
At your best mix, the price of X rises. You will:
- [x] buy less X, because its MU per dollar fell
- [ ] buy more X, because it's worth more
- [ ] buy the same amount
- [ ] stop buying Y
solution: $MU_X / P_X$ drops below $MU_Y / P_Y$, so dollars move away from X.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=numeric answer="30"
$P_X = 3$, $P_Y = 6$ and you're at your best mix with $MU_X = 15$. What is $MU_Y$?
solution: $15/3 = 5$ per dollar, so $MU_Y = 5 \times 6 = 30$.
:::
