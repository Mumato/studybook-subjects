title: How firms decide how many to hire
---
Workers are an input like any other. A firm hires one more if that worker brings in more than they cost. (OpenStax ch. 14)

::: rule
- **Marginal revenue product (MRP)** of a worker = their marginal product × the price of the output: $MRP = MP \times P$.
- A firm hires workers until **MRP = wage**: keep hiring while the next worker's MRP is at least the wage.
- The MRP curve is the firm's **labour demand** curve. It shifts right when the output's price rises or workers become more productive (better tools, more training).
:::

::: example
The 4th worker adds 10 units, each sold for \$15: $MRP = 150$. At a wage of \$120 a day, hire them: they bring in \$30 more than they cost.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="160"
A worker's marginal product is 8 units and each unit sells for \$20. What is their MRP, in dollars?
solution: $8 \times 20 = \$160$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="3"
Output sells for \$15. The 1st to 5th workers' marginal products are 12, 10, 8, 6 and 4. The wage is \$100. How many workers does the firm hire?
solution: MRPs: 180, 150, 120, 90, 60. The first three bring in at least \$100; the 4th brings in only \$90.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="4"
Same firm (MRPs 180, 150, 120, 90, 60). The wage falls to \$80. How many workers does it hire now?
solution: Now the 4th worker's \$90 beats the wage. The 5th (\$60) doesn't.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
The price of the firm's product rises. Its demand for labour:
- [x] shifts right
- [ ] shifts left
- [ ] doesn't change
solution: Every worker's output is now worth more, so MRP rises at every level of hiring.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A profit-maximizing firm hires workers until:
- [x] the last worker's MRP equals the wage
- [ ] output is as large as possible
- [ ] the average product is highest
- [ ] all applicants are hired
solution: Beyond that point, each extra worker costs more than they bring in.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
New software makes each accountant more productive. The demand for accountants:
- [x] shifts right
- [ ] shifts left
- [ ] doesn't change
solution: Higher marginal product means higher MRP.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=numeric answer="60"
The 5th worker's marginal product is 4 and output sells for \$15. What is their MRP, in dollars?
solution: $4 \times 15 = \$60$.
:::
