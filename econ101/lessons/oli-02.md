title: Oligopoly and cartels
---
When a few big firms share a market, each one's best move depends on what the others do.

::: rule
**Oligopoly:** a few large firms, high barriers to entry, and **interdependence**: each firm must guess how rivals will react.
- **Four-firm concentration ratio:** the total market share of the four largest firms.
- **Herfindahl-Hirschman Index (HHI):** the sum of the squared market shares (in percent) of all firms. Higher means more concentrated; a monopoly is $100^2 = 10{,}000$.
- Firms that **collude** (a **cartel**) can act like one monopoly and share its profit, but each member gains by secretly cheating, so cartels tend to break down. Price-fixing is illegal in most countries.
:::

::: example
Four firms with shares 40%, 30%, 20% and 10%: concentration ratio $= 100\%$; $HHI = 1600 + 900 + 400 + 100 = 3000$.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="85"
The largest firms have shares of 35%, 25%, 15%, 10% and 5%; ten small firms share the last 10%. What is the four-firm concentration ratio? Type the percent.
solution: $35 + 25 + 15 + 10 = 85\%$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="3800"
Three firms have shares of 50%, 30% and 20%. What is the HHI?
solution: $2500 + 900 + 400 = 3800$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="1000"
Ten firms each have 10% of the market. What is the HHI?
solution: $10 \times 10^2 = 1000$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Why do cartels tend to break down?
- [x] Each member can gain by secretly producing more or cutting its price
- [ ] Buyers refuse to pay cartel prices
- [ ] Cartel members' costs rise
- [ ] They always make losses
solution: At the cartel's high price, any one firm earns more by selling extra, as long as the others don't.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
What most sets oligopoly apart from other market types?
- [x] Each firm's best choice depends on what its few rivals do
- [ ] Products are identical
- [ ] There's only one seller
- [ ] Entry is free
solution: With few firms, every move gets a reaction: firms play a strategic game.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
A group of oil-exporting countries agreeing on output to raise the price is a:
- [x] cartel
- [ ] natural monopoly
- [ ] perfectly competitive market
- [ ] monopsony
solution: That's collusion: OPEC is the classic example.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=numeric answer="100"
Two firms in a cartel each earn \$400. If one cheats while the other sticks to the deal, the cheater earns \$500 and the other \$200. How many dollars does the cheater gain by cheating?
solution: $500 - 400 = \$100$.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=numeric answer="800" creative=true
Two identical firms face market demand $P = 100 - Q$, and every unit costs \$20 to make. If they form a cartel, act like one monopoly and split output equally, what is each firm's profit?
hint: For the monopoly, $MR = 100 - 2Q$.
solution: $MR = 100 - 2Q = 20$ gives $Q = 40$ and $P = 60$. Total profit $(60 - 20) \times 40 = 1600$, so each earns 800.
:::
