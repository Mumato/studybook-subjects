id: con-01
title: Total and marginal utility
---
Economists call the satisfaction you get from something **utility**. What drives your choices is not the total, but what the next unit adds. (OpenStax ch. 6)

::: rule
- **Total utility (TU):** the satisfaction from everything you consume.
- **Marginal utility (MU):** the extra utility from **one more unit**: $MU = TU_{\text{new}} - TU_{\text{old}}$.
- **Diminishing marginal utility:** each extra unit usually adds less than the one before. TU keeps rising while MU is positive and falls once MU turns negative.
:::

::: example
Pizza slices, total utility: 1 slice 20, 2 slices 36, 3 slices 48, 4 slices 56, 5 slices 60.
Marginal utilities: 20, 16, 12, 8, 4. Each slice adds less.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="12"
Total utility from pizza slices: 1 slice 20, 2 slices 36, 3 slices 48, 4 slices 56. What is the marginal utility of the 3rd slice?
solution: $48 - 36 = 12$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="4"
Total utility from songs: 1 song 10, 2 songs 18, 3 songs 24, 4 songs 28. What is the marginal utility of the 4th song?
solution: $28 - 24 = 4$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="23"
The marginal utilities of the 1st, 2nd and 3rd cookie are 10, 8 and 5. What is the total utility of 3 cookies?
solution: $10 + 8 + 5 = 23$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Diminishing marginal utility means:
- [x] each extra unit adds less satisfaction than the one before
- [ ] total utility falls as you consume more
- [ ] you stop wanting the good
- [ ] the price falls as you buy more
solution: MU falls, but TU still rises as long as MU is positive.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="33"
Four coffees give a total utility of 30, and the 5th has a marginal utility of 3. What is the total utility of 5 coffees?
solution: $30 + 3 = 33$.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
When marginal utility is negative, total utility is:
- [ ] rising
- [x] falling
- [ ] at its lowest
- [ ] zero
solution: The extra unit makes you worse off (one slice of cake too many), so the total drops.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Water is essential to life but cheap; diamonds aren't, but are expensive. Why?
- [x] Water is plentiful, so the marginal utility of one more litre is low
- [ ] Water has low total utility
- [ ] Diamonds have higher total utility than water
- [ ] Prices have nothing to do with utility
solution: Prices reflect what the **next** unit is worth. Water's total utility is huge; its marginal utility is tiny.
:::
