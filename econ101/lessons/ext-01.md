id: ext-01
title: Externalities
---
Markets count the costs and benefits of the buyer and the seller. When others are affected too, the market gets the quantity wrong. (OpenStax ch. 12)

::: rule
An **externality** is a cost or benefit that falls on people outside the trade.
- **Negative** (pollution, noise): social cost = private cost + external cost. The market makes **too much**.
- **Positive** (vaccines, education): social benefit = private benefit + external benefit. The market makes **too little**.
- The best quantity for society is where marginal **social** benefit equals marginal **social** cost.
:::

::: example
Demand $P = 100 - Q$ and private supply (cost) $P = 20 + Q$. Each unit causes \$20 of pollution damage, so social cost is $P = 40 + Q$.
Market: $100 - Q = 20 + Q$ gives $Q = 40$. Best for society: $100 - Q = 40 + Q$ gives $Q = 30$.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="30"
Demand is $P = 60 - Q$ and private supply is $P = Q$. How much does the market produce?
solution: $60 - Q = Q$, so $Q = 30$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="25"
Same market (demand $P = 60 - Q$, private supply $P = Q$), but each unit causes \$10 of damage to others. What quantity is best for society?
solution: Social cost: $P = Q + 10$. $60 - Q = Q + 10$ gives $Q = 25$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="35"
At the quantity best for society (25 units on demand $P = 60 - Q$), what is the price?
solution: $P = 60 - 25 = 35$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
With a negative externality, a free market produces:
- [x] too much
- [ ] too little
- [ ] exactly the right amount
solution: Sellers ignore the cost they put on others, so the good looks cheaper to make than it is.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Which is a **positive** externality?
- [x] Your flu shot makes others less likely to catch the flu
- [ ] A factory's smoke
- [ ] A neighbour's loud music at 3 a.m.
- [ ] Traffic jams
solution: People who didn't pay for your shot benefit from it.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="50"
A flu shot is worth \$30 to you, and protecting others is worth another \$20 to them. What is its social benefit, in dollars?
solution: $30 + 20 = \$50$.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
That flu shot costs \$40. Left to themselves, people like you:
- [x] skip it, though society gains from the shot
- [ ] get it, and society gains
- [ ] skip it, and society agrees
- [ ] get it, though society loses
solution: Your own benefit (\$30) is below the cost (\$40), but the social benefit (\$50) is above it. Positive externalities lead to too little.
:::
