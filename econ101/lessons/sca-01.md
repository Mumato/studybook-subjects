title: Scarcity and opportunity cost
---
Economics is the study of choices under **scarcity**: there is never enough time, money or resources for everything people want. (OpenStax ch. 1-2)

::: rule
The **opportunity cost** of a choice is the value of the **next best alternative** you give up: only the best one, not all of them added up. It counts money paid *and* what your time could have earned.
- Ignore **sunk costs**: money already spent that you can't get back is gone whatever you choose, so it shouldn't change the decision.
- **Microeconomics** studies single people, firms and markets; **macroeconomics** studies the whole economy (unemployment, inflation, growth).
:::

::: example
You skip a \$90 shift to see a concert. The ticket is \$60. The opportunity cost of the concert is $60 + 90 = \$150$.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="48"
You take a free class instead of working a 3-hour shift at \$16 an hour. What is the opportunity cost of the class, in dollars?
solution: The class is free, so you give up only the wage: $3 \times 16 = \$48$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
Tonight you could see a movie (worth \$15 to you), go bowling (worth \$20) or study (worth \$12). You choose the movie. Its opportunity cost is:
- [x] bowling, worth \$20
- [ ] bowling and studying, worth \$32
- [ ] studying, worth \$12
- [ ] nothing, because you wanted the movie
solution: Only the **next best** alternative counts: bowling, worth \$20.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="39000"
A year of college costs \$8,000 tuition, \$1,000 for books and \$10,000 for rent and food (the same as you'd pay if you worked instead). You give up a job paying \$30,000. What is the opportunity cost of the year?
hint: Rent and food are paid either way.
solution: $8000 + 1000 + 30000 = \$39{,}000$. The rent and food aren't caused by the choice, so they don't count.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=text answers="opportunity;opportunity cost"
The value of the next best alternative you give up is called the ____ cost.
solution: The **opportunity** cost.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Which question is **microeconomic**?
- [x] How does a pizza shop choose its prices?
- [ ] Why did the country's unemployment rate rise?
- [ ] What causes inflation?
- [ ] How fast is the economy growing?
solution: A single firm's pricing is micro; the others are about the whole economy.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Which statement is **positive** (a testable claim about what is), not normative (an opinion about what should be)?
- [x] Raising the minimum wage to \$20 would reduce teen employment.
- [ ] The government should raise the minimum wage.
- [ ] Taxes are too high.
- [ ] Rich people ought to pay more tax.
solution: The first can be checked with data, even if it turns out false. The others say what *should* happen.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
You paid \$50 for a non-refundable concert ticket. On the night, a friend invites you to something worth \$60 to you; the concert is worth \$40 to you. What should you do?
- [ ] Go to the concert, because you already paid \$50
- [x] Go with your friend: the \$50 is gone either way
- [ ] Go to the concert, because \$50 + \$40 is more than \$60
- [ ] It makes no difference
solution: The \$50 is a sunk cost. Compare only what's left: \$60 beats \$40.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=numeric answer="20000" creative=true
A farmer can rent her field to a neighbour for \$5,000 a year, or plant corn. Planting costs \$12,000 for seed and fuel and takes 200 hours she could have worked at a job paying \$15 an hour. What is the full opportunity cost of planting corn, in dollars?
hint: Money spent, plus the rent she gives up, plus the wages she gives up.
solution: $12000 + 5000 + 200 \times 15 = 12000 + 5000 + 3000 = \$20{,}000$. If the corn sells for less than that, she's better off renting the field and taking the job.
:::
