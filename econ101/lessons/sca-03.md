id: sca-03
title: The production possibilities frontier
---
A whole economy faces the same trade-off as a single shopper: making more of one thing means making less of another.

::: rule
The **production possibilities frontier (PPF)** shows the most an economy can make of two goods with its resources and technology.
- A point **on** the PPF is efficient; **inside** it wastes resources (e.g. unemployment); **outside** is unattainable for now.
- Moving along it, the opportunity cost of one more unit of X is the units of Y given up divided by the units of X gained.
- It bows outward because of **increasing opportunity cost**: resources aren't equally good at making both goods.
- Better technology or more resources shift it **out**.
:::

::: example
An economy can make (robots, food): A (0, 100), B (10, 90), C (20, 70), D (30, 40), E (40, 0).

From B to C, 10 more robots cost 20 food: 2 food per robot.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="1"
PPF points (robots, food): A (0, 100), B (10, 90), C (20, 70), D (30, 40), E (40, 0). What does each robot cost in food going from A to B?
solution: $(100 - 90) / 10 = 1$ food per robot.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="3"
Same PPF: A (0, 100), B (10, 90), C (20, 70), D (30, 40), E (40, 0). What does each robot cost in food from C to D?
solution: $(70 - 40) / 10 = 3$ food per robot.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="4"
Same PPF. What does each robot cost in food from D to E?
solution: $40 / 10 = 4$ food per robot. The cost keeps rising: increasing opportunity cost.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
On this PPF, C is (20, 70). Making 20 robots and 60 food is:
- [x] inefficient (inside the PPF)
- [ ] efficient (on the PPF)
- [ ] unattainable (outside the PPF)
solution: At 20 robots the economy could make 70 food, so 60 wastes resources.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
On this PPF, D is (30, 40). Making 30 robots and 50 food is:
- [ ] inefficient
- [ ] efficient
- [x] unattainable
solution: At 30 robots the most food is 40, so 50 is outside the PPF.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Why is a PPF usually bowed outward?
- [x] Resources are specialized, so each extra unit of one good costs more of the other
- [ ] People prefer a mix of both goods
- [ ] Prices rise when production rises
- [ ] The economy always has some unemployment
solution: The first robots are made by the workers and land best suited to robots; later ones pull in resources that were much better at food.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Which shifts the PPF **outward**?
- [x] A new technology that makes both goods faster
- [ ] Moving from point A to point B
- [ ] Unemployed workers finding jobs
- [ ] A rise in the price of food
solution: Better technology lets the economy make more of everything. Hiring the unemployed moves a point from inside the PPF onto it; the PPF itself stays put.
:::
