id: cst-01
title: Costs and economic profit
---
An accountant and an economist can look at the same business and disagree about its profit. The economist also counts what the owner gave up. (OpenStax ch. 7)

::: rule
- **Explicit costs:** money actually paid out (wages, rent, materials).
- **Implicit costs:** the opportunity cost of the owner's own resources (the salary they gave up, the interest their savings could earn).
- **Accounting profit** = revenue − explicit costs.
- **Economic profit** = revenue − explicit costs − implicit costs.
Zero economic profit (a **normal profit**) means the owner earns exactly what they'd earn in their best alternative.
:::

::: example
A bakery takes in \$200,000 and pays \$120,000 in costs. The owner could earn \$60,000 at a job.
Accounting profit $= 200000 - 120000 = \$80{,}000$. Economic profit $= 80000 - 60000 = \$20{,}000$.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="60000"
A shop has revenue of \$150,000 and explicit costs of \$90,000. What is its accounting profit?
solution: $150000 - 90000 = \$60{,}000$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="10000"
The shop (revenue \$150,000, explicit costs \$90,000) is run by an owner who gave up a \$50,000 job. What is its economic profit?
solution: $150000 - 90000 - 50000 = \$10{,}000$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="15000"
A firm has revenue of \$300,000 and explicit costs of \$200,000. The owner gave up an \$80,000 salary and put in \$100,000 of savings that could earn 5% a year. What is the economic profit?
solution: Implicit costs: $80000 + 0.05 \times 100000 = 85000$. Economic profit: $300000 - 200000 - 85000 = \$15{,}000$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
A firm earning zero economic profit:
- [ ] is losing money and should close
- [x] earns exactly what its owners could earn in their best alternative
- [ ] has no revenue
- [ ] has zero accounting profit
solution: All costs, including opportunity costs, are covered. Accounting profit is positive.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Which is an **implicit** cost?
- [ ] The rent the firm pays for its office
- [ ] Wages paid to workers
- [x] The salary the owner gave up to run the firm
- [ ] The electricity bill
solution: No money changes hands, but the owner gives it up.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="-10000"
Revenue is \$100,000, explicit costs \$70,000 and implicit costs \$40,000. What is the economic profit?
solution: $100000 - 70000 - 40000 = -\$10{,}000$.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
A firm has a positive accounting profit but a negative economic profit. Its owner:
- [x] would be better off in their best alternative
- [ ] is doing better than in any alternative
- [ ] is breaking even in every sense
- [ ] must have made an accounting error
solution: The business pays less than what the owner gives up to run it.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=numeric answer="222000" creative=true
Maya earns \$70,000 at her job. She could open a café instead: rent and supplies would cost \$150,000 a year, and she'd put in \$40,000 of savings that now earn 5%. How much yearly revenue does the café need just to match staying at her job (zero economic profit), in dollars?
solution: All costs: $150000 + 70000 + 0.05 \times 40000$, which is $150000 + 70000 + 2000 = \$222{,}000$.
:::
