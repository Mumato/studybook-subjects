title: Income and substitution effects
---
When a price changes, two things happen at once: the good's price changes compared with others, and your budget stretches further or less far.

::: rule
When a good's price **falls**:
- **Substitution effect:** it's cheaper compared with other goods, so you buy **more** of it. Always.
- **Income effect:** your money buys more, like a raise. You buy **more** of a **normal** good and **less** of an **inferior** good.
For normal goods both effects point the same way, so demand slopes down. A price change is like an income change of (the quantity you were buying) × (the price change).
:::

::: example
You buy 10 coffees a week at \$4. The price falls to \$3. Buying the same 10 now costs \$10 less: it's like a \$10 raise (income effect), and coffee is now cheaper next to tea (substitution effect).
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="10"
You buy 20 litres of gas a week at \$2.00. The price falls to \$1.50. How many dollars a week does that free up, if you buy the same amount?
solution: $20 \times (2.00 - 1.50) = \$10$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
When a good's price falls, the substitution effect makes you buy:
- [x] more of it, always
- [ ] less of it, always
- [ ] more if it's normal, less if it's inferior
solution: The good is cheaper compared with everything else.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A normal good's price falls. Its income effect makes you buy:
- [x] more
- [ ] less
- [ ] the same
solution: You're effectively richer, and you buy more normal goods when richer.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
An inferior good's price falls. Its income effect makes you buy:
- [ ] more
- [x] less
- [ ] the same
solution: You're effectively richer, and you buy less of an inferior good when richer. (The substitution effect still pushes the other way.)
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Movie tickets get more expensive, so you see fewer movies and stream more at home. That switch is mainly the:
- [x] substitution effect
- [ ] income effect
- [ ] law of supply
solution: You're replacing a good that got relatively dearer with one that's now relatively cheaper.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="15"
You buy 15 burgers a month at \$4. The price rises to \$5. How many more dollars a month would the same 15 burgers cost?
solution: $15 \times (5 - 4) = \$15$: like a \$15 pay cut.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Why does the demand curve for a normal good slope down?
- [x] When its price rises, both the substitution and income effects make you buy less
- [ ] Sellers charge less when they sell more
- [ ] Marginal utility rises as you buy more
- [ ] Incomes fall when prices rise
solution: Both effects point the same way, so a higher price always means a lower quantity demanded.
:::
