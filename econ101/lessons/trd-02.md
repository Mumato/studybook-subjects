title: Gains from trade
---
Specializing makes the total bigger. Trading at the right price splits that bigger total so both sides end up with more.

::: rule
- When each side specializes in its comparative advantage, **total output rises**.
- Trade helps both sides when the **terms of trade** (the price, in one good per unit of the other) lie **between** their two opportunity costs.
- Within a country, trade has winners and losers: cheaper imports help consumers and hurt the domestic producers who compete with them.
:::

::: example
From the last lesson, a cake costs Ana 2 shirts and Ben 1 shirt. At 1.5 shirts per cake, Ana buys cakes for less than the 2 shirts they'd cost her, and Ben sells cakes for more than the 1 shirt they cost him. Both gain.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
Ada catches 12 fish or 4 coconuts a day; Bo 6 fish or 6 coconuts. At which price (fish per coconut) can both gain from trade?
- [ ] 0.5
- [x] 2
- [ ] 4
- [ ] 5
solution: A coconut costs Ada 3 fish and Bo 1 fish. The price must be between 1 and 3.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="9"
Same islanders. Without trade, each spends half the day on each good: Ada gets 6 fish and 2 coconuts, Bo 3 fish and 3 coconuts. How many fish do they have in total?
solution: $6 + 3 = 9$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="5"
Same no-trade split. How many coconuts in total?
solution: $2 + 3 = 5$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="12"
Same islanders. Now Ada only fishes and Bo only gathers coconuts. How many fish in total?
solution: Ada catches 12.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="6"
Same: if Ada only fishes and Bo only gathers coconuts, how many coconuts in total?
solution: Bo gathers 6. Together that's 3 more fish and 1 more coconut than without specializing.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="3"
Ada and Bo trade at 2 fish per coconut. If Ada gives 6 fish, how many coconuts does she get?
solution: $6 / 2 = 3$. Ada ends with 6 fish and 3 coconuts, more than the 6 and 2 she had alone.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Canada starts importing cheaper steel. Who loses?
- [ ] Canadian car makers, who buy steel
- [x] Canadian steel producers and their workers
- [ ] Canadian consumers
- [ ] The foreign steel makers
solution: Domestic producers now compete with cheaper imports. Steel buyers gain.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=numeric answer="2" creative=true
Ada and Bo specialize and trade at 2 fish per coconut. Bo wants to end up with 4 coconuts. How many **more** fish does he end with than if he'd made 4 coconuts and some fish on his own?
hint: Alone, each coconut Bo makes costs him 1 fish out of his 6.
solution: Trading: Bo makes 6 coconuts, keeps 4 and sells 2 for 4 fish. Alone: 4 coconuts leave time for $6 - 4 = 2$ fish. He gains $4 - 2 = 2$ fish.
:::
