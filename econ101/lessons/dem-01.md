title: Demand
---
Demand describes how much buyers want to buy at each price. (OpenStax ch. 3)

::: rule
**Law of demand:** when a good's price rises, the **quantity demanded** falls (other things equal). The demand curve slopes down.
- A change in the good's **own price** moves you **along** the curve.
- Anything else **shifts** the whole curve: buyers' income, prices of **substitutes** (used instead) and **complements** (used together), tastes, the number of buyers, expectations.
- With more income, demand for a **normal** good rises; for an **inferior** good it falls.
:::

::: example
Demand is $Q_d = 100 - 5P$. At $P = 4$: $Q_d = 100 - 20 = 80$.
If tea (a substitute) gets pricier, more people switch to coffee: coffee's demand curve shifts **right**.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="50"
Demand is $Q_d = 100 - 5P$. What is the quantity demanded at $P = 10$?
solution: $100 - 5 \times 10 = 50$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="14"
Demand is $Q_d = 100 - 5P$. At what price is the quantity demanded 30?
solution: $30 = 100 - 5P$, so $5P = 70$ and $P = 14$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
The price of coffee rises. In the coffee market:
- [x] the quantity demanded falls: a move along the demand curve
- [ ] the demand curve shifts left
- [ ] the demand curve shifts right
- [ ] nothing changes
solution: A change in the good's own price never shifts its demand curve.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
The price of tea, a substitute for coffee, rises. The demand curve for coffee:
- [x] shifts right
- [ ] shifts left
- [ ] doesn't move; we move along it
solution: Some tea drinkers switch to coffee, so more coffee is wanted at every price.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Printers get cheaper. The demand curve for ink cartridges (a complement):
- [x] shifts right
- [ ] shifts left
- [ ] doesn't move
solution: More people buy printers, and every printer needs ink.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=text answers="inferior;an inferior;inferior good;an inferior good"
People's incomes rise and they take fewer bus rides. Bus rides are a(n) ____ good.
solution: Demand falls as income rises: an **inferior** good.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=point answer="4,6"
Here is the demand curve $P = 10 - Q$ (quantity across, price up). Click its point at a price of 6.
graph:
x 0..10
y 0..10
plot 10-x blue
label 8.5,2.5 D
solution: $6 = 10 - Q$ gives $Q = 4$: the point $(4, 6)$.
:::
