id: ela-03
title: Other elasticities and who pays a tax
---
The same "percent over percent" idea measures how buyers react to income and to other goods' prices, and how sellers react to price.

::: rule
- **Income elasticity** = %ΔQ ÷ %Δincome. Positive: **normal** good (above 1: a **luxury**). Negative: **inferior** good.
- **Cross-price elasticity** = %ΔQ of good X ÷ %ΔP of good Y. Positive: **substitutes**. Negative: **complements**.
- **Price elasticity of supply** = %ΔQ supplied ÷ %ΔP.
- **Tax incidence:** whoever is **less elastic** (less able to walk away) pays more of a tax, no matter who sends it to the government.
:::

::: example
Incomes rise 10% and restaurant meals rise 15%: income elasticity $= 15/10 = 1.5$. A normal good, and a luxury.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="-0.4" tolerance=0.01
Incomes rise 5% and bus rides fall 2%. What is the income elasticity of bus rides?
solution: $-2 / 5 = -0.4$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
A good with an income elasticity of $-0.4$ is:
- [ ] a luxury
- [ ] a normal necessity
- [x] an inferior good
solution: Negative income elasticity: people buy less of it as they get richer.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="0.6" tolerance=0.01
Butter's price rises 10% and sales of margarine rise 6%. What is the cross-price elasticity?
solution: $6 / 10 = 0.6$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
A positive cross-price elasticity means the goods are:
- [x] substitutes
- [ ] complements
- [ ] unrelated
solution: When one gets pricier, people buy more of the other.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
The cross-price elasticity between game consoles and video games is $-1.2$. They are:
- [ ] substitutes
- [x] complements
- [ ] inferior goods
solution: Negative: dearer consoles mean fewer games sold. They're used together.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="1.5" tolerance=0.01
The price of strawberries rises 20% and farmers offer 30% more. What is the price elasticity of supply?
solution: $30 / 20 = 1.5$.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Demand for cigarettes is very inelastic and supply is elastic. A tax on cigarettes is paid mostly by:
- [x] smokers, through higher prices
- [ ] cigarette companies, through lower profits
- [ ] whoever sends the tax to the government
- [ ] it's always split 50-50
solution: Smokers can't easily walk away, so the price they pay rises by most of the tax.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=numeric answer="0.2" tolerance=0.001
A \$1 tax is put on each movie ticket. The price buyers pay rises from \$5.00 to \$5.80. How many dollars of the tax do sellers bear per ticket?
solution: Buyers pay \$0.80 more; the other $1 - 0.80 = \$0.20$ comes out of what sellers keep.
:::
