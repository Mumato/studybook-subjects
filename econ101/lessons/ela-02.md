id: ela-02
title: Elasticity and total revenue
---
Should a business raise its price? It depends on elasticity: the quantity lost might matter more, or less, than the higher price.

::: rule
**Total revenue** is $TR = P \times Q$.
- **Elastic** demand: raising the price **lowers** TR; cutting it raises TR.
- **Inelastic** demand: raising the price **raises** TR; cutting it lowers TR.
- **Unit elastic**: TR doesn't change.
On a straight-line demand curve, TR is largest at the **midpoint**: halfway down from the top price.
:::

::: example
The price rises from \$10 to \$12 and the quantity falls from 100 to 80: TR goes from \$1,000 to \$960. It fell, so demand is elastic here (we found 1.22).
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="400"
A store sells 50 shirts at \$8. What is its total revenue?
solution: $8 \times 50 = 400$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
Demand for a firm's product has $E_d = 0.3$. If it raises its price, total revenue:
- [x] rises
- [ ] falls
- [ ] stays the same
solution: Inelastic: the quantity falls by a smaller percent than the price rises.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="40"
A café cuts its price from \$5 to \$4, and sales rise from 200 to 260. By how much does its total revenue change, in dollars?
solution: $4 \times 260 - 5 \times 200 = 1040 - 1000 = 40$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
In the café example, between \$4 and \$5 demand is:
- [x] elastic
- [ ] inelastic
- [ ] unit elastic
solution: A price cut raised total revenue, so the quantity rose by a bigger percent than the price fell.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="25"
Demand is $Q = 100 - 2P$. At what price is total revenue largest?
hint: The curve meets the price axis at $P = 50$.
solution: The midpoint is halfway between 0 and 50: $P = 25$.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="1250"
Demand is $Q = 100 - 2P$. What is the largest possible total revenue?
solution: At $P = 25$, $Q = 50$: $TR = 25 \times 50 = 1250$.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Demand for wheat is inelastic. A record harvest (supply shifts right) makes farmers' total revenue:
- [x] fall
- [ ] rise
- [ ] stay the same
solution: The price falls by a bigger percent than the quantity rises. A great harvest can be bad news for farmers.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=numeric answer="31350" creative=true
A bus company sells 10,000 rides a week at \$3. Demand has $E_d = 0.5$. It raises the fare by 10%. Using simple percentages, what is its new weekly revenue, in dollars?
hint: The quantity falls by $0.5 \times 10\% = 5\%$.
solution: Rides: $10000 \times 0.95 = 9500$. Fare: $3 \times 1.10 = 3.30$. Revenue: $9500 \times 3.30 = 31{,}350$, up from \$30,000, as expected with inelastic demand.
:::
