id: ctrl-02
title: Consumer surplus, producer surplus and deadweight loss
---
How much do buyers and sellers gain from a market, and how much does a price control throw away?

::: rule
- **Consumer surplus** = what buyers would have paid minus what they pay: the area **below demand, above the price**.
- **Producer surplus** = the price minus what sellers needed to get: the area **above supply, below the price**.
- With straight-line curves these are triangles: $\frac{1}{2} \times \text{base} \times \text{height}$.
- Total surplus is largest at the equilibrium. A binding control blocks trades that would have helped both sides; the surplus lost is the **deadweight loss**.
:::

::: example
Demand $P = 10 - Q$, supply $P = Q$: equilibrium $Q = 5$, $P = 5$.
Consumer surplus $= \frac{1}{2} \times 5 \times (10 - 5) = 12.5$. Producer surplus $= \frac{1}{2} \times 5 \times 5 = 12.5$.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="10"
Demand is $P = 20 - Q$ and supply is $P = 5 + Q/2$. What is the equilibrium quantity?
solution: $20 - Q = 5 + Q/2$, so $15 = 1.5Q$ and $Q = 10$ (price 10).
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="50"
Demand $P = 20 - Q$, supply $P = 5 + Q/2$; equilibrium $Q = 10$, $P = 10$. What is the consumer surplus?
solution: $\frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times (20 - 10) = 50$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="25"
Same market (equilibrium $Q = 10$, $P = 10$; supply starts at $P = 5$). What is the producer surplus?
solution: $\frac{1}{2} \times 10 \times (10 - 5) = 25$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="15"
You'd pay up to \$40 for a concert ticket and buy one for \$25. What is your consumer surplus, in dollars?
solution: $40 - 25 = 15$.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="12"
Demand $P = 20 - Q$, supply $P = 5 + Q/2$ (equilibrium 10 units at 10). A price ceiling of 8 is set. What is the deadweight loss?
hint: At 8, sellers offer only 6 units. The lost trades are units 6 to 10.
solution: $8 = 5 + Q/2$ gives $Q_s = 6$. At $Q = 6$, buyers value a unit at $20 - 6 = 14$ and it costs sellers 8. Deadweight loss $= \frac{1}{2} \times (10 - 6) \times (14 - 8) = 12$.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Deadweight loss is:
- [ ] money the government collects
- [ ] surplus moved from sellers to buyers
- [x] surplus nobody gets, because trades that would help both sides don't happen
- [ ] the cost of making the good
solution: It's lost, not moved: no one receives it.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=numeric answer="54" creative=true
Same market and the ceiling of 8: 6 units are sold, and they go to the buyers who value them most. What is the consumer surplus now?
hint: For units 0 to 6, buyers value them from 20 down to 14 and pay 8. The area is a trapezoid.
solution: The gap above the price runs from $20 - 8 = 12$ at the first unit to $14 - 8 = 6$ at the sixth. Area $= 6 \times \frac{12 + 6}{2} = 54$. Buyers gain (54 vs 50), sellers lose ($\frac{1}{2} \times 6 \times 3 = 9$ vs 25), and 12 is lost altogether.
:::
