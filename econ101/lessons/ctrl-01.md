title: Price ceilings and price floors
---
Sometimes the government sets the price by law instead of letting the market find it. (OpenStax ch. 3.4)

::: rule
- A **price ceiling** is a legal **maximum** price (e.g. rent control). It matters only if it's **below** equilibrium, and then it causes a **shortage**: $Q_d - Q_s$ at the ceiling.
- A **price floor** is a legal **minimum** price (e.g. a minimum wage). It matters only if it's **above** equilibrium, and then it causes a **surplus**: $Q_s - Q_d$ at the floor.
- The amount actually traded is the **smaller** of $Q_d$ and $Q_s$.
:::

::: example
$Q_d = 100 - 5P$ and $Q_s = 10P - 20$; the equilibrium price is 8. With a ceiling of 6: $Q_d = 70$ and $Q_s = 40$, a shortage of 30. Only 40 units are sold.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="25"
$Q_d = 60 - 2P$ and $Q_s = 3P - 15$ (equilibrium price 15). A price ceiling is set at 10. How big is the shortage?
solution: $Q_d = 40$, $Q_s = 15$. Shortage $= 25$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="15"
$Q_d = 60 - 2P$ and $Q_s = 3P - 15$. A price floor is set at 18. How big is the surplus?
solution: $Q_d = 24$, $Q_s = 39$. Surplus $= 15$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="0"
$Q_d = 60 - 2P$ and $Q_s = 3P - 15$ (equilibrium price 15). A price ceiling is set at 18. How big is the shortage?
solution: The ceiling is above the equilibrium price, so the market price of 15 is legal: no shortage, 0.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
A city caps rents below the market rent. Most likely:
- [x] more people want apartments than are offered: a shortage
- [ ] more apartments are offered than wanted: a surplus
- [ ] nothing changes
- [ ] landlords build more apartments
solution: A binding ceiling raises the quantity demanded and lowers the quantity supplied.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
The minimum wage is a:
- [ ] price ceiling
- [x] price floor
- [ ] tax
- [ ] subsidy
solution: It's the lowest legal price of labour.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="90"
$Q_d = 200 - 4P$ and $Q_s = 6P$ (equilibrium price 20). With a ceiling of 15, how many units are actually bought and sold?
solution: $Q_d = 140$ and $Q_s = 90$. Buyers can't buy what isn't offered, so 90 are traded.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Which is a typical sign of a binding price ceiling?
- [x] Long waiting lists and illegal resale at higher prices
- [ ] Unsold goods piling up
- [ ] Sellers cutting their prices
- [ ] Prices rising above the ceiling in stores
solution: With a shortage, goods get rationed by queues, luck or black markets instead of by price.
:::
