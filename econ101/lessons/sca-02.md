id: sca-02
title: Budget constraints
---
A budget limits what one person can buy. Drawing it shows every choice they can afford, and what each costs in terms of the other.

::: rule
With income $M$ and prices $P_1$, $P_2$, the **budget constraint** is
$$P_1 Q_1 + P_2 Q_2 = M$$
- The most of good 1 you can buy is $M / P_1$; of good 2, $M / P_2$.
- The opportunity cost of one more unit of good 1 is $P_1 / P_2$ units of good 2 (the slope of the line).
- More income shifts the line out, parallel. A price change pivots it.
:::

::: example
With \$60, burgers cost \$6 and movies \$10. At most $60/6 = 10$ burgers or $60/10 = 6$ movies. One movie costs $10/6 \approx 1.67$ burgers.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="5"
You have \$40. Books cost \$8 and magazines \$4. What's the most books you can buy?
solution: $40 / 8 = 5$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="10"
Same budget: what's the most magazines you can buy?
solution: $40 / 4 = 10$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="2"
Same budget: how many magazines does one book cost you?
solution: $P_{book} / P_{mag} = 8 / 4 = 2$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="3"
She has \$100. Shirts cost \$20 and jeans \$40. If she buys one pair of jeans, how many shirts can she still afford?
solution: $(100 - 40) / 20 = 3$.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Your income doubles and prices stay the same. Your budget line:
- [x] shifts out, staying parallel
- [ ] pivots and gets steeper
- [ ] pivots and gets flatter
- [ ] doesn't move
solution: You can buy twice as much of everything, and the price ratio (the slope) is the same.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Apples are on the horizontal axis. Their price falls; everything else stays the same. The budget line:
- [x] pivots out along the apple axis; the other end stays put
- [ ] shifts out, parallel
- [ ] pivots in along the apple axis
- [ ] doesn't move
solution: You can now buy more apples if you spend everything on them, but the most of the other good is unchanged.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=point answer="4,4"
You have \$12. Apples (across) cost \$1 and pears (up) cost \$2. Click the point where you buy 4 apples and spend everything.
graph:
x 0..12
y 0..8
plot 6-x/2 blue
hint: After 4 apples, \$8 is left for pears.
solution: $12 - 4 = 8$ dollars left buys $8 / 2 = 4$ pears: the point $(4, 4)$.
:::
