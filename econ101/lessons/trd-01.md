title: Absolute and comparative advantage
---
Why trade at all, if you could make everything yourself? Because what matters isn't who is best at something, but who gives up the least to make it. (OpenStax ch. 20)

::: rule
- **Absolute advantage:** you can make more of a good with the same resources.
- **Comparative advantage:** you make a good at a **lower opportunity cost**.
Specializing in your comparative advantage and trading lets both sides gain, even when one side has the absolute advantage in everything.
:::

::: example
In an hour, Ana sews 6 shirts or bakes 3 cakes; Ben sews 2 shirts or bakes 2 cakes.
- Ana: 1 cake costs $6/3 = 2$ shirts. Ben: 1 cake costs $2/2 = 1$ shirt.
- Ana has the absolute advantage in both, but Ben has the comparative advantage in cakes (1 shirt < 2 shirts) and Ana in shirts.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="3"
In a day, a Canadian worker makes 10 laptops or 30 shirts; a Mexican worker makes 4 laptops or 20 shirts. What is Canada's opportunity cost of one laptop, in shirts?
solution: $30 / 10 = 3$ shirts.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="5"
Same workers (Canada: 10 laptops or 30 shirts; Mexico: 4 or 20). What is Mexico's opportunity cost of one laptop, in shirts?
solution: $20 / 4 = 5$ shirts.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="0.2"
Same workers. What is Mexico's opportunity cost of one shirt, in laptops?
solution: $4 / 20 = 0.2$ laptops (the reverse of 5 shirts per laptop).
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Canada: 10 laptops or 30 shirts a day; Mexico: 4 laptops or 20 shirts. Who has the comparative advantage in laptops?
- [x] Canada
- [ ] Mexico
- [ ] Neither
solution: A laptop costs Canada 3 shirts and Mexico 5. Canada gives up less.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Same workers. Who has the absolute advantage in shirts?
- [x] Canada
- [ ] Mexico
- [ ] Neither
solution: A Canadian worker makes 30 shirts a day, a Mexican worker 20. (Mexico still has the comparative advantage in shirts: 0.2 laptops each against Canada's 0.33.)
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Can a country that's slower at making everything still gain from trade?
- [x] Yes, by specializing in the good where its opportunity cost is lower
- [ ] No, it will lose every market to the faster country
- [ ] Only if it puts tariffs on imports
- [ ] Only if it has an absolute advantage in something
solution: Every country has a comparative advantage in something, because opportunity costs are ratios.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=numeric answer="2"
Sam takes 2 hours to bake a pie and 1 hour to make a pizza; Lee takes 3 hours for either. How many pizzas does one pie cost Sam?
solution: A pie takes the 2 hours that would make $2 / 1 = 2$ pizzas.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=choice
Same bakers. Who should bake the pies?
- [ ] Sam
- [x] Lee
solution: A pie costs Lee $3/3 = 1$ pizza and Sam 2 pizzas. Lee has the comparative advantage in pies, though Sam is faster at both.
:::
