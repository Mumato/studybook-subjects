id: dem-04
title: When supply or demand shifts
---
News, weather, incomes and costs move the curves. Work out which curve moves and which way, and the new price and quantity follow.

::: rule
- Demand rises (shifts right): price **up**, quantity **up**.
- Demand falls (shifts left): price **down**, quantity **down**.
- Supply rises (shifts right): price **down**, quantity **up**.
- Supply falls (shifts left): price **up**, quantity **down**.
If both curves shift, one of price or quantity is certain and the other depends on which shift is bigger.
:::

::: example
$Q_s = 10P - 20$; demand rises from $Q_d = 100 - 5P$ to $Q_d = 130 - 5P$.
$$130 - 5P = 10P - 20 \Rightarrow P = 10, \; Q = 80$$
The price rose from 8 to 10 and the quantity from 60 to 80.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
A frost destroys much of the orange crop. In the orange juice market:
- [x] price rises, quantity falls
- [ ] price falls, quantity rises
- [ ] price and quantity both rise
- [ ] price and quantity both fall
solution: Supply shifts left.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
A widely reported study says eggs are healthy. In the egg market:
- [ ] price rises, quantity falls
- [ ] price falls, quantity rises
- [x] price and quantity both rise
- [ ] price and quantity both fall
solution: Tastes change: demand shifts right.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Bakery workers get higher wages, and at the same time people eat less bread. In the bread market:
- [x] quantity falls; the price could go either way
- [ ] price rises; the quantity could go either way
- [ ] price and quantity both fall
- [ ] price and quantity both rise
solution: Supply shifts left (price up, quantity down) and demand shifts left (price down, quantity down). Quantity falls for sure; the price depends on which shift is bigger.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="19"
$Q_s = 3P - 15$. Demand rises from $Q_d = 60 - 2P$ to $Q_d = 80 - 2P$. What is the new equilibrium price?
solution: $80 - 2P = 3P - 15$, so $95 = 5P$ and $P = 19$ (up from 15).
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="42"
$Q_s = 3P - 15$ and the new demand $Q_d = 80 - 2P$. What is the new equilibrium quantity?
solution: $P = 19$, so $Q = 80 - 38 = 42$ (up from 30).
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="6"
$Q_d = 100 - 5P$. Supply rises from $Q_s = 10P - 20$ to $Q_s = 10P + 10$. What is the new equilibrium price?
solution: $100 - 5P = 10P + 10$, so $90 = 15P$ and $P = 6$ (down from 8).
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Gasoline gets much more expensive. In the market for large gas-guzzling SUVs:
- [ ] price and quantity both rise
- [x] price and quantity both fall
- [ ] price rises, quantity falls
- [ ] price falls, quantity rises
solution: Gas and SUVs are complements, so SUV demand shifts left.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=numeric answer="-2" creative=true
$Q_d = 120 - 3P$ and $Q_s = 2P - 10$. Then demand rises by 20 units at every price and supply rises by 30 units at every price. By how much does the equilibrium price change? (Negative if it falls.)
hint: Find the old price, then solve $140 - 3P = 2P + 20$.
solution: Old: $120 - 3P = 2P - 10$ gives $P = 26$. New: $140 - 3P = 2P + 20$ gives $P = 24$. The price changes by $24 - 26 = -2$: supply grew more than demand.
:::
