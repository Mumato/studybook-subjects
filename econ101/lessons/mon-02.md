---
id: mon-02
title: How a monopoly sets its price
---
A monopoly picks its quantity, and the demand curve tells it what price that quantity sells for.

::: rule
To sell one more unit, a monopoly must lower the price on **every** unit, so **marginal revenue is below the price**.
- For straight-line demand $P = a - bQ$: $MR = a - 2bQ$ (same start, twice as steep).
- Profit is largest where $MR = MC$. Find $Q$ there, then read the **price off the demand curve**.
- Profit $= (P - ATC) \times Q$.
:::

::: example
Demand $P = 100 - 2Q$ and $MC = 20$. $MR = 100 - 4Q = 20$ gives $Q = 20$, and $P = 100 - 40 = 60$.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="4"
A monopoly can sell 5 units at \$10 each or 6 units at \$9 each. What is the marginal revenue of the 6th unit?
solution: $6 \times 9 - 5 \times 10 = 54 - 50 = 4$. Much less than the \$9 price.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="20"
Demand is $P = 50 - Q$ and $MC = 10$. What quantity maximizes the monopoly's profit?
solution: $MR = 50 - 2Q = 10$, so $Q = 20$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="30"
Demand $P = 50 - Q$, $MC = 10$, best quantity 20. What price does it charge?
solution: $P = 50 - 20 = 30$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="400"
Same monopoly: $Q = 20$, $P = 30$, and every unit costs \$10 (no fixed costs). What is its profit?
solution: $(30 - 10) \times 20 = 400$.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="75"
Demand is $P = 120 - 3Q$ and $MC = 30$. What price does the monopoly charge?
solution: $MR = 120 - 6Q = 30$ gives $Q = 15$. $P = 120 - 45 = 75$.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Why is a monopoly's marginal revenue less than its price?
- [x] To sell another unit it must cut the price on all its units
- [ ] Its costs rise as it sells more
- [ ] The government taxes its revenue
- [ ] Buyers pay late
solution: The extra unit brings in its price, but every other unit now sells for less.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
At the monopoly's best output, the price is:
- [x] above marginal cost
- [ ] equal to marginal cost
- [ ] below marginal cost
- [ ] equal to marginal revenue
solution: $MR = MC$ and $P > MR$, so $P > MC$. Some buyers who value the good above its cost go without.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=numeric answer="200" creative=true
Demand is $P = 50 - Q$ and $MC = 10$. A competitive market would produce where $P = MC$; the monopoly makes 20 units at \$30. What is the deadweight loss of the monopoly?
hint: Find the competitive quantity, then the triangle between it and 20.
solution: Competitive: $50 - Q = 10$ gives $Q = 40$. Deadweight loss $= \frac{1}{2} \times (40 - 20) \times (30 - 10) = 200$.
:::
