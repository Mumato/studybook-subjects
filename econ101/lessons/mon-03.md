title: The cost of monopoly, and what to do about it
---
Compared with competition, a monopoly charges more and sells less. Some firms go further and charge different buyers different prices.

::: rule
- A monopoly sets $P > MC$ and makes less than a competitive market would: the lost trades are a **deadweight loss**.
- **Price discrimination:** charging different buyers different prices for the same good, by what they're willing to pay (student and senior discounts, early-bird fares). It needs market power, a way to tell groups apart, and a way to stop resale. Perfect price discrimination charges each buyer their maximum, leaving no deadweight loss but no consumer surplus.
- Governments respond with **competition (antitrust) law**, blocking mergers, breaking up firms, or regulating a natural monopoly's price.
:::

::: example
Demand $P = 100 - 2Q$, $MC = 20$. The monopoly makes 20 units at \$60; competition would make 40 at \$20.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="40"
Demand is $P = 100 - 2Q$ and $MC = 20$. How many units would a competitive market produce?
solution: $P = MC$: $100 - 2Q = 20$, so $Q = 40$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="400"
Same market: the monopoly makes 20 units at \$60, competition would make 40, and $MC = 20$. What is the deadweight loss?
solution: $\frac{1}{2} \times (40 - 20) \times (60 - 20) = 400$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="800"
Same monopoly: 20 units at \$60, each costing \$20, no fixed costs. What is its profit?
solution: $(60 - 20) \times 20 = 800$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Which is price discrimination?
- [x] Cheaper movie tickets for seniors
- [ ] A store raising all its prices
- [ ] Two stores charging different prices
- [ ] Prices falling in a sale for everyone
solution: The same seat, sold at different prices to different groups of buyers.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Price discrimination only works if the seller can:
- [x] stop buyers who pay less from reselling to those who'd pay more
- [ ] sell to everyone at the same price
- [ ] find a perfectly competitive market
- [ ] lower its marginal cost
solution: Otherwise students would buy cheap tickets and sell them to everyone else.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="0"
A monopoly charges every buyer exactly the most they're willing to pay (perfect price discrimination). How big is the deadweight loss?
solution: Zero: it sells to everyone who values the good above its cost. It just takes all the surplus.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Competition (antitrust) law mainly aims to:
- [ ] set the price of every good
- [x] stop mergers and practices that reduce competition
- [ ] protect firms from foreign rivals
- [ ] guarantee every firm a profit
solution: In Canada, the Competition Bureau reviews mergers and acts against price-fixing.
:::
