title: Monopolistic competition
---
Most stores and restaurants you know aren't perfect competitors or monopolies. They're in between. (OpenStax ch. 10)

::: rule
**Monopolistic competition:** many firms, **differentiated** products (each brand is a bit different), easy entry and exit.
- Each firm faces its own downward-sloping demand, so like a monopoly it sets $MR = MC$ and reads its price off its demand curve.
- **Short run:** it can earn profits. **Long run:** profits attract new firms, each firm's demand shrinks, and economic profit falls to **zero** ($P = ATC$).
- Even then $P > MC$, and it produces below the output of lowest ATC: **excess capacity**.
:::

::: example
A pizza shop's demand is $P = 40 - Q$ and $MC = 10$. $MR = 40 - 2Q = 10$ gives $Q = 15$ and $P = 25$.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
Which market is the best example of monopolistic competition?
- [x] Restaurants in a big city
- [ ] Wheat
- [ ] Tap water
- [ ] Passenger jets
solution: Many sellers, each a little different, and anyone can open one.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="15"
A café's demand is $P = 40 - Q$ and $MC = 10$. What quantity maximizes its profit?
solution: $MR = 40 - 2Q = 10$, so $Q = 15$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="75"
Same café: 15 units at a price of \$25, with an ATC of \$20. What is its profit?
solution: $(25 - 20) \times 15 = 75$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Cafés in a neighbourhood earn economic profits. Over time, each café's demand curve:
- [x] shifts left as new cafés open
- [ ] shifts right
- [ ] becomes horizontal
- [ ] stays the same
solution: New cafés take some of each existing café's customers.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="0"
What is a monopolistically competitive firm's economic profit in the long run?
solution: Zero: entry continues until $P = ATC$.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
"Excess capacity" in monopolistic competition means firms:
- [x] produce less than the output where their ATC is lowest
- [ ] produce more than buyers want
- [ ] earn large profits forever
- [ ] have too many workers
solution: In the long run $P = ATC$ on the falling part of the ATC curve, so making more would lower the cost per unit.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Product differentiation means:
- [x] buyers see each firm's product as somewhat different
- [ ] all firms sell identical goods
- [ ] firms charge different buyers different prices
- [ ] the government sets product standards
solution: Location, quality, style and brand all make products different, which gives each firm a little market power.
:::
