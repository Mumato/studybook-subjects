title: When one side knows more
---
Markets work best when both sides know what they're trading. When one side knows more, good deals can vanish. (OpenStax ch. 16)

::: rule
**Asymmetric information:** one side of a trade knows something the other doesn't.
- **Adverse selection** (before the deal): the people most eager to trade are the ones the other side least wants. Used-car buyers fear "lemons"; health insurance attracts the sickest.
- **Moral hazard** (after the deal): once protected, people take more risks, because someone else bears the cost.
- Fixes: warranties, inspections and reviews; **signals** (a degree, a guarantee); **screening** (medical exams); deductibles and co-pays; required insurance for everyone.
:::

::: example
Half of used cars are good (worth \$10,000) and half are lemons (\$4,000). A buyer who can't tell offers the average, \$7,000. Owners of good cars won't sell at that, so mostly lemons are left.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
Which is **adverse selection**?
- [x] People who expect big medical bills are the most likely to buy health insurance
- [ ] Insured drivers drive less carefully
- [ ] A worker slacks off when the boss is away
- [ ] An insured renter leaves windows open
solution: It happens before the deal: the riskiest buyers select themselves in.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
Which is **moral hazard**?
- [ ] Only sick people buy insurance
- [x] After buying theft insurance, people stop locking their bikes
- [ ] Sellers of bad cars are eager to sell
- [ ] Buyers pay the average price
solution: Behaviour changes after the deal, because someone else now bears the risk.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Which mainly reduces **moral hazard** in insurance?
- [x] A deductible, so you pay the first part of any claim
- [ ] A lower price for everyone
- [ ] Advertising
- [ ] Selling only to young people
solution: With some of your own money at stake, you stay careful.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
A used-car dealer offers a one-year warranty. This helps because:
- [x] it signals the car is good and protects the buyer if it isn't
- [ ] it makes the car cheaper
- [ ] it creates moral hazard
- [ ] it's required by law
solution: Sellers of lemons wouldn't want to promise repairs, so the warranty is believable.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A job applicant's university degree can work as a **signal** because:
- [x] it's harder to earn for people who'd be less productive
- [ ] employers are required to ask for one
- [ ] it guarantees the applicant knows the job
- [ ] it costs nothing
solution: A signal is believable when it's costly to fake.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="7000"
Half the used cars for sale are good (worth \$10,000) and half are lemons (worth \$4,000). A buyer who can't tell them apart pays the average value. How much, in dollars?
solution: $0.5 \times 10000 + 0.5 \times 4000 = \$7{,}000$.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
At that \$7,000 price, owners of good cars (worth \$10,000):
- [x] mostly keep their cars, so the market fills with lemons
- [ ] happily sell
- [ ] raise the price to \$10,000 and sell
- [ ] buy lemons
solution: They'd lose \$3,000 by selling. As they leave, the average quality and price fall further.
:::
