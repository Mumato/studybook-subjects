id: ext-02
title: Fixing externalities
---
Governments have several tools to make buyers and sellers count the costs and benefits they leave out.

::: rule
- **Pigouvian tax:** a tax per unit equal to the external cost. It makes sellers count the damage, so the market moves to the best quantity.
- **Subsidy:** a payment per unit for positive externalities (education, vaccines).
- **Regulation:** limits or required technologies (command and control).
- **Cap and trade:** a fixed total of pollution permits that firms can buy and sell. Firms that can cut pollution cheaply do the cutting, so the cap is reached at the lowest total cost.
- **Coase theorem:** if property rights are clear and bargaining is cheap, the people involved can negotiate their way to the best outcome themselves.
:::

::: example
Each unit causes \$10 of damage. A \$10 tax per unit raises the supply curve by \$10, exactly to the social cost curve.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="10"
Each unit of a good causes \$10 of damage to others. What per-unit tax moves the market to the best quantity?
solution: A tax equal to the external cost: \$10.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="25"
Demand is $P = 60 - Q$ and private supply is $P = Q$. With a \$10 tax, supply becomes $P = Q + 10$. What quantity is sold?
solution: $60 - Q = Q + 10$, so $Q = 25$: exactly the best quantity.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="250"
With the \$10 tax, 25 units are sold. How much tax does the government collect?
solution: $25 \times 10 = \$250$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Firm A can cut a tonne of pollution for \$20; firm B for \$50. To cut one tonne at the lowest cost:
- [x] A should cut it, perhaps selling a permit to B
- [ ] B should cut it
- [ ] they should each cut half
solution: A does it for \$20 instead of \$50. With cap and trade, B pays A somewhere between \$20 and \$50 for a permit, and both gain.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="150" creative=true
Two firms each emit 10 tonnes. A cap of 14 permits is set, 7 for each, so 6 tonnes must be cut. Firm A can cut a tonne for \$30, firm B for \$80. How many dollars does society save if A does all the cutting and sells 3 permits to B, instead of each cutting 3?
solution: Each cutting 3: $3 \times 30 + 3 \times 80 = 330$. A cutting all 6: $6 \times 30 = 180$. Saving: $330 - 180 = \$150$.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
A government pays part of university tuition. Economically, this:
- [x] corrects a positive externality, since educated people benefit others too
- [ ] corrects a negative externality
- [ ] creates a deadweight loss with no benefit
- [ ] is a Pigouvian tax
solution: Without the subsidy, people would buy less education than is best for society.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
The Coase theorem says private bargaining can fix an externality when:
- [x] property rights are clear and bargaining is cheap
- [ ] the government sets a tax
- [ ] many people are affected
- [ ] the externality is positive
solution: With few parties and clear rights, they can pay each other to reach the best outcome. With thousands affected, bargaining breaks down.
:::
