::: problem id=e1 type=numeric answer="42"
What is the complement of a 48° angle?
solution: $90 - 48 = 42°$.
:::

::: problem id=e2 type=numeric answer="70"
Two vertical angles are $5x - 20$ and $3x + 16$ degrees. How big is each angle?
solution: $5x - 20 = 3x + 16$, so $x = 18$. Angle $5(18) - 20 = 70°$.
:::

::: problem id=e3 type=numeric answer="76"
Each base angle of an isosceles triangle is 52°. What is the top angle?
solution: $180 - 2 \cdot 52 = 76°$.
:::

::: problem id=e4 type=numeric answer="100"
A triangle's angles are x, 3x and 5x. What is the largest angle?
solution: $9x = 180$, $x = 20$. Largest $5 \cdot 20 = 100°$.
:::

::: problem id=e5 type=numeric answer="80"
An exterior angle of a triangle is 140°. The two interior angles not next to it are in the ratio 3 : 4. What is the larger of them?
solution: They add to 140°: 7 parts, so one part is 20°. Larger $4 \cdot 20 = 80°$.
:::

::: problem id=e6 type=numeric answer="20"
A triangle has sides 6, 9, 12. A similar triangle has shortest side 10. How long is its longest side?
solution: $k = \frac{10}{6} = \frac{5}{3}$. Longest $12 \cdot \frac{5}{3} = 20$.
:::

::: problem id=e7 type=numeric answer="100/3"
Two similar triangles have matching sides in the ratio 3 : 5. The smaller has area 12. What is the area of the larger?
solution: Areas scale by $\left(\frac{5}{3}\right)^2 = \frac{25}{9}$. $12 \cdot \frac{25}{9} = \frac{100}{3}$.
:::

::: problem id=e8 type=numeric answer="48pi"
What is the volume of a cone with radius 4 and height 9?
solution: $\frac{1}{3}\pi \cdot 16 \cdot 9 = 48\pi$.
:::

::: problem id=e9 type=numeric answer="20"
A cylinder-shaped tub has radius 3 cm and height 10 cm. How many balls of ice cream, each a sphere of radius 1.5 cm, have the same total volume as the full tub?
solution: Tub $\pi \cdot 9 \cdot 10 = 90\pi$. Ball $\frac{4}{3}\pi \cdot 3.375 = 4.5\pi$. $\frac{90\pi}{4.5\pi} = 20$.
:::

::: problem id=e10 type=numeric answer="3/5"

solution: $\frac{9}{15} = \frac{3}{5}$.
:::

::: problem id=e15 type=numeric answer="22.62" tolerance="0.01"
Use a calculator in degree mode. A right triangle has legs 5 and 12. What is its smallest angle in degrees? (to hundredths)

:::

::: problem id=e16 type=numeric answer="100sqrt(3)/3" tolerance="0.01"
From 100 m away, the angle of elevation to the top of a tower is 30°. How tall is the tower? (exact or to hundredths)
solution: $100\tan 30° = \frac{100}{\sqrt{3}} = \frac{100\sqrt{3}}{3} \approx 57.74$ m.
:::

::: problem id=e17 type=numeric answer="4sqrt(6)" tolerance="0.01"
In triangle ABC, $A = 45°$, $B = 60°$ and $a = 8$. Find b. (exact or to hundredths)
solution: $b = \frac{8\sin 60°}{\sin 45°} = \frac{8\sqrt{3}}{\sqrt{2}} = 4\sqrt{6} \approx 9.80$.
:::

::: problem id=e18 type=numeric answer="18"
A triangle has sides 8 and 9 with a 30° angle between them. What is its area?
solution: $\frac{1}{2} \cdot 8 \cdot 9 \cdot \frac{1}{2} = 18$.
:::

::: problem id=e19 type=numeric answer="0"
In triangle ABC, $A = 30°$, $a = 3$ and $b = 8$. How many triangles fit these measurements?
solution: $\sin B = \frac{8 \cdot \frac{1}{2}}{3} = \frac{4}{3} > 1$: **0**.
:::

::: problem id=e20 type=numeric answer="7"

::: problem id=e30 type=numeric answer="-sqrt(5)/3"

::: problem id=e31 type=numeric answer="-pi/4"

::: problem id=e32 type=numeric answer="5/13"

:::

::: problem id=e33 type=numeric answer="-2-sqrt(3)"
$\tan 105° = ?$ (exact)
solution: $\tan(60° + 45°) = \frac{\sqrt{3} + 1}{1 - \sqrt{3}}$. Multiply by $\frac{1 + \sqrt{3}}{1 + \sqrt{3}}$: $\frac{4 + 2\sqrt{3}}{-2} = -2 - \sqrt{3}$.
:::

::: problem id=e34 type=numeric answer="-24/25"
$\cos\theta = -\frac{3}{5}$ and θ is in quadrant II. What is $\sin 2\theta$?
solution: $\sin\theta = \frac{4}{5}$. $2 \cdot \frac{4}{5} \cdot \left(-\frac{3}{5}\right) = -\frac{24}{25}$.
:::

::: problem id=e35 type=multi answers="pi/6;5pi/6;3pi/2"
Solve $\cos 2x = \sin x$ for $0 \le x < 2\pi$.
solution: $1 - 2\sin^2 x = \sin x$, so $2\sin^2 x + \sin x - 1 = 0$, $(2\sin x - 1)(\sin x + 1) = 0$. $\sin x = \frac{1}{2}$: $\frac{\pi}{6}, \frac{5\pi}{6}$. $\sin x = -1$: $\frac{3\pi}{2}$.
:::
