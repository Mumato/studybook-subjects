---
id: mul-02
title: Multiplying bigger numbers
---
::: rule
To multiply by a one-digit number, multiply each digit starting with the **ones**, and **carry** just like in adding.

To multiply by a two-digit number:
1. multiply by the ones digit
2. multiply by the tens digit (that's really tens, so put a 0 at the end)
3. add the two results
:::

::: example
$47 \times 6$

Ones: $7 \times 6 = 42$. Write 2, carry 4.
Tens: $4 \times 6 = 24$, plus the 4 carried is 28.

The answer is **282**.
:::

::: example
$34 \times 25$

$34 \times 5 = 170$
$34 \times 20 = 680$
$170 + 680 = 850$

The answer is **850**.
:::

::: problem id=x9 type=choice set=extra
Kim worked out $46 \times 23$ like this: $46 \times 3 = 138$, $46 \times 2 = 92$, $138 + 92 = 230$. What went wrong?
- [x] The 2 means 20, so she needed $46 \times 20 = 920$; the answer is 1058
- [ ] Nothing, 230 is right
- [ ] She should multiply $46 \times 3 \times 2 = 276$
- [ ] She should add $46 + 23 = 69$

::: problem id=x19 type=numeric answer="128" set=extra
Use the digits 2, 3 and 4 once each to make a 2-digit number times a 1-digit number. What is the largest product?
hint: Try putting the biggest digit as the single multiplier, and compare with other choices.
solution: $32 \times 4 = 128$, $42 \times 3 = 126$, $43 \times 2 = 86$. The largest is **128**.
:::

::: problem id=x20 type=numeric answer="1312" set=extra creative=true
Use the digits 1, 2, 3 and 4 once each to make a 2-digit number times a 2-digit number. What is the largest product?
hint: 4 and 3 go in the tens places. Then decide where 2 and 1 go.
solution: $42 \times 31 = 1302$ but $41 \times 32 = 1312$. (Putting the bigger ones digit with the smaller tens digit wins.) The largest is **1312**.
:::
