---
id: pct-04
title: Discounts, tax and percent change
---
::: rule
- **Discount** of d%: new price = price × (1 − d as a decimal)
- **Tax** or **increase** of t%: new price = price × (1 + t as a decimal)
- **Percent change** = change ÷ original × 100
:::

::: example
An 80-dollar game is 25% off. $80 \times 0.75 = 60$ dollars.
:::

::: example
A price goes from 50 to 60 dollars. The change is 10, and $10 \div 50 = 0.2$, so it rose by **20%**.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="48"
A 60-dollar jacket is 20% off. What is the sale price in dollars?
solution: $60 \times 0.8 = 48$ dollars.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="42"
A meal costs 40 dollars plus 5% tax. What is the total?
solution: $40 \times 1.05 = 42$ dollars.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="20"
A price goes from 25 to 30 dollars. By what percent did it increase?
hint: The change is 5. Divide by the original price.
solution: $5 \div 25 = 0.2 = 20\%$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="25"
A price drops from 80 to 60 dollars. By what percent did it decrease?
solution: The change is 20. $20 \div 80 = 0.25 = 25\%$.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="35"
A 50-dollar game is 30% off. What is the sale price?
solution: $50 \times 0.7 = 35$ dollars.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="230"
A town of 200 people grows by 15%. How many people live there now?
solution: $200 \times 1.15 = 230$.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=numeric answer="50"
After a 10% discount you pay 45 dollars. What was the original price?
hint: You paid 90% of the original.
solution: $45 \div 0.9 = 50$ dollars.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=choice
A price of 100 goes up 10%, then the new price goes down 10%. What is the final price?
- [x] 99
- [ ] 100
- [ ] 101
hint: The second 10% is taken from a bigger number.
solution: $100 \times 1.1 = 110$, then $110 \times 0.9 = 99$.
:::

::: problem id=c1 type=numeric answer="60" creative=true
A shirt is 50% off, and then there is a further 20% off the sale price. Altogether, what percent off the original price is that?
hint: Start with a price of 100.
solution: $100 \to 50 \to 50 \times 0.8 = 40$. You pay 40 out of 100, so it is **60%** off (not 70%).
:::
