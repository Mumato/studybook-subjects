title: Attachment, parenting and psychosocial stages
---
Children need more than food and shelter: they need a secure bond with someone who cares for them. That bond shapes relationships for years. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 9)

::: rule
- **Harlow's monkeys** clung to a soft cloth "mother" rather than a wire one that gave milk: **contact comfort**, not just food, drives attachment.
- **Ainsworth's Strange Situation** sorts infants' attachment: **secure** (upset when the parent leaves, comforted on return), **avoidant** (ignores the parent), **resistant**/ambivalent (clingy, hard to soothe) and **disorganised**.
- **Parenting styles** (Baumrind): **authoritative** (warm, with clear rules: tends to work best), **authoritarian** (strict, cold, "because I said so"), **permissive** (warm, few rules) and **uninvolved**.
- **Erikson** described 8 psychosocial stages, each with a challenge: trust vs mistrust (infancy), autonomy vs shame, initiative vs guilt, industry vs inferiority, **identity vs role confusion** (adolescence), **intimacy vs isolation** (young adulthood), generativity vs stagnation, **integrity vs despair** (old age).
:::

::: example
In the Strange Situation, a one-year-old plays happily while mum is in the room, cries when she leaves, and calms quickly in her arms when she returns, then goes back to playing. That's **secure attachment**: the parent is a safe base for exploring.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
Harlow's monkey studies showed that attachment depends mostly on:
- [ ] who provides food
- [x] comfort and closeness
- [ ] which mother is bigger
solution: Monkeys preferred the soft cloth mother, even when the wire one held the bottle.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
In the Strange Situation, an infant barely reacts when the parent leaves and ignores them on return. This is:
- [ ] secure attachment
- [x] avoidant attachment
- [ ] resistant attachment
solution: Avoidant infants show little distress and avoid the parent on reunion.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Parents who are warm, explain their rules and listen to their child, but stay firm, are:
- [x] authoritative
- [ ] authoritarian
- [ ] permissive
- [ ] uninvolved
solution: This style is linked with the best outcomes in many studies.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
"Do it because I said so!" with strict rules and little warmth describes:
- [ ] authoritative parenting
- [x] authoritarian parenting
- [ ] permissive parenting
solution: Authoritarian parents demand obedience without explanation.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="8"
How many psychosocial stages did Erikson describe?
solution: Eight, from infancy to old age.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
According to Erikson, the main challenge of adolescence is:
- [ ] trust vs mistrust
- [x] identity vs role confusion
- [ ] integrity vs despair
solution: Teens work out who they are and what they value.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
An older adult looks back on life with satisfaction rather than regret. In Erikson's terms, they have achieved:
- [x] integrity
- [ ] intimacy
- [ ] industry
solution: Integrity vs despair is the final stage.
:::
