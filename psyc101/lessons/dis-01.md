title: Defining disorders; anxiety, OCD and PTSD
---
Psychological disorders are common: in any given year, roughly one adult in five experiences one. Understanding them starts with how psychologists decide what counts as a disorder. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 15)

::: rule
- A **psychological disorder** involves significant **distress** or **dysfunction** (trouble functioning in daily life), not just being unusual. Clinicians diagnose using the **DSM-5** (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual, 5th edition).
- Causes are usually **biopsychosocial**: genes and brain chemistry, thoughts and learning, and life circumstances combine. The **diathesis-stress** model: an inherited vulnerability plus stress can trigger a disorder.
- **Anxiety disorders:** **generalised anxiety disorder** (constant, hard-to-control worry), **panic disorder** (sudden panic attacks and fear of them), **specific phobia** (intense fear of one thing), **social anxiety disorder** (fear of being judged).
- **Obsessive-compulsive disorder:** unwanted, repeated thoughts (**obsessions**) and urges to perform rituals (**compulsions**).
- **PTSD:** after a trauma, intrusive memories, avoidance, negative mood and being constantly on edge, lasting more than a month.
:::

::: example
Disliking spiders is common. Someone has a **specific phobia** when the fear is out of proportion and gets in the way of life: they can't go into their garden, check every room before entering, and feel panic at a picture of a spider.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="20"
Roughly one adult in five experiences a psychological disorder in a given year. What percent is that?
solution: $1/5 = 20\%$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="5"
Which edition of the DSM is current (as in "DSM-?")?
solution: The DSM-5 (with a 2022 text revision, DSM-5-TR).
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
What mainly separates a disorder from unusual behaviour?
- [ ] it is rare
- [x] it causes significant distress or interferes with daily life
- [ ] other people disapprove of it
solution: Being different isn't a disorder; distress and dysfunction are key.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Someone has sudden, intense episodes of pounding heart and terror, and starts avoiding places where it happened. This fits:
- [ ] specific phobia
- [x] panic disorder
- [ ] obsessive-compulsive disorder
solution: Recurring panic attacks plus fear of having more.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A woman can't stop thinking her hands are contaminated and washes them dozens of times a day until they're raw. This fits:
- [x] obsessive-compulsive disorder
- [ ] generalised anxiety disorder
- [ ] PTSD
solution: An obsession (contamination) with a compulsion (washing).
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
A soldier back from war has nightmares and flashbacks, avoids crowds and jumps at loud noises, for months. This fits:
- [ ] social anxiety disorder
- [x] post-traumatic stress disorder
- [ ] panic disorder
solution: Intrusive memories, avoidance and hyperarousal after trauma.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
The diathesis-stress model says a disorder develops from:
- [ ] bad parenting alone
- [x] an underlying vulnerability combined with stressful experiences
- [ ] stress alone, in anyone
solution: Vulnerability and stress interact; neither alone usually explains it.
:::
