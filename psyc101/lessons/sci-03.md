title: Statistics and ethics
---
Data mean nothing until they're summarised, and research on people is only acceptable if it protects them. This lesson covers both. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 2)

::: rule
- **Mean:** the average (add up, divide by how many). **Median:** the middle score when sorted. **Mode:** the most common score.
- **Standard deviation** (SD) measures how spread out scores are. In a **normal distribution**, about **68%** of scores fall within 1 SD of the mean and about **95%** within 2 SD.
- A result is **statistically significant** when it's unlikely to be chance, usually less than a 5% chance ($p < 0.05$).
- **Ethics:** **informed consent** (people agree knowing what's involved), the right to **withdraw**, **confidentiality**, **debriefing** afterward (especially if deception was used), and approval by an ethics board (IRB).
:::

::: example
Five quiz scores: 6, 7, 7, 9, 21.
- Mean: $(6+7+7+9+21)/5 = 50/5 = 10$
- Median: the middle score, 7
- Mode: 7

The one high score (21) pulls the mean up, but not the median. That's why incomes are usually reported as a median.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="8"
What is the mean of 4, 6, 8, 10, 12?
solution: $(4+6+8+10+12)/5 = 40/5 = 8$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="5"
What is the median of 3, 9, 1, 7, 5?
hint: Sort them first.
solution: Sorted: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9. The middle one is 5.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="4"
What is the mode of 2, 4, 4, 6, 9?
solution: 4 appears twice, more than any other score.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="68"
In a normal distribution, about what percent of scores lie within 1 standard deviation of the mean?
solution: About 68%. Within 2 SD it's about 95%.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="95"
A test has a mean of 50 and a standard deviation of 10, and scores are normally distributed. About what percent of people score between 30 and 70?
hint: How many standard deviations from the mean are 30 and 70?
solution: 30 and 70 are 2 SD below and above the mean, so about 95%.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
After a study that used mild deception, the researcher explains its true purpose to each participant. This is called:
- [ ] informed consent
- [x] debriefing
- [ ] random assignment
- [ ] confidentiality
solution: Debriefing comes afterward; informed consent comes before.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
A participant wants to stop halfway through a study. Ethically, the researcher must:
- [x] let them withdraw at any time, without penalty
- [ ] ask them to finish, since they signed up
- [ ] withdraw only if they give a good reason
solution: The right to withdraw is a core protection in research ethics.
:::
