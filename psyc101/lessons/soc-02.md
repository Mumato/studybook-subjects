title: Conformity, obedience and groups
---
Some of psychology's most famous experiments showed how strongly ordinary people are swayed by the group and by authority, often against their own judgement. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 12)

::: rule
- **Conformity** (Asch): people matched an obviously wrong group answer about line lengths on about **one third** of trials, and about **three quarters** conformed at least once.
- **Obedience** (Milgram): told by an experimenter, about **65%** of participants went on to give what they believed were 450-volt shocks to another person. Obedience fell when the authority was absent or others refused.
- **Groupthink:** a close group's wish for harmony leads it to ignore problems and make bad decisions.
- **Social facilitation:** an audience improves performance on easy, well-practised tasks but hurts it on hard ones. **Social loafing:** people put in less effort in groups.
- **Deindividuation:** losing self-awareness in a crowd, which can unleash behaviour people wouldn't do alone.
:::

![Which line matches the standard?](assets/soc-asch.3x.png)

::: example
In Asch's study, a participant sat with 6 others who, secretly working with the researcher, all said line A matched the standard, when it was clearly B. Many participants went along, at least sometimes. When just **one** other person gave the right answer, conformity dropped sharply.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="65"
In Milgram's original study, about what percent of participants continued to the maximum 450-volt shock?
solution: About 65%, far more than experts had predicted.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
In Asch's line study, what most reduced conformity?
- [ ] a bigger group
- [x] one other person giving the correct answer
- [ ] longer lines
solution: A single ally breaks the group's unanimity.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A team project member does little work, knowing others will pick up the slack. This is:
- [ ] social facilitation
- [x] social loafing
- [ ] groupthink
solution: Effort drops when individual contributions aren't noticed.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
A skilled pianist plays even better in front of an audience, while a beginner plays worse. This is:
- [x] social facilitation
- [ ] deindividuation
- [ ] obedience
solution: An audience boosts arousal, which helps well-learned tasks and hurts new or hard ones.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A government advisory team, eager to agree with its leader, never discusses the risks of a plan, which then fails badly. This is:
- [x] groupthink
- [ ] social loafing
- [ ] the bystander effect
solution: Harmony is put above realistic evaluation.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
In masked crowds, people sometimes vandalise in ways they never would alone. This is explained by:
- [ ] social facilitation
- [x] deindividuation
- [ ] cognitive dissonance
solution: Anonymity in a group lowers self-awareness and responsibility.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
Why might Milgram's participants have obeyed, even though most were visibly distressed?
- [ ] They were unusually cruel people.
- [x] The authority took responsibility, the steps increased gradually, and they were in an unfamiliar situation where the experimenter seemed to know best.
- [ ] They didn't believe anything was happening.
solution: Small steps (15 volts at a time) and a legitimate-seeming authority pulled ordinary people along. Milgram's work is a warning about situational pressure, and it also led to stricter research ethics.
:::
