title: Classical conditioning
---
Ivan Pavlov was studying digestion in dogs when he noticed they started drooling before the food arrived, at the sound of footsteps. He had stumbled on one of the most basic kinds of learning. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 6)

::: rule
**Classical conditioning:** learning to link two stimuli, so that one starts to trigger a response the other already caused.
- **Unconditioned stimulus (US):** naturally triggers a response (food). **Unconditioned response (UR):** the natural response (drooling at food).
- **Neutral stimulus:** causes no response at first (a bell). Paired repeatedly with the US, it becomes a **conditioned stimulus (CS)** that triggers a **conditioned response (CR)** (drooling at the bell).
- **Extinction:** if the CS keeps appearing without the US, the CR fades. **Spontaneous recovery:** after a pause, the CR briefly returns.
- **Generalisation:** similar stimuli trigger the CR too. **Discrimination:** learning to respond only to the CS.
:::

::: example
In 1920, John Watson conditioned "Little Albert", an 11-month-old, to fear a white rat. Each time Albert touched the rat, a loud bang sounded behind him. Soon the rat alone made him cry, and so did a rabbit and a fur coat (**generalisation**).
- US: loud bang. UR: fear of the bang.
- CS: the rat. CR: fear of the rat.

(Such a study would never be allowed today.)
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
In Pavlov's experiment, the bell, **after** conditioning, is the:
- [ ] unconditioned stimulus
- [x] conditioned stimulus
- [ ] unconditioned response
- [ ] conditioned response
solution: It's conditioned because the dog learned to respond to it.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
Drooling when food is placed in the mouth is the:
- [x] unconditioned response
- [ ] conditioned response
- [ ] conditioned stimulus
solution: Nobody has to learn to drool at food: it's unconditioned.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Your phone's ringtone made you anxious after many stressful calls. Now a similar ringtone on someone else's phone makes you tense too. This is:
- [x] generalisation
- [ ] discrimination
- [ ] extinction
solution: The response spreads to stimuli similar to the CS.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Pavlov rang the bell again and again without food, and the dogs drooled less and less. This is:
- [ ] spontaneous recovery
- [x] extinction
- [ ] generalisation
solution: Without the US, the CS gradually stops triggering the CR.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=text answers="spontaneous recovery"
Days after extinction, Pavlov rang the bell and the dogs drooled a little again. What is this return of the response called?
solution: Spontaneous recovery: extinction weakens the link rather than erasing it.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
You got food poisoning after eating sushi, and now the smell of sushi makes you feel sick. What is the conditioned stimulus?
- [ ] the food poisoning
- [x] the sushi
- [ ] feeling sick
solution: The sushi (CS) was paired with the illness (US); now the sushi alone causes nausea (CR). Taste aversions can form after just one pairing.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
In the Little Albert study, the loud noise was the:
- [x] unconditioned stimulus
- [ ] conditioned stimulus
- [ ] conditioned response
solution: Loud noises frighten babies naturally, with no learning needed.
:::
