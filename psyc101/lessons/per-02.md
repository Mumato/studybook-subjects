title: Humanistic and social-cognitive views
---
Humanistic psychologists saw people as basically good and growing; social-cognitive psychologists saw personality as shaped by thinking, learning and the situations we're in. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 11)

::: rule
- **Carl Rogers:** people naturally strive to grow. Growth needs **unconditional positive regard** (acceptance without conditions), genuineness and empathy. Problems arise when our **self-concept** doesn't match our experience.
- **Maslow:** people who reach **self-actualisation** fulfil their potential.
- **Bandura's reciprocal determinism:** our thoughts, our behaviour and our environment all influence each other.
- **Self-efficacy:** belief in your ability to succeed at a task. High self-efficacy leads to more effort and persistence.
- **Locus of control** (Rotter): **internal** (I control what happens to me) or **external** (luck or others control it).
:::

::: example
Two students fail a test. One thinks, "I didn't study enough; next time I'll start earlier" (**internal** locus of control). The other thinks, "The teacher hates me, nothing I do matters" (**external**). The first is more likely to change what they do, and to do better.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
A parent shows love for their child no matter what grades the child gets. Rogers would call this:
- [x] unconditional positive regard
- [ ] reciprocal determinism
- [ ] an external locus of control
solution: Love and acceptance that don't depend on performance.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
"Luck decides whether I get the job, so why prepare?" shows:
- [ ] an internal locus of control
- [x] an external locus of control
- [ ] high self-efficacy
solution: Believing outside forces control outcomes.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=text answers="self-efficacy;self efficacy;selfefficacy"
What is the term for a person's belief that they can succeed at a specific task?
solution: Self-efficacy (Bandura).
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Bandura's reciprocal determinism says that:
- [ ] personality is set by the id
- [x] behaviour, thoughts and environment all affect one another
- [ ] only the environment matters
solution: A shy person avoids parties (behaviour), meets fewer people (environment), and stays unsure in social settings (thoughts), each feeding the others.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
The humanistic view of human nature is mostly:
- [x] optimistic: people strive to grow
- [ ] pessimistic: people are driven by hidden urges
- [ ] neutral: people are shaped only by rewards
solution: Humanism focused on growth, choice and potential.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Rogers believed distress comes from a gap between:
- [ ] the id and the superego
- [x] your self-concept and your actual experiences
- [ ] rewards and punishments
solution: When how you see yourself doesn't match reality, anxiety follows.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Which person is likely to keep trying after failure?
- [x] someone with high self-efficacy for the task
- [ ] someone with an external locus of control
- [ ] someone who thinks ability can't change
solution: Believing you can succeed sustains effort.
:::
