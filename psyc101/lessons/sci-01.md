title: What psychology is
---
People have always wondered why we think, feel and act as we do. Psychology turned those questions into science: instead of trusting common sense, it tests ideas with careful observation and measurement. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 1)

::: rule
**Psychology** is the scientific study of the **mind** (thoughts, feelings, memories) and **behaviour** (what people and animals do).
- **1879:** Wilhelm **Wundt** opened the first psychology laboratory in Leipzig, Germany.
- Early schools: **structuralism** (Wundt, Titchener: break experience into parts by introspection), **functionalism** (William James: what is the mind *for*?), **psychoanalysis** (Freud: the unconscious), **behaviourism** (Watson, Skinner: study only observable behaviour), **humanism** (Maslow, Rogers: growth and free will).
- Today psychologists combine **perspectives**: biological, cognitive, behavioural, psychodynamic, humanistic, sociocultural and evolutionary. Each asks a different "why".
:::

::: example
Why does someone feel anxious before an exam? Each perspective gives part of the answer:
- **Biological:** stress hormones and a racing heart.
- **Cognitive:** thoughts like "If I fail, everything is ruined."
- **Behavioural:** anxiety was learned after past bad test experiences.
- **Sociocultural:** family and school pressure to succeed.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
Who opened the first psychology laboratory, in 1879?
- [x] Wilhelm Wundt
- [ ] Sigmund Freud
- [ ] B. F. Skinner
- [ ] William James
solution: Wundt's lab in Leipzig is usually taken as the start of psychology as a separate science.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
Which school of thought said psychology should study only behaviour that can be observed and measured?
- [ ] structuralism
- [ ] humanism
- [x] behaviourism
- [ ] psychoanalysis
solution: Behaviourists like Watson and Skinner set aside the unobservable mind and studied responses to the environment.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A psychologist explains depression by looking at levels of brain chemicals. Which perspective is that?
- [x] biological
- [ ] humanistic
- [ ] sociocultural
- [ ] behavioural
solution: Brain chemistry, genes and hormones belong to the biological perspective.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Which perspective focuses on how people think: memory, reasoning, beliefs and problem solving?
- [ ] psychodynamic
- [x] cognitive
- [ ] evolutionary
- [ ] behavioural
solution: Cognitive psychology studies mental processes.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Freud's psychoanalysis emphasised:
- [ ] observable responses to rewards
- [x] unconscious desires and childhood experiences
- [ ] the purpose of each mental process
- [ ] personal growth and free will
solution: Freud believed hidden, unconscious forces drive much of what we do.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Carl Rogers and Abraham Maslow are associated with which approach, which stresses free will and reaching one's potential?
- [ ] behaviourism
- [ ] structuralism
- [x] humanism
- [ ] functionalism
solution: Humanistic psychology arose partly as a reaction to the "mechanical" views of behaviourism and psychoanalysis.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Why is psychology called a science, rather than just common sense?
- [ ] Psychologists all agree with each other.
- [x] It tests ideas with systematic observation and measurement, and changes its views when the evidence says so.
- [ ] It only studies the brain.
solution: Common sense often contradicts itself ("birds of a feather flock together" vs "opposites attract"). Research decides which is true when.
:::
