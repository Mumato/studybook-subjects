title: The brain
---
The brain is about 1.4 kg of tissue that runs everything you are. Different areas specialise, but they work together. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 3)

::: rule
- **Hindbrain:** the **medulla** (breathing, heartbeat), **pons**, and **cerebellum** (balance, coordination).
- **Forebrain** structures: the **thalamus** relays sensory signals; the **hypothalamus** controls hunger, thirst, temperature and hormones; the **amygdala** handles fear and emotion; the **hippocampus** forms new memories.
- The wrinkled **cerebral cortex** has four lobes on each side:
- **frontal:** planning, decisions, personality, movement (motor cortex), speech production (**Broca's area**)
- **parietal:** touch and body sense
- **temporal:** hearing, language understanding (**Wernicke's area**)
- **occipital:** vision
- The two **hemispheres** are linked by the **corpus callosum**. The brain can rewire itself (**plasticity**), especially when young.
:::

::: example
In 1848, an iron rod shot through railway worker **Phineas Gage**'s frontal lobe. He survived and could walk and talk, but went from responsible and polite to impulsive and rude. His case was early evidence that the frontal lobes shape personality and self-control.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
Which lobe is mainly responsible for vision?
![](assets/bio-brain.3x.png)
- [ ] frontal
- [ ] parietal
- [ ] temporal
- [x] occipital
solution: The occipital lobe, at the back of the head, processes what you see.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=text answers="hippocampus;the hippocampus"
Which structure is essential for forming new long-term memories?
solution: The hippocampus. People with damage there can't form new lasting memories, though old ones remain.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A person has trouble keeping their balance and their movements are clumsy. Which structure is most likely damaged?
- [ ] amygdala
- [x] cerebellum
- [ ] thalamus
- [ ] occipital lobe
solution: The cerebellum coordinates movement and balance.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
After a stroke, a man understands speech but can only speak in slow, broken words. The damage is probably in:
- [x] Broca's area, in the frontal lobe
- [ ] Wernicke's area, in the temporal lobe
- [ ] the occipital lobe
solution: Broca's area produces speech. Damage to Wernicke's area instead leaves fluent speech that makes little sense, and poor understanding.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Which structure plays a key role in fear?
- [x] amygdala
- [ ] medulla
- [ ] pons
- [ ] corpus callosum
solution: The amygdala detects threats and triggers fear responses.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Hunger, thirst and body temperature are regulated by the:
- [ ] thalamus
- [x] hypothalamus
- [ ] cerebellum
- [ ] hippocampus
solution: The hypothalamus also controls the pituitary gland.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
A young child has half of her brain removed to stop severe seizures. Years later she walks, talks and does well in school. What does this show?
- [ ] Half the brain is unused.
- [x] Neuroplasticity: especially in childhood, the remaining brain can take over functions of the lost half.
- [ ] Brain areas don't specialise at all.
solution: Areas do specialise, but the young brain can reorganise remarkably. Recovery after injury is usually much harder in adults.
:::
