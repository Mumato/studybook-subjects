title: Prenatal development and how children think
---
Development starts before birth, and a child's thinking isn't just a smaller version of an adult's: it works differently, in stages. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 9)

::: rule
- **Prenatal stages:** germinal (first 2 weeks), embryonic (weeks 3-8, organs form), fetal (week 9 to birth). **Teratogens** are harmful agents such as alcohol, smoking and some drugs; alcohol can cause **fetal alcohol spectrum disorder**.
- Newborns have **reflexes**: **rooting** (turning toward a touch on the cheek), **grasping**, and the **Moro** (startle) reflex.
- **Piaget:** children build **schemas** (mental frameworks). They **assimilate** new things into existing schemas and **accommodate** (change schemas) when things don't fit.
- Piaget's four stages:
- **sensorimotor** (0-2): learning through senses and action; develops **object permanence**
- **preoperational** (2-7): pretend play and language, but **egocentric** and no **conservation**
- **concrete operational** (7-11): logical about concrete things; understands conservation
- **formal operational** (11+): abstract and hypothetical thinking
- **Vygotsky** stressed social learning: the **zone of proximal development** is what a child can do with help but not yet alone.
:::

::: example
Pour juice from a short, wide glass into a tall, thin one in front of a 4-year-old. The child says the tall glass has **more**. They don't yet grasp **conservation**: that the amount stays the same when its shape changes. A 9-year-old knows it's the same.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
A baby searches for a toy after it's hidden under a blanket. The baby has developed:
- [x] object permanence
- [ ] conservation
- [ ] egocentrism
solution: Knowing objects still exist when out of sight appears during the sensorimotor stage.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
A child calls every four-legged animal a "doggy", then learns cats are different and creates a new category. Creating the new category is:
- [ ] assimilation
- [x] accommodation
- [ ] conservation
solution: Changing a schema to fit new information is accommodation. Calling all animals "doggy" was assimilation.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A 4-year-old covers her eyes and says "You can't see me!" This shows:
- [x] egocentrism (preoperational stage)
- [ ] object permanence
- [ ] formal operational thinking
solution: Preoperational children struggle to take others' viewpoints.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="7"
At about what age does Piaget's concrete operational stage begin?
![](assets/dev-piaget.3x.png)
solution: Around age 7, lasting to about 11.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Reasoning about "What if there were no gravity?" first becomes possible in which stage?
- [ ] preoperational
- [ ] concrete operational
- [x] formal operational
solution: Abstract, hypothetical thinking marks the formal operational stage.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Drinking alcohol during pregnancy is dangerous because alcohol is a:
- [x] teratogen that can harm the developing baby
- [ ] reflex
- [ ] schema
solution: It can cause fetal alcohol spectrum disorder, with lasting effects on learning and behaviour.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
A child can't solve a puzzle alone but can with hints from a parent. According to Vygotsky, the puzzle is in the child's:
- [x] zone of proximal development
- [ ] sensorimotor stage
- [ ] object permanence
solution: That's the zone where guided help produces the most learning.
:::
