title: Freud and the psychodynamic view
---
Personality is each person's typical pattern of thinking, feeling and acting. Sigmund Freud offered the first big theory of it, centred on forces we're unaware of. Much of it is now doubted, but parts live on. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 11)

::: rule
- Freud pictured the mind as an iceberg: a small **conscious** part above water, a **preconscious** just below, and a vast **unconscious** of hidden wishes and memories.
- Personality has three parts: the **id** (basic urges, wants pleasure **now**), the **superego** (internalised morals, the conscience) and the **ego** (the realistic mediator between them and the outside world).
- The ego protects itself from anxiety with **defence mechanisms**: **repression** (pushing painful memories out of awareness), **denial**, **projection** (seeing your own unacceptable feelings in others), **displacement** (redirecting feelings to a safer target), **rationalisation** (making excuses), **sublimation** (channelling urges into acceptable activities), **regression** (acting younger).
- **Neo-Freudians** kept the unconscious but changed the focus: **Adler** (feelings of inferiority), **Jung** (a **collective unconscious** of shared archetypes), **Horney** (social and cultural factors, not just sex).
:::

::: example
After a bad day at work, a man shouts at his dog instead of his boss. That's **displacement**: the anger goes to a safer target. If he instead went to the gym and boxed a punching bag, that would be closer to **sublimation**: turning the urge into an acceptable activity.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
In Freud's theory, the part of personality that wants immediate pleasure is the:
- [x] id
- [ ] ego
- [ ] superego
solution: The id runs on the pleasure principle.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
The part that acts as your conscience, making you feel guilty, is the:
- [ ] id
- [ ] ego
- [x] superego
solution: The superego holds internalised rules and ideals.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A student who failed an exam says, "The test was unfair anyway." This defence mechanism is:
- [ ] projection
- [x] rationalisation
- [ ] regression
solution: Making a reasonable-sounding excuse to protect self-esteem.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Someone who dislikes a coworker insists that the coworker dislikes them. This is:
- [x] projection
- [ ] sublimation
- [ ] denial
solution: Their own feeling is attributed to the other person.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
An adult under stress starts sucking their thumb. This is:
- [ ] displacement
- [x] regression
- [ ] repression
solution: Regression is retreating to behaviour from an earlier stage.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=text answers="collective unconscious;the collective unconscious"
Carl Jung proposed that all humans share deep, inherited memories and symbols. What did he call this?
solution: The collective unconscious.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
A main criticism of Freud's theory is that:
- [x] many of its ideas are hard to test scientifically
- [ ] it ignores childhood
- [ ] it's based on large experiments
solution: Concepts like the id can explain almost any behaviour after the fact, which makes them hard to prove wrong.
:::
