id: soc-03
title: Prejudice, attraction and helping
---
How we treat others, with hostility, liking or help, depends heavily on group membership, familiarity and the situation. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 12)

::: rule
- A **stereotype** is a belief about a group; **prejudice** is a negative attitude toward a group; **discrimination** is negative **behaviour** toward its members. **In-group bias:** favouring our own group.
- **Reducing prejudice:** cooperation toward **superordinate goals** (shared goals neither group can reach alone) and equal-status **contact** between groups.
- **Attraction** grows with **proximity** (being near), **similarity** and the **mere-exposure effect** (familiar things become more liked).
- Sternberg's **triangular theory of love:** **intimacy**, **passion** and **commitment**; all three together is "consummate love".
- **Bystander effect:** the more people present, the less likely any one helps, because of **diffusion of responsibility**.
:::

::: example
In the **Robbers Cave** study, boys at a summer camp were split into two groups, and competitions soon led to name-calling and fights. Contact alone didn't fix it. What worked: tasks only both groups together could solve, like pulling a stuck truck. Shared (superordinate) goals reduced the hostility.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
Refusing to hire someone because of their ethnicity is:
- [ ] a stereotype
- [ ] prejudice
- [x] discrimination
solution: Discrimination is the action; prejudice is the attitude; a stereotype is the belief.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
In the Robbers Cave study, what finally reduced hostility between the groups?
- [ ] more competitions
- [ ] simply eating meals together
- [x] working together on goals neither group could reach alone
solution: Superordinate goals.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Students tend to become friends with people who live on their floor of the dorm. This shows the role of:
- [x] proximity
- [ ] social loafing
- [ ] deindividuation
solution: Being near makes contact, and liking, more likely.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
A song you disliked at first grows on you after hearing it many times. This is the:
- [ ] bystander effect
- [x] mere-exposure effect
- [ ] in-group bias
solution: Repeated exposure tends to increase liking.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="3"
How many components make up Sternberg's triangular theory of love?
solution: Three: intimacy, passion and commitment.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Someone collapses on a busy street, and dozens walk past without helping. The best explanation is:
- [ ] everyone in big cities is uncaring
- [x] diffusion of responsibility: each person assumes someone else will help
- [ ] the mere-exposure effect
solution: The bystander effect. If you need help in a crowd, point to one person: "You, call an ambulance."
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=text answers="in-group bias;ingroup bias;in group bias;in-group favouritism;in-group favoritism"
Favouring members of your own group over others is called what?
solution: In-group bias. It appears even when groups are formed randomly, by a coin flip.
:::
