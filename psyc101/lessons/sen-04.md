title: The other senses
---
Vision and hearing get the most attention, but taste, smell, touch, pain and your sense of balance and body position work all the time, mostly without you noticing. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 5)

::: rule
- **Taste** (gustation): taste buds detect five basic tastes: **sweet, salty, sour, bitter** and **umami** (savoury). What we call "flavour" is taste **plus smell**.
- **Smell** (olfaction): receptors high in the nose send signals to the olfactory bulb, which links directly to the **amygdala** and **hippocampus**. That's why smells can bring back vivid memories and emotions.
- **Touch** (somatosensation): skin receptors detect pressure, temperature and pain. Fingertips and lips are the most sensitive.
- **Pain:** pain receptors (nociceptors) signal damage. The **gate-control theory** (Melzack and Wall) says the spinal cord has a "gate" that can block pain signals: other touch signals (like rubbing a bump) and messages from the brain (attention, mood, endorphins) can close it.
- **Body senses:** **kinesthesia** tells you the position and movement of your body parts (receptors in muscles and joints); the **vestibular sense** tells you about balance and head movement (the **semicircular canals** in the inner ear).
:::

::: example
Hold your nose and eat a jelly bean with your eyes closed. You can tell it's sweet, but often not whether it's cherry or lemon. Let go of your nose and the flavour rushes in: most of "taste" is really **smell**.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="5"
How many basic tastes can taste buds detect?
solution: Five: sweet, salty, sour, bitter and umami.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=text answers="umami"
What is the savoury basic taste, found in foods like broth, cheese and mushrooms?
solution: Umami.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
With a blocked nose from a cold, food tastes bland. Why?
- [ ] Colds damage taste buds.
- [x] Much of flavour comes from smell, which the blocked nose cuts off.
- [ ] Taste buds only work when you breathe through your mouth.
solution: Flavour combines taste and smell.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
You bump your elbow and rub it, and the pain eases. The gate-control theory explains this because:
- [x] the rubbing sends touch signals that help close the spinal "gate" to pain signals
- [ ] rubbing repairs the damage
- [ ] rubbing removes the pain receptors
solution: Non-painful touch signals compete with pain signals in the spinal cord.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
The smell of a certain perfume suddenly brings back a vivid childhood memory. Why is smell so tied to memory and emotion?
- [ ] Smell signals are the slowest to arrive.
- [x] Smell pathways connect directly to the amygdala and hippocampus.
- [ ] Smells are stored in the retina.
solution: Smell has unusually direct links to the brain's emotion and memory centres.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Your sense of balance comes mainly from the:
- [ ] cochlea
- [x] semicircular canals of the inner ear (the vestibular sense)
- [ ] taste buds
solution: Fluid moving in the semicircular canals signals head movement and balance.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
With your eyes closed, you can still touch your nose with your finger. Which sense makes this possible?
- [x] kinesthesia
- [ ] the vestibular sense
- [ ] olfaction
solution: Receptors in muscles and joints tell you where your body parts are.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=choice creative=true
Some people who lose an arm keep feeling pain in the missing hand (phantom limb pain). What does this show about pain?
- [ ] The arm's pain receptors are still working.
- [x] Pain is created by the brain, which can produce it even with no signals from the body part.
- [ ] The person is imagining it on purpose.
solution: The brain's map of the body remains, and can generate real pain. That's why pain treatment also works on the brain's side: attention, expectations, mood.
:::
