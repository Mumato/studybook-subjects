title: Therapy and treatment
---
Treatments for psychological disorders work, and they keep improving. They fall into two broad families: talking and learning-based therapies, and biomedical treatments. Many people do best with both. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 16)

::: rule
- **Psychodynamic therapy** (from Freud): uncovering unconscious conflicts, e.g. through **free association**.
- **Humanistic, client-centred therapy** (Rogers): a warm, accepting therapist who listens with **empathy** and **unconditional positive regard**.
- **Behaviour therapy:** changing behaviour through learning. **Exposure therapy** and **systematic desensitisation** (relaxing while facing a feared thing step by step) treat phobias; **aversive conditioning** pairs an unwanted habit with something unpleasant.
- **Cognitive-behavioural therapy (CBT):** spotting and challenging distorted, negative thoughts while changing behaviour. Strong evidence for depression and anxiety.
- **Biomedical:** **antidepressants** such as SSRIs, **antipsychotics** (for schizophrenia, reducing dopamine activity), **lithium** (mood stabiliser for bipolar disorder), and **ECT** for severe depression that hasn't responded to other treatment.
- Across approaches, a good **therapeutic alliance** (trust between client and therapist) strongly predicts success.
:::

::: example
**Systematic desensitisation** for a fear of dogs: first learn to relax deeply; then, while relaxed, imagine a dog far away, then look at photos, watch a dog through a window, stand across the room, and finally pet a calm dog. Each step only once the last feels calm. Fear is replaced by relaxation through classical conditioning.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
A therapist asks a client to say whatever comes to mind, however silly. This technique comes from:
- [x] psychodynamic therapy
- [ ] behaviour therapy
- [ ] CBT
solution: Free association aims to bring unconscious material to light.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
A therapist mostly listens, reflects the client's feelings and accepts them without judging. This is:
- [ ] psychodynamic therapy
- [x] client-centred (humanistic) therapy
- [ ] aversive conditioning
solution: Rogers' approach relies on empathy and unconditional positive regard.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Facing a feared situation in small, graded steps while staying relaxed is:
- [x] systematic desensitisation
- [ ] free association
- [ ] ECT
solution: A behaviour therapy built on classical conditioning.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
A client learns to catch the thought "I'm a total failure" and replace it with "I made a mistake, and I can fix it." This is:
- [ ] psychodynamic therapy
- [x] cognitive-behavioural therapy
- [ ] client-centred therapy
solution: CBT targets distorted thinking patterns.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=text answers="lithium"
Which mood stabiliser has long been used to treat bipolar disorder?
solution: Lithium.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Antipsychotic medications for schizophrenia mainly work by:
- [x] reducing dopamine activity
- [ ] increasing serotonin reuptake
- [ ] increasing GABA
solution: They block dopamine receptors, easing positive symptoms like hallucinations.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
Studies compare many different therapies and often find similar success rates. Which shared factor best explains this?
- [ ] all therapies use medication
- [x] the therapeutic alliance: a trusting, supportive relationship with the therapist, plus hope and a new way to understand the problem
- [ ] therapy works only through placebo
solution: Common factors matter a lot, though specific methods do matter for some problems (exposure for phobias, for example). Finding a therapist you trust is worth the effort.
:::
