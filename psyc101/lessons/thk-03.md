title: Intelligence
---
Intelligence is hard to define and harder to measure, but psychologists have built tests that predict school success well, and argued for a century about what they actually capture. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 7)

::: rule
- Spearman proposed a **general intelligence** factor, **g**, underlying all mental abilities. Gardner proposed **multiple intelligences** (linguistic, musical, spatial, interpersonal and more); Sternberg proposed analytical, creative and practical intelligence.
- **IQ tests** (like the Wechsler scales) are scored so the **mean is 100** and the **standard deviation is 15**, on a normal curve: about 68% score 85-115 and 95% score 70-130.
- A good test must be **standardised** (given the same way, compared with a large group), **reliable** (consistent results) and **valid** (measures what it claims).
- Both **genes** and **environment** matter. The **Flynn effect**: average IQ scores rose over the 20th century, too fast for genes, pointing to environment.
:::

::: example
Maya scores 130 on an IQ test. That's $(130 - 100)/15 = 2$ standard deviations above the mean, higher than about 97.5% of people (95% are within 2 SD, and half of the remaining 5% are above).
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="115"
IQ has a mean of 100 and an SD of 15. What IQ is 1 standard deviation above the mean?
solution: $100 + 15 = 115$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="68"
About what percent of people have an IQ between 85 and 115?
![](assets/thk-iq.3x.png)
solution: That's within 1 SD of the mean: about 68%.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="-2"
How many standard deviations from the mean is an IQ of 70? (Use a minus sign for below the mean.)
solution: $(70 - 100)/15 = -2$.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
A test that gives a person nearly the same score each time they take it is:
- [x] reliable
- [ ] valid
- [ ] standardised
solution: Reliability is consistency. A test can be reliable without being valid.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A bathroom scale always reads exactly 5 kg too heavy. It is:
- [x] reliable but not valid
- [ ] valid but not reliable
- [ ] both reliable and valid
solution: It's consistent (reliable), but it doesn't measure your true weight (not valid).
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Howard Gardner's theory says that:
- [ ] one general factor explains all intelligence
- [x] there are several separate kinds of intelligence, such as musical and interpersonal
- [ ] intelligence is fixed at birth
solution: Gardner's multiple intelligences contrast with Spearman's g.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
Average IQ scores rose by about 3 points per decade during the 20th century (the Flynn effect). What best explains this?
- [ ] Genes changed rapidly.
- [x] Environmental changes such as better nutrition, more schooling and more complex daily life: genes can't change that fast.
- [ ] Tests got easier every year.
solution: Tests are re-standardised, which is how the rise was noticed. The speed of the change points to environment, showing intelligence isn't fixed by genes alone.
:::
