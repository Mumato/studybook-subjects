title: Emotion
---
An emotion is more than a feeling: it's a body reaction, an expression and a thought, all at once. Psychologists have argued for over a century about which comes first. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 10)

::: rule
Emotion has three parts: **physiological arousal**, **expressive behaviour** and **conscious experience**.
- **James-Lange theory:** the body reacts first, and the emotion *is* our awareness of that reaction ("I'm afraid because I tremble").
- **Cannon-Bard theory:** body reaction and emotion happen **at the same time**, independently.
- **Schachter-Singer two-factor theory:** emotion = arousal + a **cognitive label** for it, based on the situation.
- **Facial feedback:** making a facial expression can slightly strengthen the matching feeling.
- Some basic facial expressions (happiness, sadness, fear, anger, surprise, disgust) are recognised across cultures (Ekman). The **amygdala** is key for fear.
:::

::: example
Your heart is pounding as you step off a scary rope bridge, and you meet someone attractive. In a famous study, men interviewed on a high bridge were more likely to call the female interviewer afterward than men on a low, steady bridge. They may have **labelled** their arousal as attraction: the two-factor theory at work.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
"I see a bear, my heart races, and noticing that, I feel afraid." Which theory is this?
- [x] James-Lange
- [ ] Cannon-Bard
- [ ] two-factor
solution: In James-Lange, the body's reaction comes first and creates the emotion.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
Which theory says arousal and the feeling of emotion happen at the same time?
- [ ] James-Lange
- [x] Cannon-Bard
- [ ] two-factor
solution: Cannon and Bard argued the thalamus sends signals to the body and cortex simultaneously.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Which theory says we feel the emotion that matches how we **interpret** our arousal?
- [ ] James-Lange
- [ ] Cannon-Bard
- [x] Schachter-Singer two-factor
solution: The same arousal can be labelled as fear, excitement or attraction depending on the situation.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Holding a pen in your teeth (forcing a smile) makes cartoons seem slightly funnier. This supports:
- [x] the facial feedback hypothesis
- [ ] the Yerkes-Dodson law
- [ ] drive-reduction theory
solution: Expressions can feed back into feelings.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
People from very different cultures recognise a photo of a fearful face. This suggests that some emotional expressions are:
- [x] universal
- [ ] learned only from television
- [ ] unique to each culture
solution: Ekman found basic expressions are recognised worldwide, though cultures differ in when it's acceptable to show them.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=text answers="amygdala;the amygdala"
Which brain structure plays a central role in fear?
solution: The amygdala.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
A polygraph ("lie detector") actually measures:
- [ ] lies directly
- [x] physiological arousal, such as heart rate, breathing and sweating
- [ ] facial expressions
solution: Arousal can come from nervousness, not just lying, so innocent people can "fail" and practised liars can pass. That's why polygraph results usually aren't accepted in court.
:::
