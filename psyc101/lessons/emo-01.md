title: Motivation
---
Why do we do what we do? Motivation is what starts, directs and keeps up our behaviour, from eating a sandwich to training for a marathon. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 10)

::: rule
- **Drive-reduction theory:** needs (like hunger) create tension, a **drive**, that we act to reduce, restoring balance (**homeostasis**).
- **Arousal theory:** we seek an **optimal level** of arousal. The **Yerkes-Dodson law**: performance is best at moderate arousal; too little or too much hurts it (and easy tasks tolerate more arousal than hard ones).
- **Intrinsic** motivation comes from enjoying the activity itself; **extrinsic** motivation from outside rewards. The **overjustification effect**: rewarding something people already enjoy can reduce their intrinsic motivation.
- **Maslow's hierarchy of needs:** physiological, safety, love and belonging, esteem, and at the top **self-actualisation** (reaching one's potential). Lower needs generally press first.
- Hunger is regulated by the **hypothalamus** and hormones: **ghrelin** signals hunger, **leptin** signals fullness.
:::

::: example
Two students practise guitar: one because she loves it (**intrinsic**), one to win a 100-dollar prize (**extrinsic**). If the prize is later cancelled, the second student is far more likely to stop playing.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
You feel thirsty and drink until the thirst is gone. Which theory fits best?
- [x] drive-reduction theory
- [ ] arousal theory
- [ ] the overjustification effect
solution: A need creates a drive; drinking reduces it and restores homeostasis.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
According to the Yerkes-Dodson law, a student will do best on an exam when they feel:
![](assets/emo-yerkes.3x.png)
- [ ] completely relaxed
- [x] moderately alert and a bit nervous
- [ ] extremely anxious
solution: Moderate arousal is optimal. Too little and you're sluggish; too much and anxiety gets in the way.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Reading novels for pleasure is an example of:
- [x] intrinsic motivation
- [ ] extrinsic motivation
- [ ] drive reduction
solution: The activity is rewarding in itself.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
In Maslow's hierarchy, which need sits at the top?
![](assets/emo-maslow.3x.png)
- [ ] esteem
- [ ] safety
- [x] self-actualisation
solution: Self-actualisation, reaching your full potential, comes once lower needs are largely met.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
In Maslow's hierarchy, which need would typically come first for someone who has no home and no food?
- [x] physiological needs, like food and water
- [ ] esteem
- [ ] self-actualisation
solution: The most basic needs press first.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=text answers="ghrelin"
Which hormone, released by an empty stomach, signals hunger?
solution: Ghrelin. Leptin, from fat cells, signals fullness.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
Children who loved drawing were promised a prize for drawing. Later, with no prize offered, they drew less than children who never got a prize. Why?
- [ ] They were tired of drawing.
- [x] The overjustification effect: the reward made drawing feel like work done "for the prize", weakening their own enjoyment.
- [ ] Prizes always increase motivation.
solution: Expected rewards for an already-enjoyed activity can undermine intrinsic motivation. Unexpected praise for effort tends not to.
:::
