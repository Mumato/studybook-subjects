title: Vision and hearing
---
Most of what we know about the world arrives through two senses. Both turn waves (light and sound) into nerve signals, in surprisingly clever ways. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 5)

::: rule
**Vision:** light passes through the **cornea** and **lens**, which focus it on the **retina** at the back of the eye.
- **Rods** work in dim light and see only shades of grey; they're spread around the edges. **Cones** see colour and fine detail; they're packed in the **fovea**, the centre of vision.
- Where the optic nerve leaves the eye there are no receptors: the **blind spot**.
- Colour: three kinds of cones (red, green, blue: the **trichromatic** theory), then opposing pairs in later neurons, red-green and blue-yellow (the **opponent-process** theory, which explains afterimages).

**Hearing:** sound waves vibrate the eardrum and tiny bones, then fluid in the snail-shaped **cochlea**, where **hair cells** fire. **Pitch** comes from frequency, **loudness** from amplitude. People hear roughly 20 to 20 000 Hz.
:::

::: example
Stare at a red square for 30 seconds, then look at a white wall: you see a **green** square. The red-detecting side of the red-green opponent cells got tired, so the green side wins for a moment. That's evidence for the opponent-process theory.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
At night you can see shapes but not colours. Which receptors are doing the work?
- [x] rods
- [ ] cones
- [ ] hair cells
solution: Rods are very sensitive to dim light but don't detect colour.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=text answers="fovea;the fovea"
What is the small central area of the retina, packed with cones, where vision is sharpest?
![](assets/sen-eye.3x.png)
solution: The fovea. You move your eyes so that what you look at lands on it.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Why do we each have a blind spot?
- [ ] The lens can't focus there.
- [x] There are no rods or cones where the optic nerve leaves the eye.
- [ ] The cornea blocks light there.
solution: The brain fills in the gap, so we normally don't notice it.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
After staring at a yellow image, you see a blue afterimage. Which theory explains this best?
- [ ] trichromatic theory
- [x] opponent-process theory
- [ ] place theory
solution: Yellow and blue are an opponent pair: tiring the yellow side lets blue show.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
The **pitch** of a sound depends mainly on its:
- [x] frequency
- [ ] amplitude
- [ ] duration
solution: Higher frequency, higher pitch. Amplitude sets loudness.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=text answers="cochlea;the cochlea"
In which fluid-filled, snail-shaped structure of the inner ear are sound vibrations turned into nerve signals?
solution: The cochlea, lined with hair cells.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=numeric answer="20000"
Young people can hear sounds up to about how many hertz?
solution: About 20,000 Hz, and the upper limit drops with age.
:::
