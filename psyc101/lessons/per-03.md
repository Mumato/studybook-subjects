title: Traits, biology and assessment
---
Instead of explaining *why* people differ, trait theories measure *how* they differ, along a few broad dimensions. These dimensions turn out to be partly inherited and fairly stable. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 11)

::: rule
- The **Big Five** traits ("OCEAN"): **O**penness (curious, imaginative), **C**onscientiousness (organised, dependable), **E**xtraversion (outgoing, energetic), **A**greeableness (kind, cooperative), **N**euroticism (prone to worry and negative emotion). Each is a continuum.
- **Twin studies** suggest that genes explain roughly **40-50%** of the variation in personality traits. **Temperament** (activity level, reactivity) shows from infancy.
- **Personality tests:** **self-report inventories** like the **MMPI** ask many true/false questions and are scored objectively. **Projective tests** like the **Rorschach** inkblots and the **TAT** (making up stories about pictures) assume people reveal hidden feelings in how they interpret ambiguous images; they're much less reliable and valid.
- Cultures differ: **individualist** cultures value independence; **collectivist** cultures value the group.
:::

::: example
Identical twins raised apart (as in the Minnesota twin studies) are often strikingly similar in personality, more than fraternal twins raised together. That points to a real genetic influence, but since identical twins aren't identical in personality, environment matters a lot too.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
Someone who is organised, careful and always on time scores high on:
- [ ] openness
- [x] conscientiousness
- [ ] extraversion
- [ ] neuroticism
solution: Conscientiousness predicts success at school and work.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
A person who loves parties, talks to everyone and gets energy from crowds is high in:
- [x] extraversion
- [ ] agreeableness
- [ ] neuroticism
solution: Extraverts are sociable and energised by others.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="5"
How many broad traits are in the Big Five model?
solution: Five: openness, conscientiousness, extraversion, agreeableness, neuroticism.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Someone who worries a lot and is easily upset scores high on:
- [ ] openness
- [ ] agreeableness
- [x] neuroticism
solution: Neuroticism is the tendency toward negative emotions.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Asking someone what they see in an inkblot is an example of a:
- [ ] self-report inventory
- [x] projective test
- [ ] intelligence test
solution: The Rorschach is a projective test; its scoring is criticised as unreliable.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Twin studies suggest that genes account for about what share of the differences in personality traits?
- [ ] almost none
- [x] roughly 40-50%
- [ ] nearly 100%
solution: A substantial but partial genetic influence.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
A website offers a free "personality test" and tells you that you're "sometimes outgoing but sometimes need time alone, and have untapped potential". Most people find it very accurate. Why should you be sceptical?
- [ ] The website is too short.
- [x] The statements are vague enough to fit almost anyone (the Barnum effect), so feeling it's accurate proves nothing.
- [ ] Personality can't be measured at all.
solution: Valid tests like Big Five inventories are tested for reliability and validity; vague horoscope-style descriptions aren't.
:::
