id: sen-01
title: Sensation: thresholds and adaptation
---
**Sensation** is your sense organs picking up energy from the world; **perception** is your brain making sense of it. This lesson is about the first step: how much stimulation it takes to notice something. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 5)

::: rule
- **Transduction:** sense receptors turn physical energy (light, sound waves, pressure) into neural signals.
- **Absolute threshold:** the weakest stimulus you can detect half the time.
- **Difference threshold** (just noticeable difference, JND): the smallest change you can detect.
- **Weber's law:** the JND is a constant **proportion** of the original stimulus, not a constant amount. Adding 1 candle to 10 is easy to notice; adding 1 to 100 isn't.
- **Sensory adaptation:** an unchanging stimulus fades from notice, like the feel of your clothes or a fridge's hum.
- **Bottom-up** processing builds from raw sensory input; **top-down** processing uses expectations and knowledge.
:::

::: example
Suppose the JND for weight is 2%. Holding 100 g, you'd need 2 g more to feel a difference. Holding 1000 g, you'd need 20 g more: the same proportion, ten times the amount.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="10"
The JND for a weight is 2% of the weight. You hold 500 g. How many grams must be added before you notice a difference?
solution: $2\% \times 500 = 10$ g.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="30"
A room is lit by 60 candles, and 3 more candles are just barely noticeable. By Weber's law, how many must be added to a room lit by 600 candles to be just noticeable?
solution: $3/60 = 5\%$. And $5\% \times 600 = 30$ candles.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
After a few minutes in a bakery, you no longer notice the smell. This is:
- [ ] the absolute threshold
- [x] sensory adaptation
- [ ] top-down processing
- [ ] transduction
solution: Receptors respond less to a constant stimulus, freeing attention for changes.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
The faintest sound you can hear half the time is your:
- [x] absolute threshold
- [ ] difference threshold
- [ ] just noticeable difference
solution: The absolute threshold is about detecting something at all.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=text answers="transduction"
What is the process of converting light, sound or pressure into neural signals called?
solution: Transduction happens in the sense receptors: rods and cones in the eye, hair cells in the ear.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
You read a messy handwritten note easily because you know what your friend probably wrote. This is:
- [ ] bottom-up processing
- [x] top-down processing
- [ ] sensory adaptation
solution: Your knowledge and expectations fill in what the raw input leaves unclear.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Sensation is to perception as:
- [x] detecting is to interpreting
- [ ] seeing is to hearing
- [ ] the brain is to the eyes
solution: Sensation detects the input; perception organises and interprets it.
:::
