title: Encoding and retrieval
---
Why do you remember a song from years ago but not what you read last night? How well something is remembered depends on how it went in and what helps bring it back out. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 8)

::: rule
- **Levels of processing:** thinking about **meaning** (deep, semantic processing) makes stronger memories than noticing how something looks or sounds (shallow processing). Relating it to yourself (the **self-reference effect**) works even better.
- The **spacing effect:** study spread over several sessions beats cramming the same total time.
- Three ways to measure retrieval: **recall** (fill in the blank), **recognition** (pick from options: easier) and **relearning** (learning again goes faster: the "savings").
- **Retrieval cues** help: memory is better in the same **context** (place) or **state** (mood) as when you learned.
- **Serial position effect:** in a list, we remember the first items (**primacy**: more rehearsal) and the last items (**recency**: still in short-term memory) best.
:::

::: example
It took you 40 minutes to learn a list last month. Today you relearn it in 10 minutes. The **savings** are
$$\frac{40 - 10}{40} = 75\%$$
Even when you can't recall something, it may still be stored.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="60"
Learning a poem took 50 minutes. A year later, relearning it takes 20 minutes. What are the savings, in percent?
solution: $(50 - 20)/50 = 30/50 = 60\%$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
Which way of studying a list of words leads to the best memory?
- [ ] counting the letters in each word
- [ ] noticing which words rhyme
- [x] thinking about what each word means and how it relates to your life
solution: Deep, meaningful (and self-referential) processing makes the strongest memories.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A multiple-choice question tests:
- [ ] recall
- [x] recognition
- [ ] relearning
solution: You recognise the right answer among options. Fill-in-the-blank tests recall.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Six hours of study will help most if you:
- [ ] do it all the night before the exam
- [x] spread it over several days
- [ ] do it while listening to music
solution: The spacing effect: distributed practice beats cramming.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
In a list of 15 words, which are you least likely to remember?
![](assets/mem-serial.3x.png)
- [ ] the first few
- [x] the ones in the middle
- [ ] the last few
solution: The serial position curve dips in the middle: those items get neither primacy nor recency help.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Divers learned words either underwater or on land. They remembered best when tested:
- [x] in the same place they learned
- [ ] on land, whatever they learned
- [ ] underwater, whatever they learned
solution: Context-dependent memory: the surroundings act as retrieval cues.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
You remember the first items on a grocery list because you rehearsed them most. This is the:
- [x] primacy effect
- [ ] recency effect
- [ ] spacing effect
solution: Early items get more rehearsal, so they reach long-term memory.
:::
