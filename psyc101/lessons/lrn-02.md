title: Operant conditioning
---
Classical conditioning links stimuli. **Operant conditioning** links behaviour to its consequences: we repeat what pays off and avoid what costs us. B. F. Skinner studied it with rats and pigeons pressing levers. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 6)

::: rule
- **Reinforcement** makes a behaviour **more** likely; **punishment** makes it **less** likely.
- **Positive** means something is **added**; **negative** means something is **taken away**. (Not "good" and "bad".)
- **Shaping:** reinforcing small steps toward a target behaviour.
- **Schedules:** **continuous** reinforcement (every time) gives fast learning; **partial** reinforcement gives behaviour that lasts. **Fixed-ratio** (every *n*th response), **variable-ratio** (after an unpredictable number, like a slot machine: the hardest to extinguish), **fixed-interval** (first response after a set time), **variable-interval** (after unpredictable times).
:::

::: example
Your car beeps until you fasten your seat belt. Fastening it **removes** the annoying beep, so you buckle up faster next time. That's **negative reinforcement**: behaviour goes up because something unpleasant is taken away. It's not punishment!
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
A child gets a sticker for each page of homework, and does more homework. This is:
- [x] positive reinforcement
- [ ] negative reinforcement
- [ ] positive punishment
- [ ] negative punishment
solution: Something pleasant is added and the behaviour increases.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
You take a painkiller and your headache goes away, so you take one again next time. This is:
- [ ] positive reinforcement
- [x] negative reinforcement
- [ ] negative punishment
solution: The behaviour (taking the pill) increases because something unpleasant (the pain) is removed.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A teen comes home late and loses their phone for a week. This is:
- [ ] positive punishment
- [x] negative punishment
- [ ] negative reinforcement
solution: Something pleasant is taken away to make the behaviour less likely.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
A dog jumps on the sofa and gets a squirt of water, so it stops jumping up. This is:
- [x] positive punishment
- [ ] negative punishment
- [ ] negative reinforcement
solution: Something unpleasant is added to reduce the behaviour.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Slot machines pay out after an unpredictable number of plays. This schedule is:
- [ ] fixed-ratio
- [x] variable-ratio
- [ ] fixed-interval
- [ ] variable-interval
solution: Variable-ratio schedules produce high, steady responding that's very hard to extinguish, which is why gambling is so gripping.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="6"
A factory worker is paid for every 5 shirts sewn (fixed-ratio 5). How many payments does she earn for sewing 32 shirts?
solution: $32 / 5 = 6.4$, so 6 complete sets of 5: 6 payments (the last 2 shirts don't earn one yet).
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
To teach a dog to roll over, a trainer first rewards lying down, then lying on its side, then rolling partway, then a full roll. This is:
- [x] shaping
- [ ] extinction
- [ ] generalisation
solution: Shaping reinforces closer and closer approximations of the goal.
:::
