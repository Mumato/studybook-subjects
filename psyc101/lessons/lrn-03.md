id: lrn-03
title: Observational and cognitive learning
---
You didn't learn to tie your shoes by trial and error alone: you watched someone. Much of human learning happens by observation, and some learning stays hidden until it's needed. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 6)

::: rule
- **Observational learning** (modelling): learning by watching others. Albert **Bandura** showed it needs **attention**, **retention** (remembering), **reproduction** (being able to do it) and **motivation**.
- **Vicarious** reinforcement or punishment: seeing a model rewarded or punished changes how likely we are to copy them.
- **Latent learning:** learning that happens without reward and only shows when there's a reason to use it (Tolman's rats learned a maze while exploring, then ran it fast once food was added).
- **Insight learning:** a sudden "aha" solution, not gradual trial and error (Köhler's chimpanzees stacking boxes to reach bananas).
:::

::: example
In Bandura's **Bobo doll** study (1961), children watched an adult punch and kick an inflatable doll. Later, alone with the doll, they copied the attack, even using the same words. Children who watched a calm adult played calmly. When the adult was shown being **punished**, children copied less, yet when offered a reward to imitate, they showed they had learned it all.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
Children copying an adult who hit a doll is an example of:
- [ ] classical conditioning
- [x] observational learning
- [ ] insight learning
solution: They learned the behaviour by watching a model.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
In Bandura's studies, children who saw the model punished were less likely to copy, but could copy perfectly when offered a reward. This shows:
- [x] learning can happen without being performed
- [ ] punishment erases learning
- [ ] children can't remember what they watched
solution: The learning was there all along; motivation decided whether it showed. This is the difference between learning and performance.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A new employee sees a coworker praised for staying late, and starts staying late too. This is:
- [ ] negative reinforcement
- [x] vicarious reinforcement
- [ ] shaping
solution: Seeing someone else rewarded makes the behaviour more likely in the observer.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Rats that explored a maze with no reward ran it as fast as well-trained rats once food was placed at the end. This shows:
- [x] latent learning
- [ ] insight learning
- [ ] extinction
solution: They had built a "cognitive map" all along; the reward just gave them a reason to use it.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
A chimp, after sitting quietly, suddenly stacks boxes to reach bananas hanging out of reach. This is:
- [ ] trial-and-error learning
- [x] insight learning
- [ ] classical conditioning
solution: The solution appeared all at once, not bit by bit.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=text answers="bandura;albert bandura"
Which psychologist ran the Bobo doll studies?
solution: Albert Bandura.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
A parent spanks their child for hitting a sibling, saying "Don't hit!" Based on observational learning, what might the child learn?
- [ ] That hitting is always wrong.
- [x] That hitting is a way bigger people deal with problems: the parent is modelling the very behaviour they're punishing.
- [ ] Nothing, since punishment blocks all learning.
solution: Research links physical punishment with more aggression in children. Modelling calm problem-solving teaches the lesson better.
:::
