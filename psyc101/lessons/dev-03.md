id: dev-03
title: Adolescence, adulthood and aging
---
Development doesn't stop at 18. The teenage brain is still under construction, adults keep changing, and aging brings losses but also real gains. (OpenStax Psychology 2e ch. 9)

::: rule
- **Adolescence** begins with **puberty**. The brain's reward system matures early, but the **prefrontal cortex** (planning, impulse control) isn't fully mature until about the **mid-20s**, which helps explain teen risk-taking, especially with peers around.
- **Emerging adulthood** (about 18-25) is a period of exploring identity, work and relationships before settling into adult roles.
- With age, **fluid intelligence** (quick reasoning with new problems) slowly declines, while **crystallised intelligence** (knowledge and vocabulary) stays steady or grows.
- **Dementia** (such as Alzheimer's disease) is a disease, not a normal part of aging.
- Facing death: Kübler-Ross described stages of grief (denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance), though people don't all go through them in order.
:::

::: example
A 70-year-old may be slower than a 25-year-old at a new video game (fluid intelligence) but beat them easily at a crossword (crystallised intelligence). Many older adults also report higher well-being than younger adults: they focus on what matters most to them.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
Which part of the brain is the last to mature, around the mid-20s?
- [ ] the occipital lobe
- [x] the prefrontal cortex
- [ ] the medulla
solution: The prefrontal cortex supports planning, judgment and impulse control.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
Teens take more risks with friends watching than alone. A brain-based explanation is:
- [x] a reward system that matures early, combined with impulse control that matures later
- [ ] a fully mature prefrontal cortex
- [ ] lower intelligence
solution: Peers boost the reward signal, and the "brakes" aren't fully developed yet.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Vocabulary and general knowledge, which often grow with age, are:
- [ ] fluid intelligence
- [x] crystallised intelligence
- [ ] working memory
solution: Crystallised intelligence is accumulated knowledge.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Solving brand-new puzzles quickly, which tends to decline with age, relies on:
- [x] fluid intelligence
- [ ] crystallised intelligence
- [ ] semantic memory
solution: Fluid intelligence is reasoning with unfamiliar problems.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Severe memory loss and confusion in old age is:
- [ ] a normal part of aging that happens to everyone
- [x] a sign of disease, such as Alzheimer's
- [ ] a sign of crystallised intelligence
solution: Mild slowing is normal; dementia is not.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="25"
The prefrontal cortex is generally not fully mature until about what age?
solution: About 25.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice creative=true
Some countries raise the driving age or limit how many teen passengers new drivers may carry. How does developmental psychology support the passenger limit?
- [ ] Teens have slower reflexes.
- [x] Peers boost teens' reward-seeking while impulse control is still maturing, so friends in the car raise the risk of crashes.
- [ ] Teens can't learn to drive.
solution: Crash data match the lab findings: a teen driver's risk rises with each teen passenger.
:::
