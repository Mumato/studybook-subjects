---
id: plc-01
title: There is: あります, います and where things are
---
Japanese has two verbs for "there is": one for things that can't move by themselves, and one for people and animals. Add position words and you can say where anything is.

::: rule
- **あります** for things, plants and places: !say[ほんが あります] *There's a book.*
- **います** for people and animals: !say[ねこが います] *There's a cat.*
- Negative: !say[ありません] and !say[いません].
- **Position words** use **noun の position**: !say[つくえの うえ] (on the desk), !say[いすの した] (under the chair), !say[かばんの なか] (in the bag), !say[えきの まえ] (in front of the station), !say[ぎんこうの うしろ] (behind the bank), !say[がっこうの となり] (next to the school), !say[うちの ちかく] (near home), !say[みぎ] (right), !say[ひだり] (left), !say[そと] (outside).
- **Saying what's there**: place **に** thing **が** あります: !say[つくえの うえに ほんが あります。] *There's a book on the desk.*
- **Saying where something is**: thing **は** place **に** あります, or just **です**: !say[ねこは いすの したに います。] / !say[ねこは いすの した です。] *The cat is under the chair.*
:::

::: example
Asking the way:

- A: !say[すみません、ゆうびんきょくは どこですか。] *Excuse me, where's the post office?*
- B: !say[m|ぎんこうの となりに ありますよ。えきの まえです。] *It's next to the bank. In front of the station.*
- A: !say[ありがとうございます。この ちかくに トイレは ありますか。] *Thank you. Is there a toilet near here?*
- B: !say[m|いいえ、ありません。] *No, there isn't.*
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
Fill in: こうえんに こどもが ＿。 (There are children in the park.)
- [ ] あります
- [x] います
- [ ] です
solution: Children are people: います.
Hear it: !say[こうえんに こどもが います。]
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
Fill in: へやに テレビが ＿。 (There's a TV in the room.)
- [x] あります
- [ ] います
- [ ] ありますか
solution: A TV is a thing: あります.
Hear it: !say[へやに テレビが あります。]
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=text answers="つくえのうえ"
Type "on the desk". (desk = つくえ)
hint: tsukue no ue
solution: つくえの うえ: noun + の + position.
Hear it: !say[つくえの うえ]
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
かばんの なかに なにが ありますか means:
- [x] What's in the bag?
- [ ] Where is the bag?
- [ ] Is there a bag inside?
solution: かばんの なか (inside the bag) に なにが ありますか (what is there?).
Hear it: !say[かばんの なかに なにが ありますか。]
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=text answers="いません"
Your friend asks if there's a dog at your house. There isn't. Type "There isn't (one)."
hint: Dogs are animals: imasen.
solution: いません: the negative of います.
Hear it: !say[いません]
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Which means "The bank is next to the station"?
- [x] ぎんこうは えきの となりに あります。
- [ ] えきは ぎんこうの となりに あります。
- [ ] ぎんこうの えきは となりです。
solution: thing は place に あります: ぎんこう は えきの となり に あります.
Hear it: !say[ぎんこうは えきの となりに あります。]
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Listen: !listen[m|ねこは つくえの したに います。]
Where is the cat?
- [ ] on the desk
- [x] under the desk
- [ ] next to the desk
solution: つくえの した: under the desk.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=choice creative=true
Japanese uses いる for a taxi with a driver waiting outside (タクシーが いる) but ある for a parked, empty one. What does this show about the rule?
- [x] It's about whether the thing moves by itself, as the speaker sees it, not strictly about being alive.
- [ ] Taxis are counted as people in Japanese.
- [ ] It's a mistake native speakers make.
solution: いる is used for things that act on their own: people, animals, and even a waiting vehicle seen as "someone". Plants don't move, so they take ある.
:::
