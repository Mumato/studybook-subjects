id: kan-02
title: Kanji: days of the week and time
---
The days of the week are named after the sun, moon and five elements, and each is a kanji you'll see everywhere: on calendars, timetables and shop opening hours.

::: rule
- !say[にちようび] **日**曜日 Sunday (sun), !say[げつようび] **月**曜日 Monday (moon), !say[かようび] **火**曜日 Tuesday (fire), !say[すいようび] **水**曜日 Wednesday (water), !say[もくようび] **木**曜日 Thursday (tree), !say[きんようび] **金**曜日 Friday (gold, money), !say[どようび] **土**曜日 Saturday (earth).
- 曜日 (ようび) means "day of the week". 何曜日 (なんようび) asks "which day?"
- A kanji's reading depends on the word: 日 is **にち** in 日曜日, but **ひ** on its own (day, sun) and **に** in 日本.
- Time: **時** じ (o'clock), **分** ふん/ぷん (minute), **半** はん (half), **今** いま (now): {今|いま} {三時半|さんじはん} です。
- 年 (ねん) is "year": {二千二十六年|にせんにじゅうろくねん}.
:::

::: example
A shop sign:

**{月曜日|げつようび}～{金曜日|きんようび}　{十時|じゅうじ}～{七時|しちじ}**
**{土曜日|どようび}・{日曜日|にちようび}　お{休|やす}み**

*Monday to Friday, 10:00 to 7:00. Closed Saturday and Sunday.*

!say[げつようびから きんようび、じゅうじから しちじ。どようびと にちようびは おやすみです。]
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=choice creative=true
The five weekday kanji 火 水 木 金 土 are also the names of planets in Japanese (火星 is Mars). Why might the days be named this way?
- [x] The week came from the same ancient system as English: sun, moon and the five visible planets, which in East Asia were named after the five elements.
- [ ] They're named after Japanese festivals.
- [ ] Each day had its own weather.
solution: Like Sunday and Monday in English, the Japanese week follows the sun, moon and five planets. In East Asia those planets were named after the five elements: fire (Mars), water (Mercury), wood (Jupiter), metal (Venus) and earth (Saturn).
:::
