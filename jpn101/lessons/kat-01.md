---
id: kat-01
title: Katakana: the first half (A to N rows)
---
**Katakana** is the second Japanese script. It spells the same sounds as hiragana, but it's used for **loanwords** from other languages (コーヒー, coffee), foreign names, and for emphasis, a bit like italics. The shapes are more angular, and many come from parts of kanji.

::: rule
- The vowels: !say[ア] **a**, !say[イ] **i**, !say[ウ] **u**, !say[エ] **e**, !say[オ] **o**.
- The K row: !say[カ] **ka**, !say[キ] **ki**, !say[ク] **ku**, !say[ケ] **ke**, !say[コ] **ko**.
- The S row: !say[サ] **sa**, !say[シ] **shi**, !say[ス] **su**, !say[セ] **se**, !say[ソ] **so**.
- The T row: !say[タ] **ta**, !say[チ] **chi**, !say[ツ] **tsu**, !say[テ] **te**, !say[ト] **to**.
- The N row: !say[ナ] **na**, !say[ニ] **ni**, !say[ヌ] **nu**, !say[ネ] **ne**, !say[ノ] **no**.
- Watch the look-alikes. **シ shi** and **ツ tsu**: シ's dots sit on the left and its last stroke sweeps **up** from the bottom; ツ's dots are on top and its stroke sweeps **down**. **ソ so** and **ン n** work the same way: ソ's long stroke falls from the top, ン's rises from the bottom.
- Some match their hiragana: カ/か, キ/き, セ/せ, ニ/に, ヘ/へ.
:::

::: example
Words you can already read:

- !say[ケーキ] *keeki* (cake). The bar ー makes the vowel before it long: in katakana, long vowels are written with ー.
- !say[タクシー] *takushii* (taxi)
- !say[テニス] *tenisu* (tennis)
- !say[アイス] *aisu* (ice cream)

Japanese has no consonant clusters and few word-final consonants, so English words gain vowels: "taxi" becomes ta-ku-shi-i.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=choice creative=true
Why might Japanese use a separate script for loanwords instead of writing them in hiragana?
- [x] It shows at a glance that a word is foreign or special, the way italics or quotes do in English, which helps reading text without spaces.
- [ ] Hiragana can't spell the sounds in foreign words.
- [ ] Katakana is easier to write, so it's used for long words.
solution: Hiragana could spell most loanwords, but katakana marks them out. Japanese is written without spaces, so switching scripts also shows where words start and end.
:::
