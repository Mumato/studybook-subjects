id: kan-05
title: Kanji: everyday verbs
---
Verbs are written with a kanji for the meaning and kana (okurigana) for the ending: 食べます is た(べます), with 食 carrying "eat". Knowing eight verb kanji lets you read most of the sentences in this course the way Japanese people write them.

::: rule
- !say[いきます] **行**きます go, !say[きます] **来**ます come, !say[みます] **見**ます see, watch.
- !say[たべます] **食**べます eat, !say[のみます] **飲**みます drink: both share the "food" shape 食 (飲 has it on the left).
- !say[やすみます] **休**みます rest: a person 亻 leaning on a tree 木.
- !say[よみます] **読**みます read, !say[ききます] **聞**きます listen, hear: 聞 is an ear 耳 at a gate 門.
- The kanji keeps its meaning when the ending changes: 行きました, 食べたいです, 見ません.
- 来 is read き in 来ます but く in the dictionary form 来る, and ら in 来週 (らいしゅう, next week).
:::

::: example
A diary entry:

{日曜日|にちようび}に {友|とも}だちと {映画|えいが}を {見|み}ました。それから {喫茶店|きっさてん}で コーヒーを {飲|の}みました。{来週|らいしゅう}は {京都|きょうと}へ {行|い}きたいです。

!say[にちようびに ともだちと えいがを みました。それから きっさてんで コーヒーを のみました。らいしゅうは きょうとへ いきたいです。]

*On Sunday I saw a movie with a friend. Then I had coffee at a café. Next week I want to go to Kyoto.*
:::

solution: 食 is "eat, food": 食べます (たべます).
Hear it: !say[たべます]
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice

- [x] いきたいです

- [ ] こうきたいです
solution: いきたいです, "I want to go". (ゆく is an old-fashioned reading.)
Hear it: !say[いきたいです]
:::

- [ ] 見ました
- [x] 読みました

solution: よみました: 読みました, read.
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=choice creative=true
休 is a person 亻 next to a tree 木: "rest". Why might learning kanji as pictures and parts help, even when the story isn't historically exact?
- [x] Breaking a kanji into familiar parts and a story makes it much easier to remember than memorizing strokes, and the same parts come back in hundreds of kanji.
- [ ] Every kanji is a perfect picture of its meaning.
- [ ] It doesn't help; kanji must be memorized stroke by stroke.
solution: Most kanji combine a few hundred recurring parts (亻 person, 氵 water, 言 words). Learning the parts turns new kanji into combinations of old ones. Memory stories are a tool even when the real history differs.
:::
