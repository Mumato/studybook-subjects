id: gre-03
title: Speaking practice: shadowing
---
You learn to speak a language by speaking it. **Shadowing** is a technique language teachers use: listen to a native speaker, copy them as closely as you can, then compare. Your microphone does the comparing for you.

::: rule
How each practice item works:
- Click **▶** to hear the model. Listen two or three times.
- Click **● Record**, say the phrase, and click **■ Stop** (it also stops by itself after 15 seconds).
- Click **▶ Me** to hear yourself, then the model again. Compare, and record again as often as you like.
- Click **✔ Check** and the book listens to your recording and tells you whether it heard the right words. It checks the words, not your accent, so still compare with the model for rhythm. (The first time, it needs a one-time download.)
- When you're happy, open the solution and mark yourself.

What to listen for: the **rhythm** (each kana takes about the same time), **long vowels** (held twice as long), the short pause of a small っ, and は read **wa** as a particle.

Your recordings stay on your computer and are deleted when you close the game; the check runs on your computer too. On a Mac, Minecraft may ask for microphone access the first time: allow it (or turn it on in System Settings, Privacy and Security, Microphone).
:::

::: example
Try it with !say[こんにちは]

!speak[こんにちは]

Say it in five even beats: ko-n-ni-chi-wa. The ん is a full beat of its own, so "kon-ni" is longer than English "connie".
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=open
Say "good morning" politely, like the model.
!say[おはようございます]
!speak[おはようございます]
solution: o-ha-yo-u go-za-i-ma-su: the "you" in ohayou is a long o, so hold it. The final "su" is often almost silent: *gozaimas*.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=open
Say "thank you" politely.
!say[ありがとうございます]
!speak[ありがとうございます]
solution: a-ri-ga-to-u go-za-i-ma-su. Hold the long o in "tou", and keep the tap "r" in "ri" light.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=open
Say "excuse me / I'm sorry".
!say[すみません]
!speak[すみません]
solution: su-mi-ma-se-n: five beats, with ん as the last full beat. The first "u" is very light.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=open
Say what you say before eating.
!say[いただきます]
!speak[いただきます]
solution: i-ta-da-ki-ma-su, with an almost silent final "su": *itadakimas*.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=open
Say "good evening".
!say[こんばんは]
!speak[こんばんは]
solution: ko-n-ba-n-wa: five beats, both ん sounds held for a full beat, and the final は read "wa".
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=open
Shadow the whole self-introduction. Pause where the model pauses.
!say[はじめまして。わたしは たなか です。にほんじん です。だいがくの いちねんせい です。よろしく おねがいします。]
**はじめまして。わたしは たなか です。にほんじん です。だいがくの いちねんせい です。よろしく おねがいします。**
!speak[はじめまして。わたしは たなか です。にほんじん です。だいがくの いちねんせい です。よろしく おねがいします。]
solution: Listen for the rhythm: each sentence ends with です, a falling, almost silent "desu". If 15 seconds runs out, record it in two halves.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=open
Now introduce **yourself**: your name, then a job or school word you know (がくせい, せんせい), then the closing phrase. Use the model as a pattern.
!speak[my introduction]
solution: For example: はじめまして。わたしは （your name） です。がくせい です。よろしく おねがいします。 Compare your rhythm with the model in the previous item.
:::
