---
id: vrb-01
title: Verbs: the polite ます form
---
Japanese verbs come at the **end** of the sentence, and the polite form you use with people you don't know well ends in **ます**. The same form covers the present and the future.

::: rule
- Polite verbs end in **ます**: !say[たべます] (eat / will eat), !say[のみます] (drink), !say[いきます] (go), !say[きます] (come), !say[かえります] (go home), !say[みます] (see, watch), !say[よみます] (read), !say[ききます] (listen), !say[します] (do), !say[べんきょうします] (study), !say[おきます] (get up), !say[ねます] (sleep).
- **Negative**: change ます to **ません**: !say[たべません] (don't eat / won't eat).
- There's no separate future tense: !say[あした いきます] "I will go tomorrow"; !say[まいにち いきます] "I go every day".
- Japanese drops the subject when it's obvious: いきます alone can mean "I'll go".
- The verb goes **last**: the object comes before it. "I eat sushi" is すしを たべます (the を comes in the next lesson).
- Japanese verbs fall into **three groups** (ru-verbs like たべる, u-verbs like のむ, and two irregulars する and くる). The ます form is formed differently for each; for now, learn each verb in its ます form.
:::

::: example
Talking about your day:

- A: !say[あした なにを しますか。] *What will you do tomorrow?*
- B: !say[m|としょかんで べんきょうします。それから うちに かえります。] *I'll study at the library. After that I'll go home.*
- A: !say[テレビを みますか。] *Do you watch TV?*
- B: !say[m|いいえ、あまり みません。] *No, not much.* (あまり + negative = "not much")
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=text answers="たべます"
Type the polite form of "eat".
hint: tabemasu
solution: たべます (tabemasu).
Hear it: !say[たべます]
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=text answers="のみません"
Type "I don't drink."
hint: Change ます to ません.
solution: のみません (nomimasen).
Hear it: !say[のみません]
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
What does あした いきます mean?
- [ ] I went yesterday.
- [x] I will go tomorrow.
- [ ] I don't go tomorrow.
solution: あした (tomorrow) + いきます: the ます form covers the future.
Hear it: !say[あした いきます]
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Which means "I don't watch"?
- [ ] みます
- [x] みません
- [ ] みましょう
solution: ません makes the negative: みません.
Hear it: !say[みません]
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=text answers="べんきょうします"
Type "I study."
hint: benkyou shimasu
solution: べんきょうします: べんきょう (study) + します (do).
Hear it: !say[べんきょうします]
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Where does the verb go in a Japanese sentence?
- [ ] first
- [ ] after the subject
- [x] at the end
solution: Japanese is subject–object–verb: the verb comes last.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=choice
Listen: !listen[m|まいにち ろくじに おきます。]
What does the speaker do every day at 6?
- [x] get up
- [ ] go to sleep
- [ ] go home
solution: おきます is "get up": "I get up at 6 every day."
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=choice creative=true
English needs "I", "you" or "she" in almost every sentence. Why can Japanese say just いきます?
- [x] Japanese leaves out anything clear from context; the listener fills it in, and the polite ending already shows the speaker's attitude.
- [ ] The ます ending means "I".
- [ ] Japanese verbs change for each person (I go, she goes).
solution: Japanese verbs don't change for person at all. Subjects are dropped whenever the context makes them clear, and repeating わたしは can even sound self-centred.
:::
