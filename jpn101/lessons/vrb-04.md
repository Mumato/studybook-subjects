---
id: vrb-04
title: Invitations: ませんか and ましょう
---
"Won't you...?" is the polite way to invite someone in Japanese. You'll also learn how to accept, suggest, and turn an invitation down without ever saying "no".

::: rule
- **Inviting**: ます → **ませんか**, "won't you...?": !say[いっしょに ひるごはんを たべませんか。] *Would you like to have lunch together?*
- **Accepting / suggesting**: ます → **ましょう**, "let's": !say[ええ、たべましょう。] *Sure, let's eat.*
- **ましょうか** offers or asks: !say[なんじに あいましょうか。] *What time shall we meet?*
- **Declining politely**: don't say いいえ. Say "that day is a bit..." and let it trail off: !say[すみません、どようびは ちょっと…] *Sorry, Saturday is a bit...*
- いっしょに means "together"; に after a time and で after a place work as before.
:::

::: example
- A: !say[あした えいがを みませんか。] *Would you like to see a movie tomorrow?*
- B: !say[m|いいですね。みましょう。なんじに あいましょうか。] *Sounds good. Let's. What time shall we meet?*
- A: !say[ろくじに えきで あいましょう。] *Let's meet at the station at 6.*
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=text answers="のみませんか"
Type "Won't you drink (with me)?" from のみます.
hint: nomimasen ka
solution: のみませんか: ます → ませんか.
Hear it: !say[のみませんか]
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=text answers="いきましょう"
Type "Let's go."
hint: ikimashou
solution: いきましょう (ikimashou): ます → ましょう.
Hear it: !say[いきましょう]
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Your friend asks コーヒーを のみませんか。 What does it mean?
- [ ] You don't drink coffee?
- [x] Would you like to have coffee (with me)?
- [ ] I don't drink coffee.
solution: ませんか is a polite invitation, even though it looks negative.
Hear it: !say[コーヒーを のみませんか。]
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
You can't go on Sunday. What's the most natural reply?
- [ ] いいえ、いきません。
- [x] すみません、にちようびは ちょっと…
- [ ] いきましょう。
solution: Japanese declines indirectly: "Sunday is a bit..." A flat いいえ、いきません sounds blunt.
Hear it: !say[すみません、にちようびは ちょっと…]
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Fill in: なんじに ＿ (What time shall we meet?)
- [ ] あいます。
- [x] あいましょうか。
- [ ] あいませんでした。
solution: ましょうか asks "shall we...?"
Hear it: !say[なんじに あいましょうか。]
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Listen: !listen[m|どようびに いっしょに テニスを しませんか。]
What is the speaker inviting you to?
- [x] play tennis on Saturday
- [ ] watch tennis on Sunday
- [ ] study on Saturday
solution: どようびに (on Saturday) いっしょに テニスを しませんか (won't you play tennis together?).
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=open
Invite a classmate to do something this weekend, then suggest a time and place to meet.
!speak[my invitation]
solution: For example: しゅうまつ いっしょに えいがを みませんか。どようびの さんじに えきで あいましょう。
:::

::: problem id=p8 type=choice creative=true
Why might ちょっと… ("a bit...") work as a polite "no" in Japanese?
- [x] Leaving the refusal unfinished lets the other person understand without anyone losing face; saying "no" outright can feel confrontational.
- [ ] ちょっと means "no" in Japanese.
- [ ] Japanese speakers never refuse invitations.
solution: Japanese communication often relies on the listener reading the situation. The trailing ちょっと… signals difficulty clearly, while keeping things friendly.
:::
