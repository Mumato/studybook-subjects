## Welcome to JPN101

This book covers a first-semester university course in **Elementary Japanese**, starting from zero: the hiragana and katakana scripts, everyday conversation, the polite verb and adjective forms, and about 60 kanji. JPN102 continues with the て-form.

### Typing Japanese

You don't need a Japanese keyboard. When an answer is in Japanese, type **romaji** (Japanese in English letters) and it turns into kana as you type: `taberu` → たべる.

- Both common spellings work: `shi`/`si`, `chi`/`ti`, `tsu`/`tu`, `fu`/`hu`.
- Double a consonant for a small っ: `kitte` → きって. Use `n'` or `nn` for ん when the next letter is a vowel: `kin'en` → きんえん.
- Particles can be typed as they sound: `wa` for は, `e` for へ, `o` for を.
- Katakana answers accept the same romaji, with `-` for the long mark ー: `ko-hi-` → コーヒー.
- Answers asking for a **reading** ("type the reading of か") want romaji: `ka`.

Click **▶** next to Japanese to hear it. Listening questions show **▶ Listen** with the words hidden: play it as often as you like. (Volume: Minecraft's Voice/Speech slider.)

Small text above a kanji, like {日本|にほん}, is **furigana**: it tells you how to read it.

### How a lesson works

Every lesson gives you **a rule**, **a worked example** and then **practice problems**. Wrong answers go on your **Review mistakes** list on the contents page. Each chapter ends with a **test**: score 80% to complete it.

### The course

- Part 1: Hiragana (with handwriting practice)
- Part 2: Greetings and introductions, with speaking practice (record yourself, compare with the model, and let the book check your words)
- Part 3: Katakana
- Part 4: Numbers, time and shopping
- Part 5: Verbs and particles
- Part 6: Adjectives
- Part 7: Places, counting and dates
- Part 8: More everyday Japanese: も, と, で, から/まで, ～たい, ～から (because), family
- Part 9: Kanji: about 60 characters, from numbers to everyday verbs

The kana and kanji lessons end with **handwriting** practice: draw each character with the mouse and get each stroke checked.
