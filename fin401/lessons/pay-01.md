id: pay-01
title: Dividends and share buybacks
---
A firm with spare cash can pay it to shareholders as a **dividend** or by **buying back shares**. (Berk ch. 17)

::: rule
- **Dividend:** cash per share to everyone who owns the shares before the **ex-dividend date**. On that date the price drops by about the dividend:
$$P_{ex} = P_{cum} - Div \quad \text{(perfect markets)}$$
- **Share buyback (repurchase):** the firm buys shares at the market price. Fewer shares remain, and the price doesn't change.
- In perfect markets the two are equivalent: shareholders end up with the same total value either way.
- A **stock split** or **stock dividend** gives more shares but no cash: the price falls in proportion and nothing of value changes.
:::

::: example
A firm worth \$420M (including \$20M of spare cash) has 10 million shares at \$42.
- Pay a \$2 dividend: the shares fall to \$40, and holders have \$40 + \$2.
- Or buy back \$20M of shares at \$42 (about 476,000): 9.52 million shares remain, worth \$400M, still \$42 each.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="48.5" tolerance=0.01
A share trades at \$50 just before going ex-dividend on a \$1.50 dividend. In perfect markets, what is its ex-dividend price?
solution: $50 - 1.50 = 48.50$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="20" tolerance=0.01
A firm is worth \$500M, including \$50M of spare cash, and has 25 million shares. It uses the cash to buy back shares. What is the share price after the buyback?
solution: Before: $500 / 25 = 20$. It buys $50 / 20 = 2.5$M shares; 22.5M remain, worth \$450M: still \$20.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="18" tolerance=0.01
If that firm instead paid the \$50M as a dividend, what would the ex-dividend share price be?
solution: Dividend $= 50 / 25 = 2$ per share; price $20 - 2 = 18$. Shareholders still have \$20 of value each.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
To receive a declared dividend, an investor must own the shares:
- [x] before the ex-dividend date
- [ ] on the payment date
- [ ] on the declaration date only
solution: Buyers on or after the ex-dividend date don't get it, which is why the price drops that day.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="40" tolerance=0.01
A stock trading at \$80 splits 2-for-1. What should its price be afterwards?
solution: Twice as many shares, same firm: $80 / 2 = 40$.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
In perfect capital markets, paying cash as a dividend instead of a buyback:
- [ ] makes shareholders richer
- [ ] makes shareholders poorer
- [x] makes no difference to shareholders' wealth
solution: Either way the cash goes to shareholders; only its form differs.
:::
