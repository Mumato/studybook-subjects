---
id: gov-02
title: Executive pay and shareholder power
---
Besides watching managers, shareholders can make it pay for managers to act in their interest.

::: rule
- **Compensation:** tying pay to the share price (shares, stock options) aligns managers with shareholders. **Pay-performance sensitivity** measures how much the CEO's wealth changes when shareholder wealth changes.
- Options can backfire: they reward risk-taking and short-term price boosts, and **backdating** or timing grants is abuse.
- **Shareholder voice:** voting at annual meetings, **say on pay**, **proxy fights**, activist investors.
- **Takeovers** discipline managers too: a badly run firm is a target. **Entrenchment** (defences that protect managers) weakens this.
:::

::: example
A CEO owns 0.5% of a \$10 billion firm. If the share price rises 10%, shareholders gain \$1 billion and the CEO's stake gains \$5 million.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="5000000" tolerance=1
A CEO owns 0.5% of a firm worth \$10 billion. The share price rises 10%. How much is the CEO's stake worth now compared with before? Type the gain in dollars.
solution: Firm gain \$1 billion; $0.005 \times 1000000000 = 5000000$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
Paying the CEO mostly in stock options may encourage:
- [ ] avoiding all risk
- [x] taking more risk, since options gain from volatility
- [ ] paying higher dividends
solution: Options pay off on the upside only, so riskier strategies raise their value.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
"Say on pay" lets shareholders:
- [x] vote on executive compensation
- [ ] set the CEO's salary themselves
- [ ] receive the CEO's bonus
solution: The vote (often advisory) pressures boards on pay.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Managerial entrenchment means:
- [ ] managers owning most of the shares
- [x] managers being protected from being replaced, even when they perform badly
- [ ] the board firing the CEO
solution: Strong takeover defences and captured boards entrench managers.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
How does the threat of takeover improve governance?
- [x] poorly run firms become cheap targets, and their managers are often replaced
- [ ] it lowers the firm's taxes
- [ ] it forces higher dividends
solution: The market for corporate control is a backstop when the board fails.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
Backdating stock options means:
- [ ] granting options that expire early
- [x] pretending options were granted on an earlier date when the price was lower
- [ ] paying options in cash
solution: It quietly gives executives extra pay at shareholders' expense.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=open creative=true
Design a CEO pay package that rewards long-term value instead of short-term share price jumps. What would you include and why?
solution: For example: restricted shares that vest over several years (and must be held after vesting), performance targets based on multi-year returns relative to competitors, clawbacks if results are restated, and limits on hedging the shares. These make it pay to build lasting value rather than to boost the price briefly.
:::
