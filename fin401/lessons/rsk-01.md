id: rsk-01
title: Commodity price risk
---
Firms face risks they can reduce: the prices of what they buy and sell, and exchange rates. Reducing them is **hedging**. (Berk ch. 21.2)

::: rule
Ways to hedge commodity price risk:
- **Vertical integration:** owning your supplier (an airline buying a refinery).
- **Long-term contracts:** fix the price with a supplier for years.
- **Futures contracts:** standardized, exchange-traded agreements to buy or sell at a fixed price on a future date. A buyer of fuel takes a **long** position; a producer selling it goes **short**.

A hedge removes **both** the losses and the gains from price moves: it lowers risk, it doesn't make money.
Hedging adds value when it lowers costs of financial distress, taxes or agency problems, or lets the firm focus on its own business.
:::

::: example
An airline needs 1 million litres of jet fuel in a year and locks in \$0.80 a litre with futures. If the price rises to \$0.95, it pays \$950,000 for fuel but gains \$150,000 on the futures: net \$800,000, the locked price.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="150000" tolerance=1
In the example, the fuel price rises to \$0.95. How much does the airline gain on its futures?
solution: $1000000 \times (0.95 - 0.80) = 150000$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="800000" tolerance=1
If instead the price falls to \$0.70, what is the airline's net fuel cost (fuel bought plus futures loss)?
solution: Fuel \$700,000, futures loss \$100,000: net \$800,000, the same locked price.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A gold mining company wants to lock in the price of the gold it will sell next year. It should:
- [ ] buy gold futures (long)
- [x] sell gold futures (short)
- [ ] buy gold today
solution: A seller hedges with a short position: if gold falls, the futures gain offsets the lower sale price.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
A firm hedges its fuel costs and then fuel prices fall sharply. Compared with not hedging, the firm:
- [ ] gains even more
- [x] misses out on the lower price
- [ ] goes bankrupt
solution: Hedging locks the price in both directions: it gives up gains to avoid losses.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
Which is a good reason for a firm to hedge?
- [x] to lower the chance of financial distress
- [ ] to earn profits from trading futures
- [ ] to raise its expected cash flows from price moves
solution: Hedging reduces risk; its value comes from avoiding the costs that risk brings, like distress.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
An airline buying an oil refinery to protect itself from fuel price swings is an example of:
- [ ] a futures hedge
- [x] vertical integration
- [ ] a long-term contract
solution: Owning the supplier is vertical integration.
:::
