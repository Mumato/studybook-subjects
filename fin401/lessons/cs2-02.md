title: Financial distress and the trade-off theory
---
If debt saves tax, why not borrow 100%? Because too much debt brings the costs of financial distress. (Berk ch. 16.5)

::: rule
- **Financial distress:** trouble meeting debt payments; at worst, bankruptcy.
- **Direct costs:** lawyers, accountants, court fees.
- **Indirect costs:** lost customers and suppliers, key employees leaving, assets sold cheaply.
- **Trade-off theory:**
$$V_L = V_U + PV(\text{interest tax shield}) - PV(\text{financial distress costs})$$
The optimal debt level is where adding more debt adds as much in distress costs as it saves in tax.
- Firms with intangible assets, uncertain cash flows or high distress costs should borrow less.
:::

::: example
$V_U = 500$, PV of tax shields 40, PV of distress costs 15: $V_L = 500 + 40 - 15 = 525$.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="1050" tolerance=0.1
An unlevered firm is worth \$1,000M. Its debt has tax shields worth \$80M and distress costs worth \$30M (PVs). What is the levered firm worth, in millions?
solution: $1000 + 80 - 30 = 1050$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
Which firm should usually carry the **least** debt?
- [x] a software start-up with uncertain cash flows and mostly intangible assets
- [ ] a utility with steady cash flows
- [ ] a real estate firm with buildings as assets
solution: Uncertain cash flows make distress likely, and intangible value disappears in distress, so distress costs are high.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="200" tolerance=0.1
A firm can choose \$0, \$100M, \$200M or \$300M of debt. The PV of tax shields would be 0, 25, 50 and 75; the PV of distress costs 0, 5, 20 and 50 (millions). Which debt level maximizes firm value, in millions?
solution: Net gains: 0, 20, 30, 25. The best is \$200M of debt.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Which is an **indirect** cost of financial distress?
- [ ] fees paid to bankruptcy lawyers
- [x] customers switching to a competitor they expect to survive
- [ ] court costs
solution: Lawyers and courts are direct costs. Losing customers, suppliers and staff are indirect, and often larger.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=text answers="trade-off theory;trade-off;tradeoff theory;tradeoff;the trade-off theory;trade off theory;trade off"
Which theory says firms choose debt by balancing tax shields against distress costs?
solution: The **trade-off theory**.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
According to the trade-off theory, at the optimal level of debt:
- [ ] tax shields are as large as possible
- [ ] distress costs are zero
- [x] the extra tax shield from more debt equals the extra distress cost
solution: Beyond that point each dollar of debt costs more in distress than it saves in tax.
:::
