---
id: gov-03
title: Rules, control and ethics
---
Laws and stock exchange rules add to governance, and ownership structures differ around the world, including in Canada.

::: rule
- **Regulation:** after scandals like Enron, the US **Sarbanes-Oxley Act** (SOX) required independent audit committees and made CEOs and CFOs certify the financial statements. Canada adopted similar rules for listed firms.
- **Insider trading** (trading on material non-public information) is illegal.
- **Concentrated control** is common in Canada and around the world.
- **Dual-class shares:** some shares carry more votes, so a family can control a firm with a small stake.
- **Pyramids:** a family controls firm A, which controls firm B, and so on. Cash-flow rights multiply down the chain; control doesn't shrink.
- The risk: controlling owners can take value from minority shareholders.
:::

::: example
A family owns 50% of firm A, which owns 50% of firm B. The family controls B, but owns only $0.5 \times 0.5 = 25\%$ of its cash flows.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="25" tolerance=0.01
A family owns 50% of firm A, which owns 50% of firm B. What percent of firm B's cash flows does the family own?
solution: $0.5 \times 0.5 = 25\%$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="12.5" tolerance=0.01
Firm B owns 50% of firm C. What percent of C's cash flows does the family own?
solution: $0.25 \times 0.5 = 12.5\%$, though it still controls C.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Dual-class shares allow:
- [x] a small group to control a firm while owning a small part of its equity
- [ ] all shareholders to vote twice
- [ ] firms to avoid paying dividends
solution: The high-vote shares give control out of proportion to ownership.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Buying shares because a friend at the company told you about an unannounced takeover is:
- [ ] legal if you're not an employee
- [x] illegal insider trading
- [ ] legal if you hold the shares long-term
solution: Trading on material non-public information is insider trading.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
One requirement of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act is that:
- [x] the CEO and CFO personally certify the financial statements
- [ ] firms pay no dividends during audits
- [ ] every director must be an employee
solution: Certification makes top executives personally accountable for the numbers.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
The main governance risk in firms with a controlling shareholder is:
- [ ] managers ignoring all shareholders
- [x] the controlling owner taking value from minority shareholders
- [ ] too much monitoring
solution: Concentrated control reduces the manager-shareholder problem but creates a controller-minority one.
:::
