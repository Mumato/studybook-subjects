id: cs2-01
title: Debt and taxes
---
The first real-world reason debt matters: interest is tax-deductible, dividends aren't. (Berk ch. 16.3–16.4)

::: rule
- **Interest tax shield** each year $= T_c \times$ interest paid.
- With **permanent** debt $D$, its PV is $T_c \times D$, so
$$V_L = V_U + T_c\,D$$
- The WACC then **falls** as debt rises:
$$r_{wacc} = \frac{E}{V}\,r_E + \frac{D}{V}\,r_D\,(1 - T_c)$$
- When a firm announces a debt-financed buyback, the share price rises at once by the value of the tax shield per share.
:::

::: example
\$100M of permanent debt at 6%, tax 25%: interest \$6M saves \$1.5M of tax a year, worth $0.25 \times 100 = 25$ million today.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="2.6" tolerance=0.01
A firm has \$200M of debt at 5% and a 26% tax rate. How much tax does the interest save each year, in millions?
solution: Interest $= 10$M; $0.26 \times 10 = 2.6$ million.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="52" tolerance=0.1
If that debt is permanent, what is the PV of its tax shields, in millions?
solution: $T_c \times D = 0.26 \times 200 = 52$ million.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="852" tolerance=0.1
The firm would be worth \$800M without debt. What is it worth with that debt, in millions?
solution: $800 + 52 = 852$ million.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="21.30" tolerance=0.01
Before borrowing, the firm had 40 million shares at \$20. It announces the \$200M permanent debt to buy back shares. What is the share price after the announcement?
hint: The whole gain from the tax shield goes to the current shareholders.
solution: Equity before the buyback: $800 + 52 = 852$M over 40M shares $= 21.30$.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
With corporate taxes, adding debt lowers the WACC because:
- [ ] debt holders accept any return
- [x] interest is tax-deductible, lowering debt's after-tax cost
- [ ] equity becomes less risky
solution: The government effectively pays part of the interest through lower taxes.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=numeric answer="9" tolerance=0.01
A firm is 60% equity at 12% and 40% debt at 6%, with a 25% tax rate. What is its WACC? Type the percent.
solution: $0.6 \times 12 + 0.4 \times 6 \times 0.75 = 7.2 + 1.8 = 9\%$.
:::
