title: Debt financing
---
Firms borrow publicly (bonds) or privately (banks and institutions). The bond contract spells out who gets paid and when. (Berk ch. 15.1)

::: rule
- **Public debt:** bonds sold to investors, governed by an **indenture** (the bond contract). Types include notes, debentures (unsecured) and mortgage bonds (secured by property).
- **Private debt:** bank loans, lines of credit, private placements: cheaper to arrange, easier to renegotiate.
- **Seniority:** senior debt is repaid before subordinated debt in bankruptcy.
- **Covenants:** rules the borrower agrees to (limits on more debt, dividends, asset sales). They lower the interest rate by protecting lenders.
- **Callable bonds** let the issuer repay early at the call price: useful if rates fall, so investors demand a **higher yield**.
:::

::: example
A firm with a lot of debt agrees not to pay dividends above 50% of earnings. Lenders feel safer, so they charge a lower rate.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
An unsecured corporate bond is called a:
- [x] debenture
- [ ] mortgage bond
- [ ] asset-backed bond
solution: Debentures (and notes) are backed only by the firm's general credit.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
In bankruptcy, who is repaid first?
- [x] senior debt holders
- [ ] subordinated debt holders
- [ ] shareholders
solution: Senior claims come before subordinated ones; shareholders come last.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Why do borrowers agree to restrictive covenants?
- [ ] the law requires them
- [x] they get a lower interest rate in return
- [ ] they raise the share price directly
solution: Covenants protect lenders, who then demand less compensation.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Compared with an otherwise identical non-callable bond, a callable bond has:
- [x] a higher yield
- [ ] a lower yield
- [ ] the same yield
solution: The issuer will call it when that's good for the issuer and bad for investors, so investors want more yield.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
When is a firm most likely to call its callable bonds?
- [ ] when interest rates rise
- [x] when interest rates fall
- [ ] when its credit rating falls
solution: It can then refinance at a lower rate.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=text answers="indenture;the indenture;bond indenture;trust indenture"
The legal contract between a bond issuer and its bondholders is called the ...
solution: The **indenture**.
:::
