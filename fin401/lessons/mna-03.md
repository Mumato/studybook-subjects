title: The takeover process and defences
---
Not every target wants to be bought. Bidders and targets each have tactics.

::: rule
- **Friendly** deals are negotiated with the target's board. **Hostile** bids go around it: a **tender offer** straight to shareholders, or a **proxy fight** to replace the board.
- **Defences:** **poison pill** (cheap new shares for everyone but the raider), **staggered board** (only some directors elected each year), **white knight** (a friendlier buyer), **golden parachutes** (big payouts to fired managers).
- **Free-rider problem:** target shareholders hold out, hoping others sell and they keep the improved shares, so raiders must offer most of the gains. **Toeholds** (buying shares quietly first) and **leveraged buyouts** help raiders.
- In a **leveraged buyout** (LBO) the buyer borrows heavily, using the target's own assets and cash flows to repay the debt.
:::

::: example
A raider bids for a firm. The board answers by letting every other shareholder buy new shares at half price, making the takeover far more expensive: a poison pill.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
Offering to buy shares directly from a target's shareholders, without the board's approval, is a:
- [x] tender offer
- [ ] proxy fight
- [ ] white knight
solution: A tender offer goes straight to shareholders.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
A target finds a friendlier company to buy it instead of the hostile bidder. That company is a:
- [ ] poison pill
- [x] white knight
- [ ] toehold
solution: The white knight rescues the target from the hostile bidder.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A poison pill works by:
- [x] letting existing shareholders (but not the raider) buy shares cheaply, diluting the raider
- [ ] making the firm's products unsafe
- [ ] paying managers a bonus if they're fired
solution: It makes acquiring control extremely expensive.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
With a staggered board, a raider who wins a proxy fight:
- [ ] controls the board at once
- [x] can only replace some directors each year
- [ ] must pay a premium to every director
solution: Gaining a majority takes two or more annual meetings.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
The free-rider problem in takeovers means:
- [x] shareholders refuse to sell at a price below what the shares will be worth after the takeover
- [ ] raiders can buy shares for free
- [ ] managers get golden parachutes
solution: Each shareholder hopes to keep their shares and share in the gains, so raiders must pay away most of them.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
In a leveraged buyout, the acquisition is paid for mostly with:
- [ ] the buyer's own cash
- [x] debt backed by the target's assets and cash flows
- [ ] new shares of the target
solution: That's what "leveraged" means.
:::
