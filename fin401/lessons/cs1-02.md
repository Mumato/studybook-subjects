title: Modigliani-Miller
---
Modigliani and Miller showed that, in perfect markets, capital structure doesn't matter. Knowing why tells you when it **does** matter. (Berk ch. 16.2)

::: rule
**MM Proposition I:** $V_L = E + D = V_U$. Investors can borrow on their own (**homemade leverage**), so they won't pay extra for a firm's leverage.
**MM Proposition II:** the cost of levered equity rises with leverage:
$$r_E = r_U + \frac{D}{E}(r_U - r_D)$$
With no taxes, the WACC stays equal to $r_U$: cheaper debt is exactly offset by costlier equity.
:::

::: example
$r_U = 10\%$, $r_D = 5\%$, $D/E = 0.5$:
$$r_E = 10\% + 0.5 \times (10\% - 5\%) = 12.5\%$$
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="18" tolerance=0.01
$r_U = 12\%$, $r_D = 6\%$ and the firm has equal amounts of debt and equity. What is $r_E$? Type the percent.
solution: $D/E = 1$: $12 + 1 \times (12 - 6) = 18\%$.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=numeric answer="12.33" tolerance=0.01
$r_U = 9\%$, $r_D = 4\%$, debt \$40M and equity \$60M. What is $r_E$? Type the percent.
solution: $9 + \frac{40}{60}(9 - 4) = 9 + 3.33 = 12.33\%$.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=numeric answer="9" tolerance=0.01
For that firm (no taxes), what is the WACC? Type the percent.
solution: $0.6 \times 12.33 + 0.4 \times 4 = 9\%$, equal to $r_U$, as MM says.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=numeric answer="300" tolerance=0.1
An unlevered firm is worth \$500M. In perfect markets it borrows \$200M and uses it to buy back shares. What is its equity worth afterwards, in millions?
solution: $V_L = V_U = 500$; equity $= 500 - 200 = 300$ million.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=numeric answer="10" tolerance=0.01
Before that buyback the firm had 50 million shares at \$10. What is the share price after the buyback?
solution: It buys back $200 / 10 = 20$ million shares; 30 million remain, worth \$300M: still \$10. Leverage didn't create value.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=choice
"Homemade leverage" means:
- [x] investors can borrow or lend on their own to undo or copy a firm's leverage
- [ ] firms can issue debt without paying interest
- [ ] managers can choose the firm's leverage freely
solution: Since investors can create any leverage themselves, a firm's choice can't be worth paying for: that's the heart of MM I.
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=open creative=true
A manager says: "Debt costs 5% and equity 12%, so swapping equity for debt lowers our cost of capital." In perfect markets, what's wrong with that?
solution: Adding debt makes the remaining equity riskier, so $r_E$ rises (MM II). The cheaper debt is exactly offset, and the WACC stays at $r_U$. Debt only helps through things outside perfect markets, like taxes.
:::
