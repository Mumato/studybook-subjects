id: mna-01
title: Why firms merge
---
In a merger or acquisition, one firm (the **acquirer** or bidder) buys another (the **target**). Acquisitions come in waves, often in booming markets. (Berk ch. 24)

::: rule
- **Horizontal:** same industry (two banks). **Vertical:** along the supply chain (a carmaker buying a parts maker). **Conglomerate:** unrelated businesses.
- Paid in **cash** (a "tender offer" for shares) or in the **acquirer's stock**.
- The only good reason: **synergies**, extra value from combining. **Cost synergies** (economies of scale, cutting duplicate costs) are easier to achieve than **revenue synergies** (cross-selling, market power).
- Doubtful reasons: **diversification** (shareholders can diversify on their own), **EPS growth** from merging alone, and managers' wish to run a bigger firm.
- **Target shareholders gain** (they get a premium); acquirer shareholders gain little on average.
:::

::: example
Two regional supermarket chains merge, close duplicate warehouses and buy in bigger volumes: those cost savings are synergies.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=choice
A steel maker buys an iron ore mine. This is a:
- [ ] horizontal merger
- [x] vertical merger
- [ ] conglomerate merger
solution: The mine supplies the steel maker: it's along the supply chain.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
A software firm buys a hotel chain. This is a:
- [ ] horizontal merger
- [ ] vertical merger
- [x] conglomerate merger
solution: Unrelated businesses.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
Why is diversification a weak reason for an acquisition?
- [x] shareholders can diversify themselves, more cheaply, by buying other shares
- [ ] diversified firms pay more tax
- [ ] diversification is illegal
solution: Investors can hold both firms' shares; they won't pay a premium for the firm to do it.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Which synergies are usually easier to achieve?
- [x] cost savings
- [ ] revenue increases
solution: Cutting duplicate costs is under management's control; extra sales depend on customers.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
On average, when an acquisition is announced:
- [x] the target's share price rises a lot; the acquirer's barely moves
- [ ] both prices rise a lot
- [ ] the target's price falls
solution: Targets get a takeover premium; most of the value goes to them.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=text answers="synergies;synergy;synergies created"
The extra value created by combining two firms is called ...
solution: **Synergies**.
:::
