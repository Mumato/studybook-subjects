title: Agency costs and information
---
Debt also changes how managers and shareholders behave, and financing choices send signals. (Berk ch. 16.6–16.7)

::: rule
**Agency costs of debt** (when a firm is near distress, shareholders gain at lenders' expense):
- **Excessive risk-taking** (asset substitution): gambling, even on negative-NPV projects.
- **Debt overhang** (under-investment): refusing positive-NPV projects because much of the gain goes to lenders.
- **Cashing out:** paying out cash instead of investing.

**Agency benefit of debt:** fixed payments leave managers less free cash to waste.

**Asymmetric information:** managers know more than investors. Issuing equity hints the shares are overvalued, so the price falls on the announcement. Hence the **pecking order**: internal funds first, then debt, and new equity last.
:::

::: example
A firm close to default turns down a project that costs shareholders \$10 and creates \$12 of value, because \$4 of it would go to repaying lenders. Shareholders would gain only \$8 for their \$10.
:::

::: problem id=p1 type=numeric answer="-2" tolerance=0.01
In the example, what is the project's NPV **for the shareholders**?
solution: They put in \$10 and get \$8: $8 - 10 = -2$. They reject a project with a positive NPV overall (\$2). That's debt overhang.
:::

::: problem id=p2 type=choice
The pecking order says firms prefer to finance projects with:
- [x] retained earnings, then debt, then new equity
- [ ] new equity, then debt, then retained earnings
- [ ] debt, then new equity, then retained earnings
solution: Internal funds send no signal; equity sends the worst one.
:::

::: problem id=p3 type=choice
A firm announces it will issue new shares. Its share price typically:
- [ ] rises
- [x] falls
- [ ] doesn't react
solution: Investors suspect managers issue shares when they think they're overvalued.
:::

::: problem id=p4 type=choice
Shareholders of a firm near bankruptcy would most like to:
- [ ] take only safe projects
- [x] take very risky projects, even with negative NPV
- [ ] pay down the debt early
solution: They keep the upside if the gamble works; lenders bear most of the loss if it fails: asset substitution.
:::

::: problem id=p5 type=choice
How can debt **reduce** agency costs between managers and shareholders?
- [x] Required interest payments leave less free cash for managers to waste
- [ ] Lenders vote at shareholder meetings
- [ ] Debt raises the share price automatically
solution: That's the "discipline of debt": less spare cash, fewer wasteful projects and perks.
:::

::: problem id=p6 type=text answers="debt overhang;overhang;under-investment;underinvestment"
A distressed firm passes up a positive-NPV project because the gains would mostly go to its lenders. This problem is called ...
solution: **Debt overhang** (under-investment).
:::

::: problem id=p7 type=open creative=true
Tech firms often hold lots of cash and little debt; utilities borrow heavily. Explain this with the ideas from this chapter.
solution: Tech firms have uncertain cash flows and intangible assets (high distress costs) and big growth options that debt overhang would threaten. Utilities have steady cash flows and hard assets, so distress is unlikely and cheap, and they can use the tax shield safely.
:::
