::: problem id=t1 type=choice
A 9% APR compounded monthly has an effective annual rate of:
- [ ] 9.00%
- [x] 9.38%
- [ ] 9.20%
- [ ] 0.75%
solution: $(1 + 0.09/12)^{12} - 1 = 9.38\%$.
:::

::: problem id=t2 type=choice
A 6-year zero-coupon bond with \$1,000 face costs \$750. Its yield to maturity is:
- [ ] 5.56%
- [x] 4.91%
- [ ] 4.17%
- [ ] 33.3%
solution: $(1000/750)^{1/6} - 1 = 4.91\%$.
:::

::: problem id=t3 type=choice
You buy a share at \$40, collect a \$1.60 dividend and sell at \$43. Your total return is:
- [ ] 7.5%
- [ ] 4.0%
- [x] 11.5%
- [ ] 10.0%
solution: $(1.60 + 43 - 40) / 40 = 11.5\%$.
:::

::: problem id=t4 type=choice
Pay \$2,000 now and receive \$2,420 in two years. The IRR is:
- [ ] 21%
- [x] 10%
- [ ] 10.5%
- [ ] 9.5%
solution: $(2420/2000)^{1/2} - 1 = 1.21^{1/2} - 1 = 10\%$.
:::

::: problem id=t5 type=choice
Revenue minus costs is \$600, tax 25%, depreciation \$150, capex \$100 and NWC rises by \$30 (thousands). Free cash flow is:
- [ ] \$450.0
- [x] \$357.5
- [ ] \$507.5
- [ ] \$320.0
solution: $600 \times 0.75 + 0.25 \times 150 - 100 - 30 = 450 + 37.5 - 130 = 357.5$.
:::

::: problem id=t6 type=choice
The risk-free rate is 3%, beta 1.25 and the market risk premium 6.4%. The cost of equity is:
- [x] 11.0%
- [ ] 8.0%
- [ ] 10.4%
- [ ] 12.25%
solution: $3 + 1.25 \times 6.4 = 11\%$.
:::

::: problem id=t7 type=choice
According to the trade-off theory, a firm should increase its debt until:
- [ ] its tax payments reach zero
- [x] the marginal tax benefit of debt equals the marginal cost of financial distress
- [ ] its debt-to-equity ratio is 1
- [ ] its credit rating falls below investment grade
solution: That's the point that maximizes $V_U + PV(\text{tax shield}) - PV(\text{distress costs})$.
:::

::: problem id=t8 type=choice
In perfect capital markets, a shareholder who wants cash from a firm that pays no dividend can:
- [x] sell some shares (a homemade dividend), ending up just as well off
- [ ] do nothing: the firm's value is lost to them
- [ ] demand a dividend from the board
solution: Payout policy is irrelevant in perfect markets because investors can create their own dividends.
:::
