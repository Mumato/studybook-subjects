::: problem id=t1 type=numeric answer="185" tolerance=0.5
A project's revenue is \$800, costs \$450 and depreciation \$100 (thousands). The tax rate is 26%. What is its unlevered net income, in thousands?
solution: $EBIT = 800 - 450 - 100 = 250$; $250 \times 0.74 = 185$.
:::

::: problem id=t2 type=numeric answer="215" tolerance=0.5
That project has depreciation \$100, capital expenditures \$50 and an increase in NWC of \$20 (thousands). What is its FCF, in thousands?
solution: $185 + 100 - 50 - 20 = 215$.
:::

::: problem id=t3 type=choice
Which of these should be left **out** of a project's cash flows?
- [ ] lost rent on a building the project will use
- [ ] lost sales of the firm's existing products
- [x] a consulting study paid for last year
- [ ] the extra inventory the project needs
solution: The study is a sunk cost. The others change because of the project.
:::

::: problem id=t4 type=numeric answer="35795.45" tolerance=1
A \$200,000 asset has a 30% CCA rate, the tax rate is 25% and the cost of capital 10% (half-year rule). What is the PV of its CCA tax shield?
solution: $\frac{200000 \times 0.3 \times 0.25}{0.4} \times \frac{1.05}{1.10} = 35795.45$.
:::

::: problem id=t5 type=choice
A project will be paid for with a loan. In its free cash flows, the interest on the loan is:
- [ ] subtracted each year
- [x] left out: financing is reflected in the cost of capital
- [ ] added back like depreciation
solution: FCF is unlevered. Financing costs enter through the discount rate (the WACC).
:::

::: problem id=t6 type=choice
At a 26% tax rate, \$50,000 of depreciation saves:
- [ ] \$50,000 of tax
- [x] \$13,000 of tax
- [ ] \$37,000 of tax
- [ ] nothing: depreciation isn't cash
solution: $0.26 \times 50000 = 13000$.
:::

::: problem id=t7 type=choice
A project adds \$40,000 of inventory at the start. At the end of the project, that working capital usually:
- [x] is recovered, a cash inflow
- [ ] is a final cash outflow
- [ ] is depreciated to zero
solution: The inventory is sold off and receivables collected, so the cash comes back.
:::
