::: problem id=t1 type=choice
A share trades at \$36 cum-dividend and will pay \$1.20. In perfect markets the ex-dividend price is:
- [ ] \$36.00
- [x] \$34.80
- [ ] \$37.20
- [ ] \$30.00
solution: $36 - 1.20 = 34.80$.
:::

::: problem id=t2 type=choice
In perfect markets, a firm uses spare cash to buy back shares instead of paying a dividend. The share price after the buyback is:
- [ ] lower than before
- [x] the same as before
- [ ] higher than before
solution: The firm buys at the market price: value and shares fall in proportion.
:::

::: problem id=t3 type=numeric answer="25" tolerance=0.01
Dividends are taxed at 40% and capital gains at 20%. What is the effective dividend tax rate? Type the percent.
solution: $(0.40 - 0.20) / 0.80 = 25\%$.
:::

::: problem id=t4 type=choice
"Investors with different tax rates hold stocks with different payout policies" describes:
- [ ] the pecking order
- [x] the clientele effect
- [ ] signalling
- [ ] homemade leverage
solution: That's the clientele effect.
:::

::: problem id=t5 type=numeric answer="45" tolerance=0.01
A stock at \$135 has a 3-for-1 split. What is its new price?
solution: $135 / 3 = 45$.
:::

::: problem id=t6 type=choice
Why can keeping large amounts of cash in the firm hurt shareholders when there are taxes?
- [x] Interest on the cash is taxed at the corporate level before reaching shareholders
- [ ] Cash loses value through inflation only inside firms
- [ ] Banks charge firms more fees than individuals
solution: Corporate tax on the interest is a cost shareholders wouldn't bear if the cash were paid out.
:::

::: problem id=t7 type=choice
A firm cuts its dividend for the first time in 20 years. The most likely market reaction is:
- [ ] the price rises
- [x] the price falls
- [ ] no change, by MM
solution: Dividend cuts signal that managers expect weaker earnings ahead.
:::
